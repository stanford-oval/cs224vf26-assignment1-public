# Phenotypic Identification and Clinical Differentiation of GTF3C3-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorder (OMIM #621201)

## Cohort Overview and Reported Phenotypic Frequencies

Neurodevelopmental disorder with dysmorphic facies, brain anomalies, and seizures (NEDFBS; OMIM #621201) is an ultrarare autosomal recessive entity caused by biallelic pathogenic variants in *GTF3C3*, which encodes a core subunit of the general transcription factor TFIIIC2 complex critical for RNA polymerase III (Pol III) transcription F3. Across worldwide clinical literature as of 2026, exactly 17 affected individuals have been molecularly and clinically characterized F1, F3. The primary characterization rests on two landmark series: the cohort of 12 individuals across 7 unrelated families reported by De Hayr et al. (2025; DOI: 10.1016/j.gim.2024.101253) and the series of 4 individuals across 3 unrelated families described by Abdel et al. / Abdel-Hamid et al. (2025; DOI: 10.1093/braincomms/fcaf055 / PMC11878552), with an additional comprehensive single patient review reported by López et al. (2026; DOI: 10.3389/fgene.2026.1860633) F1, F2, F3.

## Discriminating Clinical and Neuroradiological Findings

When evaluating a patient presenting with generalized neurodevelopmental delay, microcephaly, and seizures, clinical features vary in their discriminatory utility against overlapping disorders F1, F5.

### Ranked Discriminating Value

1. **Combined Progressive Cerebellar Atrophy and Frontal Lobe Hypoplasia (Highest Discrimination; Moderate-to-High Confidence)**  
   *Phenotypic Presentation:* Structural neuroimaging reveals symmetric cerebellar atrophy—frequently involving the vermis and hemispheres—alongside anterior forebrain/frontal hypoplasia F8, F14.  
   *Overlapping Disorders:*  
   - *POLR3-related leukodystrophies (e.g., 4H syndrome):* Classically characterized by diffuse T2 hypomyelination, cerebellar atrophy, and hypodontia/hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. GTF3C3 presents with structural midline and lobar hypoplasia rather than diffuse white matter signal abnormality alone, though some myelin delay has been documented (Chapleau et al., 2026, DOI: 10.1038/s41582-026-01248-1) F5, F14.  
   - *GTF3C5-related disorder:* Also disrupts TFIIIC/Pol III-mediated transcription and causes cerebellar atrophy and thinning of the corpus callosum; however, GTF3C5 presentation often emphasizes prominent leukoencephalopathy over primary frontal lobe hypoplasia (Iwata, 2024, DOI: 10.1007/s00439-024-02656-3) F15.  
   *Confidence:* Moderate-to-High. The presence of marked frontal lobe hypoplasia in combination with progressive cerebellar volume loss strongly points toward GTF3C3 over other transcriptional or epileptic encephalopathies F8.

2. **Profound Expressive Speech Deficit Exceeding Motor Impairment (Intermediate Discrimination; Moderate Confidence)**  
   *Phenotypic Presentation:* Affected children consistently present with absent or severely restricted speech, often completely non-verbal, accompanied by severe to profound cognitive disability F1.  
   *Overlapping Disorders:* Differentiates from milder syndromic intellectual disabilities, though it overlaps significantly with severe epileptic encephalopathies (e.g., *ARX*-related West syndrome/epileptic encephalopathy) and *CDK19*-related neurodevelopmental disorder F1, F11.

3. **Craniofacial Dysmorphisms: Hypertelorism, Depressed Nasal Bridge, and Low-Set Ears (Low Discrimination; Low Confidence)**  
   *Phenotypic Presentation:* Includes ocular hypertelorism (HP:0000316), broad or depressed nasal bridge (HP:0000425), and low-set ears (HP:0000369) F11.  
   *Overlapping Disorders:* Highly indistinguishable from *CDK19*-, *ARX*-, or other Pol III/TFIIIC-related conditions on physical exam alone. Facial gestalt has generated conflicting interpretations regarding specificity F11, F17.

4. **Secondary Cortical Gyral Simplification (Poor Sensitivity; Low Discriminating Value)**  
   *Phenotypic Presentation:* Simplified gyral pattern (HP:0009879) occurs in only 1 of 4 assessed individuals in the Abdel cohort and <30% overall F4, F8. Its presence reflects severe developmental compromise rather than a sensitive discriminator F4.

## Findings Looked for and Found Absent (Negative Predictors)

In differential diagnosis, several negative findings provide clinical exclusion value:

1. **Intact Cognitive and Motor Development (HP:0001263 / HP:0001249)**  
   Normal neurodevelopment has been consistently absent in confirmed individuals (0/16 in primary cohorts) F1, F9. The presence of mild developmental delay or normal cognitive milestones strongly argues against GTF3C3-related disorder F1, F9.
2. **Absence of Corpus Callosum Anomalies Cannot Rule Out Diagnosis**  
   Importantly, agenesis or hypoplasia of the corpus callosum (HP:0001274 / HP:0002079) was present in only 7 of 16 (43.75%) evaluated individuals in the pooled review by López et al. (2026) F7. Therefore, clinicians **must not** exclude GTF3C3-related disorder on the basis of a structurally normal corpus callosum F7.
3. **Early Normal Brain MRI Does Not Exclude GTF3C3**  
   While Abdel et al. (2025) and De Hayr et al. (2025) reported neuroimaging malformations in all evaluated patients, Papuc et al. (2019; DOI: 10.1038/s41431-018-0299-8) identified a patient with GTF3C3 involvement whose early cerebral MRI showed no identifiable malformations F9. Progressive neurodegenerative processes (cerebellar volume loss) emerge over chronological time, meaning a normal scan in early infancy does not rule out the disease F9.

## Phenotypic Variation by Variant Type, Position, Age, and Sex

### Variant Type and Functional Position
- **Impact:** Functional studies suggest that biallelic loss-of-function variants or severe missense variants disrupting the TFIIIC complex assembly yield severe neurodevelopmental phenotypes F2. Zebrafish knockout models confirm disrupted motility and spontaneous seizure-like behavior upon loss of functional *gtf3c3* F16.
- **Genotype-Phenotype Correlations:** Formal genotype-phenotype correlations are currently unestablished due to small sample sizes (17 total individuals) F2. Early hypotheses proposing that cortical malformations (simplified gyral pattern) are restricted to truncating/null alleles have been refuted: simplified gyral pattern was identified in individuals harboring missense alleles F18.

### Age-Dependent Phenotypic Evolution
- **Seizure Emergence:** Seizures manifest early in life (infancy to early childhood), frequently evolving into refractory epilepsy F5, F6, F10.
- **Neuroradiological Progression:** Midline cerebellar atrophy appears progressive; neurodegenerative changes become pronounced as children age, explaining why early scans may appear near-normal F8, F9.
- **Facial Features Across Lifespan:** While Abdel et al. (2025) described facial dysmorphisms as recognizable in young children, longitudinal evidence shows that features in older individuals (up to 27 years of age) do not maintain a unique gestalt, confounding recognition in older adolescents and adults F11, F17.

### Sex-Specific Differences
- The disorder affects both males and females across unrelated kindreds (e.g., affected sister pairs and affected males), consistent with standard autosomal recessive transmission F1, F10. No sex-limited penetrance or sex-dependent phenotypic severity has been identified F1, F2.

## Contested and Contradictory Literature Findings

Several published observations exhibit direct friction across the literature:

1. **True Clinical Penetrance of Seizures (Deprioritised Finding [F10], [F12], [F13])**  
   - *Contradiction:* Abdel et al. (2025) reported seizures in exactly 2 of 4 (50%) individuals gathered via GeneMatcher F6, F10. In contrast, De Hayr et al. (2025) reported seizures in 11 of 12 (91.7%) individuals F6, F12, and the pooled review by López et al. (2026) cited 82.4% penetrance F6.
   - *Underlying Cause:* While ascertainment bias (epileptic encephalopathy panels vs. general neurodevelopmental clinics) has been hypothesized to explain the discrepancy, De Hayr et al. primarily evaluated individuals for intellectual disability, yet observed >90% seizure rates F13.

2. **Distinct Facial Gestalt vs. Non-Specific Variable Features (Deprioritised Finding [F11], [F17])**  
   - *Contradiction:* Abdel et al. (2025; PMC11878552) state that affected individuals exhibit a "distinctive dysmorphic facies that appear recognizable in very young children" F11, F17. Conversely, De Hayr et al. (2025) concluded that craniofacial features represent "variable nonfamilial facial features" that do not allow clinical diagnosis F11, F17.

3. **White Matter Pathology vs. Classical POLR3-Related Leukodystrophy (Deprioritised Finding [F14], [F15])**  
   - *Contradiction:* Abdel et al. (2025) emphasized structural tissue loss (predominant cerebellar and frontal atrophy) as the hallmark F8, F14. However, Chapleau et al. (2026; DOI: 10.1038/s41582-026-01248-1) and Genomics England PanelApp document white matter abnormalities, including delayed myelination and demyelination, demonstrating that GTF3C3-related pathology bridges structural brain malformations and leukodystrophies F5, F14, F15.

4. **Obligate Brain Malformations at Initial Presentation (Deprioritised Finding [F9])**  
   - *Contradiction:* In the series by Abdel et al. (2025) and De Hayr et al. (2025), 100% of imaged individuals exhibited identifiable structural abnormalities (cerebellar atrophy, frontal hypoplasia, or callosal dysgenesis) F8, F9. However, Papuc et al. (2019; DOI: 10.1038/s41431-018-0299-8) identified a confirmed case presenting with early-onset epileptic encephalopathy where cerebral MRI showed no overt malformations early on F9.

## Synthesis and Diagnostic Framework

To pick an individual with GTF3C3-related disorder out of a cohort of patients with overlapping neurodevelopmental phenotypes:

1. **Core Triage Criteria:** The individual must display severe-to-profound global developmental delay/intellectual disability (100% penetrance) with severe expressive language deficit F1, F9.
2. **Neuroimaging Key Discriminator:** Look for symmetric progressive cerebellar atrophy combined with frontal lobe hypoplasia F8. Do not exclude the diagnosis if the corpus callosum is intact (present in only ~44% of cases) F7, or if cortical gyration is normal (simplified gyral pattern is seen in <30%) F4.
3. **Seizure Assessment:** Expect early-onset epilepsy in the majority (~80%) of patients, but recognize that ~20–50% of confirmed cases in non-epilepsy cohorts may lack seizures during early observation F6, F10, F12.
4. **Definitive Diagnosis:** Because facial features (hypertelorism, broad nasal bridge, low-set ears) heavily overlap with other TFIIIC-, CDK19-, and Pol III-related conditions F11, F15, phenotypic suspicion must be confirmed by identifying biallelic pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants in *GTF3C3* via comprehensive exome or genome sequencing F2, F3.
