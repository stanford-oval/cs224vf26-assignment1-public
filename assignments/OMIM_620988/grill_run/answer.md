# Clinical Phenotypic Identification of Intellectual Developmental Disorder, Autosomal Dominant 75 (DHX9; OMIM #620988)

Intellectual developmental disorder, autosomal dominant 75 (OMIM #620988) is caused by monoallelic pathogenic variants in the *DHX9* gene on chromosome 1q25, which encodes a DExH-box RNA/DNA helicase involved in transcription regulation, R-loop resolution, and genomic stability (Calame et al., 2023, 10.1016/j.ajhg.2023.06.013; Lederbauer et al., 2024, 10.3389/fnmol.2024.1414949) [F1]. Distinguishing affected individuals from patients with clinically overlapping syndromic neurodevelopmental disorders (NDDs) on the basis of phenotype alone presents significant diagnostic challenges due to the absence of an invariant visual dysmorphic gestalt and the pleiotropic bifurcation between central neurodevelopmental and peripheral axonal manifestations (Calame et al., 2023, PMC10432148) [F1, F2, F3].

---

## 1. Clinical Phenotype Spectrum and Cohort Frequencies

In the primary delineation of *DHX9*-related disease by Calame et al. (2023), 20 individuals carrying ultra-rare, monoallelic, heterozygous de novo variants (average age 10.5 years) were evaluated across clinical centers (Calame et al., 2023, 10.1016/j.ajhg.2023.06.013) [F1, F3, F4]. Phenotypic evaluation reveals sub-threshold penetrance across somatic, craniofacial, and neurological domains rather than a single obligate somatic presentation [F1, F2].

## 2. Discriminating Features and Differential Diagnosis

Clinical differentiation of *DHX9*-related neurodevelopmental disorder cannot rely on facial gestalt alone because physical hallmarks lack invariant penetrance (Lederbauer et al., 2024, 10.3389/fnmol.2024.1414949) [F2]. Instead, clinicians must evaluate specific multi-system manifestations to distinguish *DHX9* from paralogous RNA helicase syndromes.

### Differentiation from DHX30 and DDX6 Syndromes
- **Distal Symmetric Axonal Polyneuropathy (CMT2):** The manifestation of a progressive, motor-and-sensory peripheral axonal neuropathy (axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2) is uniquely associated with monoallelic *DHX9* alterations and is absent in canonical *DHX30*-related and *DDX6*-related neurodevelopmental disorders (Calame et al., 2023, PMC10432148; Rossor, 2024, PMC11377048; Paine et al., 2019, 10.1016/j.ajhg.2019.06.001; Fiorenzani et al., 2025, 10.1016/j.tig.2024.11.006) [F3]. 
- **Diagnostic Confidence:** High for distinguishing *DHX9* from *DHX30* when adult-onset or adolescent-onset axonal neuropathy is present [F3, F19]. However, this distinction has low utility in infancy and early childhood, because neuropathy manifests later in life and does not systematically co-occur with early-onset cognitive delay within the same individual (Calame et al., 2023, 10.1101/2023.03.01.23286475v1.full-text) [F1, F19].
- **Cardiomyopathy / Conduction Defects:** An isolated report identified ventricular non-compaction cardiomyopathy in a patient harboring an ATP-binding domain variant p.(Gly414Arg) (Yamada et al., 2023, 10.1016/j.ejmg.2023.104797) [F7, F8]. While structural septal defects or normal cardiac morphology typify *DHX30*, confidence in using non-compaction cardiomyopathy as a discriminating marker for *DHX9* is low because it has been documented in only a single patient (N=1) and non-compaction phenotypes occur in other helicase defects such as *DHX36* (Yang et al., 2022, 10.1002/ame2.12251) [F8, F12].

## 3. Pertinent Absent Findings and Non-Exclusionary Pitfalls

### Findings Absent in Discrete Cohort Subsets
- **Absence of Seizures in Loss-of-Function Alleles:** Across the seminal cohort of 20 individuals evaluated by Calame et al. (2023), epileptic seizures were entirely absent in patients carrying loss-of-function (LoF) truncating alleles (frameshift, nonsense, or splice-site mutations), being restricted exclusively to missense alleles within conserved functional domains (Calame et al., 2023, 10.1016/j.ajhg.2023.06.013) [F4]. Thus, the presence of early-onset refractory seizures argues strongly against a simple haploinsufficient LoF variant [F4].
- **Absence of Cognitive Impairment in Isolated Neuropathy:** Intellectual disability and global developmental delay are absent in individuals presenting with isolated peripheral axonal neuropathy caused by core helicase variants, demonstrating that neurodevelopmental delay is not obligate for all pathogenic *DHX9* variants (Calame et al., 2023, 10.1016/j.ajhg.2023.06.013) [F1, F19].

### Features Mistakenly Presumed to Exclude DHX9 (Non-Exclusionary Pitfalls)
- **Congenital Microcephaly:** Clinical assumptions that microcephaly excludes *DHX9* are refuted; microcephaly is an established feature in severe phenotypes, particularly in patients harboring nuclear localization signal (NLS) missense variants (Genomics England PanelApp; Calame et al., 2023, 10.1016/j.ajhg.2023.06.013) [F18].
- **Structural Cardiac Abnormalities:** Congenital heart defects or cardiomyopathy do not argue against *DHX9*; systemic cardiac involvement occurs in 4/14 assessed patients, including ventricular non-compaction cardiomyopathy (Calame et al., 2023, PMC10432148; Yamada et al., 2023, PMID:37369308) [F7, F18].
- **Sensorineural Hearing Loss:** Although prominent in homozygous knockout (*Dhx9*−/−) mice, sensorineural hearing loss is not a defining human phenotype in monoallelic *DHX9* cohorts and should not be used as an inclusion criterion (Calame et al., 2023, PMC10432148; Yamada et al., 2023, 10.1016/j.ejmg.2023.104797) [F16].

## 4. Phenotypic Correlations Across Variant Type, Position, Age, and Sex

*DHX9* exhibits pronounced genotype-phenotype correlations dictated by variant class and topological domain position:

```
DHX9 Variant Locus & Molecular Mechanism
  │
  ├── Loss-of-Function (LoF) Truncating Alleles
  │     └── Mild NDD / ID; absence of epileptic seizures; preserved phrase speech [F1, F4, F15]
  │
  ├── Nuclear Localization Signal (NLS) Missense Variants
  │     └── Cytoplasmic mislocalization; severe NDD; microcephaly; epileptic seizures [F1, F4, F10, F18]
  │
  ├── N-Terminal dsRBD Missense Variants (e.g., p.Arg141Gln)
  │     └── Retained nuclear localization; elevated R-loop accumulation; severe genomic stress [F9, F10]
  │
  └── Helicase Core & Nucleotide-Binding Variants
        └── Nucleolar sequestration under stress; axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease (CMT2) [F3, F10, F19]
              └── Late adolescent / adult onset (distal symmetric weakness, muscle wasting)
```

### Variant Class and Functional Domain
- **Loss-of-Function vs Missense:** Heterozygous truncating loss-of-function variants induce haploinsufficiency and lead to milder neurodevelopmental delays without unprovoked seizures (Calame et al., 2023, 10.1016/j.ajhg.2023.06.013) [F4, F10]. In contrast, missense variants targeting the nuclear localization signal (NLS) lead to cytoplasmic protein accumulation and cause severe developmental delay, motor impairment, and microcephaly (Calame et al., 2023, PMC10432148; Lederbauer et al., 2024, 10.3389/fnmol.2024.1414949) [F4, F10, F18].
- **Helicase Core Variants:** Missense alterations residing in the central helicase core and nucleotide-binding domain (such as p.Gly414Arg) impair ATPase activity or promote nucleolar entrapment under cellular stress, correlating with adult-onset axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease (CMT2) rather than early severe dysmorphic encephalopathy (Yamada et al., 2023, 10.1016/j.ejmg.2023.104797; Calame et al., 2023, 10.1016/j.ajhg.2023.06.013) [F3, F10, F19].

### Age and Sex
- **Age-Dependent Expression:** *DHX9* manifestations segregate temporally. Neurodevelopmental delay, axial hypotonia, and early seizure onset present in infancy and early childhood (Calame et al., 2023, 10.1016/j.ajhg.2023.06.013) [F4, F17]. In contrast, axonal neuropathy (CMT2) exhibits delayed penetrance, with adult onset documented in 2 of 3 evaluated neuropathy cases (Calame et al., 2023, 10.1101/2023.03.01.23286475v1.full-text) [F19].
- **Sex Distribution:** Pathogenic variants occur de novo in both males and females without evidence of sex-biased penetrance or sexual dimorphism in clinical severity (Calame et al., 2023, 10.1016/j.ajhg.2023.06.013) [F1, F4].

## 5. Contradictory and Contested Findings in the Literature

Literature on *DHX9* exhibits several notable points of tension and divergence across reported cohorts:

1. **Cardiac Involvement: Incidental vs Core Organopathy**
   - *Yamada et al. (2023, PMID:37369308)* reported left ventricular non-compaction cardiomyopathy in an individual with the ATP-binding domain variant p.(Gly414Arg) and proposed that cardiac dysfunction represents a distinct component of *DHX9* pathology [F7, F8, F12].
   - In contrast, the broader multicenter cohort of *Calame et al. (2023, PMC10432148)* noted non-specific cardiac findings in 4 of 14 assessed individuals, without establishing ventricular non-compaction as a recurrent feature across patients [F7, F12]. Furthermore, mouse models harboring homologous p.Gly416Arg alterations developed cardiac conduction defects rather than structural non-compaction cardiomyopathy (Yamada et al., 2023, 10.1016/j.ejmg.2023.104797) [F8].

2. **Epileptogenesis and Disease Classification**
   - *Calame et al. (2023, 10.1016/j.ajhg.2023.06.013)* observed epileptic seizures strictly in missense variant carriers, reporting that seizures are not ubiquitous across the general *DHX9* spectrum [F4, F14].
   - In contrast, *Specchio et al. (2025, 10.1111/epi.18209)* and *Chen (2025, 10.1016/j.brainresbull.2025.111650)* classify *DHX9* as a developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE) gene, reflecting contrasting cohort selection between general genetics clinics and tertiary epilepsy programs [F14].

3. **Craniofacial Distinctiveness and Penetrance**
   - Early reviews posited that facial dysmorphism is present in a clear majority (>60%) of patients [F5]. However, granular analysis of primary clinical data shows dysmorphic features in 8/14 individuals (57.1%), demonstrating that dysmorphic signs fall short of being cardinal or pathognomonic (Calame et al., 2023, 10.1016/j.ajhg.2023.06.013) [F2, F5].
