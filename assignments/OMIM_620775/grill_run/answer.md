# Phenotypic Identification of Congenital Neuromuscular Disorder with Dysmorphic Facies (OMIM #620775 / *FILIP1*)

## Introduction and Core Clinical Cohort Profile

Congenital neuromuscular disorder with dysmorphic facies (NMDF; OMIM #620775) is an ultra-rare autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic loss-of-function or pathogenic variants in *FILIP1* (*filamin-A-interacting protein 1*) on chromosome 6q14 [F3]. FILIP1 encodes a structural regulator involved in actin dynamics and filamin degradation (FLNa and FLNc) in skeletal muscle and neurodevelopment [F1, F3, F14]. 

The definitive published clinical evidence base is founded upon two seminal discovery cohorts detailing exactly 10 individuals across 7 independent consanguineous families: the cohort of Schnabel et al. (2023, 10.1007/s00439-023-02528-2) encompassing 5 affected individuals across 3 unrelated consanguineous families, and the cohort of Roos et al. (2023, PMID:37163662) reporting 5 affected individuals across 4 unrelated families [F1, F2, F3]. Supplemental phenotypic expansion has been established by Watts et al. (2024, 10.1093/braincomms/fcae330) reporting an affected individual with paternal uniparental disomy 6 (UPD6) unmasking a pathogenic *FILIP1* variant [F1, F5, F6, F9], and Chaurasia et al. (2026, 10.1038/s41431-026-02158-y) describing homozygous copy-number deletions [F2, F8].

## Discriminative Features and Differential Diagnosis

When evaluating a patient with overlapping congenital weakness and dysmorphisms, the clinical discriminator profile consists of a triad: **generalized arthrogryposis multiplex congenita**, **congenital microcephaly**, and **reduced palmar/plantar skin creases** [F2, F4, F6].

### Overlapping Disorders and Confidence of Clinical Distinction

1. **Spinal Muscular Atrophy with Respiratory Distress Type 1 (SMARD1 / *IGHMBP2*):**
   - *Overlapping findings:* Severe infant hypotonia, distal contractures/foot deformities, respiratory compromise [F7].
   - *Differentiating features:* Neonatal respiratory distress is present in nearly all SMARD1 infants (early diaphragmatic paralysis), but was documented in **0/5 (0%)** of initial neonatal presentations in Roos et al. (2023) [F7]. Furthermore, SMARD1 does not feature dysmorphic facies or microcephaly [F3, F4].
   - *Confidence:* **High** [F3, F7].

2. **Dystrophinopathies and Congenital Muscular Dystrophies (e.g., LAMA2-RD, Fukuyama, Walker-Warburg):**
   - *Overlapping findings:* Congenital hypotonia, contractures, delayed motor milestones, CNS involvement [F5, F16].
   - *Differentiating features:* Serum creatine kinase (CK) levels in *FILIP1* disease are normal or only mildly elevated (<3–5× upper limit of normal), directly contrasting with the marked CK elevations (>10–50× ULN) pathognomonic of classic congenital muscular dystrophies or dystrophinopathies [F5]. Additionally, while *FILIP1* patients exhibit structural CNS malformations [F1], they lack severe cobblestone lissencephaly or ocular malformations characteristic of alpha-dystroglycanopathies [F16].
   - *Confidence:* **High** [F1, F5, F16].

3. **Classic Congenital Core Myopathies (e.g., *RYR1*, *SEPN1/SELENON*, *ACTA1*):**
   - *Overlapping findings:* Core-like myofibrillar disintegration on muscle biopsy, facial weakness, axial contractures/scoliosis [F3, F11, F15].
   - *Differentiating features:* Obligate microcephaly (5/5 in truncating cohorts) [F4] and obliteration of distal palmar and plantar skin folds (5/5 in Schnabel et al. 2023; 1/1 in Watts et al. 2024) [F6] are virtually never present in isolated congenital core myopathies [F4, F6].
   - *Confidence:* **High** [F4, F6].

## Negative Discriminators: Features Absent or Atypical in *FILIP1* Disease

Specific phenotypic findings have been looked for and found absent or unusually rare, serving as key negative discriminators:

1. **Immediate Neonatal Respiratory Failure:** Severe congenital respiratory failure requiring immediate neonatal mechanical ventilation was documented in **0/5 (0%)** neonates evaluated by Roos et al. (2023) [F7]. The presence of catastrophic respiratory failure at birth strongly points toward conditions like SMARD1, myotubular myopathy (*MTM1*), or severe congenital hypomyelinating neuropathies rather than *FILIP1* disease [F7].
2. **Marked Serum Creatine Kinase Elevation:** Massive CK elevations (>1,000 U/L) have been consistently absent; CK is normal or only marginally elevated (<3–5× ULN) [F5]. A massive CK elevation strongly argues in favor of dystrophinopathy, sarcoglycanopathy, or *LAMA2*-related dystrophy [F5].
3. **Normal Palmar/Plantar Creases in the Context of Severe AMC:** All evaluated AMC cases secondary to truncating *FILIP1* mutations exhibited profound loss/reduction of palmar and plantar creases (5/5 in Schnabel et al. 2023; 1/1 in Watts et al. 2024) [F6]. The preservation of deep, normal distal flexion creases in a child with arthrogryposis multiplex congenita argues against homozygous loss-of-function *FILIP1* mutations [F6].

## Phenotypic Variations: Stratification by Variant Type, Position, Age, and Sex

- **Variant Type:** Truncating loss-of-function variants (nonsense, frameshift, and homozygous structural copy-number deletions spanning up to 86 kb) consistently generate severe arthrogryposis multiplex congenita with microcephaly and distal crease obliteration (Schnabel et al. 2023; Chaurasia et al. 2026) [F2, F4, F6, F8]. In contrast, the clinical presentation reported by Roos et al. (2023) also manifested core-like myopathy and neurodevelopmental delay [F1, F3, F13].
- **Variant Position:** Published discovery cohorts do not currently demonstrate a definitive intragenic position-dependent phenotypic gradient along the *FILIP1* coding sequence [F13, F17].
- **Age:** The syndrome presents congenitally with multi-joint contractures, microcephaly, and facial dysmorphism at birth [F2, F4, F10]. Longitudinal assessment demonstrates that respiratory vulnerability may evolve into recurrent childhood chest infections (2/5 in Roos et al. 2023) [F7], and structural myopathy or motor limitations become prominent with post-neonatal development [F3, F15].
- **Sex:** No sex-linked phenotypic divergence has been identified; the disorder is inherited as an autosomal recessive trait with equal expressivity and penetrance in males and females across consanguineous kindreds [F1, F3].

## Contradictory and Contested Literature Findings

Evaluation of the pooled evidence base reveals several deprioritised and contested observations between studies:

1. **Absence vs. Presence of Structural CNS Malformations:** Early board hypotheses posited that marked structural CNS malformations are absent, allowing simple separation from Walker-Warburg or Fukuyama congenital muscular dystrophies [F16]. This was contradicted by Roos et al. (2023, PMID:37163662), who reported brain malformations in all 5/5 patients assessed in their cohort, confirming that central nervous system dysgenesis is an integral part of the *FILIP1* phenotypic spectrum [F1, F16].
2. **Universality of Generalized Limb Muscle Weakness:** Roos et al. (2023) characterized the disease primarily as a core-like congenital myopathy with weakness in 5/5 patients [F3, F15]. However, Watts et al. (2024, 10.1093/braincomms/fcae330) specifically documented an affected patient harboring pathogenic *FILIP1* disruption who exhibited facial hypotonia and joint contractures but *lacked* classic limb muscle weakness and generalized flaccidity [F9, F11, F15]. Consequently, generalized limb muscle weakness cannot be considered an absolute 100% universal finding across all clinical presentations [F9, F15].
3. **Craniofacial/Axial Hypotonia vs. Generalized Flaccidity:** Several studies highlight prominent facial hypotonia and axial contractures alongside arthrogryposis [F11], whereas muscle biopsy findings of core-like myofibrillar disruptions support generalized myopathic involvement [F11, F14].
4. **Proposed Binary Genotype-Phenotype Divergence (Truncating vs. Missense):** Early hypotheses suggested that truncating variants cause severe prenatal akinesia AMC, whereas missense variants preserve partial function leading to isolated progressive core myopathy [F13, F14]. This binary hypothesis was deprioritised/refuted because the primary literature lacks established comparative cohorts of isolated homozygous missense variants, and Roos et al. (2023) described truncating nonsense variants that manifested primarily as congenital myopathy rather than prenatal akinesia AMC [F13, F17].

## Synthesis, Cross-Cutting Patterns, and Diagnostic Algorithm

### Clinical Diagnostic Algorithm

To identify a patient with *FILIP1*-related congenital neuromuscular disorder from their phenotype alone, clinicians should apply the following decision framework:

```
Patient with Congenital Joint Contractures / Arthrogryposis Multiplex Congenita
  │
  ├── Dysmorphic Facies (myopathic face, ptosis, micrognathia)? ─────────► [Required: 10/10 in core cohorts]
  │
  ├── Microcephaly Present? ─────────────────────────────────────────────► [Highly specific: 5/5 in AMC LoF cohort]
  │
  ├── Reduced / Absent Palmar and Plantar Skin Creases? ─────────────────► [Obligate dermatoglyphic marker: 6/6 evaluated]
  │
  ├── Serum Creatine Kinase (CK) < 3-5x ULN? ────────────────────────────► [Confirms non-dystrophic pattern; rules out DMD/LAMA2]
  │
  └── Preserved Initial Neonatal Respiration? ───────────────────────────► [Distinguishes from SMARD1 / MTM1]
       │
       ▼
Strongly Indicated for Targeted FILIP1 Sequencing & Copy-Number Loss Analysis
```

### Key Clinical Considerations
- **Dermatoglyphic Markers:** Palmar and plantar crease obliteration is a hallmark sign of profound, early-onset intrauterine akinesia and distal hypokinesia, appearing in 100% of assessed *FILIP1* arthrogryposis patients [F6].
- **Structural CNV Screening:** In consanguineous or resource-limited cohorts, homozygous structural deletions (e.g., 86 kb microdeletions) account for an under-recognized proportion of undiagnosed syndromic AMC and microcephaly; CNV calling algorithms must be incorporated into standard exome evaluation [F8].
- **Central and Peripheral Duality:** Clinicians must not view OMIM #620775 purely as a peripheral myopathy; structural brain anomalies and microcephaly reflect *FILIP1*’s vital role in filamin-mediated neuroblast migration and cortical organization [F1, F4].
