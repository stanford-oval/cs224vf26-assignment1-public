# Clinical Identification of Ramond-Elliott Neurodevelopmental Syndrome (TRA2B)

## Executive Summary & Worldwide Cohort Definition

Ramond-Elliott neurodevelopmental syndrome (RAMELN; OMIM #621421) is an ultra-rare monogenic neurodevelopmental disorder caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants in *TRA2B* (transformer 2 beta homolog) on chromosome 3q27 (F1, F2). Identifying an affected individual from their clinical presentation alone is challenging due to phenotypic convergence with other early-onset epileptic encephalopathies and microcephalic syndromes (F6).

Across the published medical literature, the total worldwide cohort consists of **13 unique individuals across 12 unrelated families** (F2):
1. **The Discovery Cohort**: 12 affected individuals from 11 unrelated families reported by Ramond et al. (2023, PMID: 36549593; DOI: 10.1016/j.gim.2022.10.013) (F1, F2).
2. **The Second Case Study**: 1 individual reported by Shatokhina et al. (2023, DOI: 10.3390/ijms242115572) (F2, F4).

Secondary literature databases and registries occasionally misattribute or inflate this denominator to 15 individuals, but primary auditing confirms a cumulative total of exactly 13 published patients (F1, F2, F10).

## Discriminating Findings and Differential Diagnosis

Bedside differentiation of Ramond-Elliott syndrome relies on comparing its constellation of symptoms against specific clinical mimics (F6). However, the degree of diagnostic confidence varies substantially depending on the comparative disease class (F6, F8).

### 1. Isolated Primary Microcephalies (e.g., *ASPM*, *WDR62*, *MCPH1*)
- **Discriminators**: The development of **infantile spasms (HP:0012469)**, early epileptic encephalopathy, and **early-onset feeding difficulties (HP:0011968)** strongly separates Ramond-Elliott syndrome from non-syndromic primary microcephaly (F6).
- **Diagnostic Confidence**: **High**. Isolated primary microcephalies do not typically exhibit early-onset epileptic encephalopathy with hypsarrhythmia/infantile spasms or severe feeding failure in the neonatal/infantile period (F6, F12).

### 2. Other Microcephalic Developmental and Epileptic Encephalopathies (DEEs; e.g., *ADARB1* Deficiency)
- **Overlapping Features**: Bi-allelic *ADARB1* variants and related microcephalic DEEs present with severe microcephaly, profound intellectual disability, early-onset seizures, and severe feeding difficulties (present in 4/4 [100%] of *ADARB1* individuals) (F6, F12).
- **Discriminators**: There are **no reliable positive clinical discriminators** at the bedside (F6, F12). The positive features of *TRA2B* possess near-zero specificity when evaluated within a cohort of microcephalic DEE patients (F6).
- **Diagnostic Confidence**: **Near-Zero (Clinical bedside alone)**. Purely phenotypic separation is impossible; definitive identification requires molecular genetic testing (exome or genome sequencing) or transcriptomic RNA-splicing profiles (F6).

### 3. Syndromic Spliceosomopathies and Chromatinopathies (e.g., CHARGE Syndrome)
- **Discriminators**: Ramond-Elliott syndrome is characterized by the **absence** of major craniofacial/sensory organ malformations (coloboma, choanal atresia) and major conotruncal heart defects (F7, F8).
- **Diagnostic Confidence**: **Moderate to High**. The absence of structural ocular malformations (0% in *TRA2B*) provides a reliable differential filter against CHARGE syndrome (*CHD7*) and related syndromic ocular conditions (F8).

## Negative Clinical Criteria: Findings Absent in Affected Individuals

Findings consistently evaluated and found **absent** in molecularly confirmed *TRA2B* individuals provide essential exclusionary filters. If present, the following features argue strongly against a diagnosis of Ramond-Elliott syndrome:

1. **Severe Structural Ocular Malformations (0/12 assessed, 0%)**:
   - **Coloboma (HP:0000589)** and **Microphthalmia (HP:0000568)** have not been observed in any patient with Ramond-Elliott syndrome (0/12, 0%) (F7, F8). Their presence points toward alternative diagnoses such as CHARGE syndrome, *PAX6* spectrum, or ocular-coloboma-associated chromatinopathies (F8).
2. **Major Structural Congenital Heart Defects (HP:0001627)**:
   - Complex conotruncal defects, transposition of the great arteries, tetralogy of Fallot, or hypoplastic left heart syndrome are absent (F7). While minor non-structural or resolving cardiac findings have been noted in 2/12 individuals (16.7%), major structural cardiac malformations have not been reported (F7).
3. **Postaxial Polydactyly and Limb Reduction Defects**:
   - Major limb patterning defects are absent in *TRA2B* cohorts (F7).

*Note on Statistical Power*: While severe ocular malformations and major cardiac defects have not been observed in the primary cohort, the small total cohort size ($N=12$ to $13$) means that by the mathematical rule of three, rare manifestations up to ~25% frequency cannot be ruled out with >95% certainty purely on sample statistics (F17). Nevertheless, clinically, their presence strongly favors an alternative diagnosis (F7, F8).

## Genotype-Phenotype Correlations by Variant Type, Position, Age, and Sex

### 1. Variant Position and Mutational Hotspot
- **Strict 5' Hotspot Clustering**: All 13 molecularly confirmed germline pathogenic variants in *TRA2B* cluster strictly within the **5' coding region (exon 2 / RS1 domain)** upstream of an alternative translation initiation site (F2, F5, F16).
- **Absence of Gene-Wide Loss-of-Function Phenotype**: Heterozygous loss-of-function variants distributed throughout the broader gene do not cause classic Ramond-Elliott syndrome; copy-number deletions spanning *TRA2B* in 35 control individuals do not manifest this syndromic phenotype (F5). Pathogenicity requires localized 5' spatial disruption (F5).
- **Mechanism**: Pathogenic 5' variants result in translation initiation at an internal downstream methionine, generating an N-terminally truncated TRA2B isoform lacking the RS1 domain that exerts dominant-negative or toxic effects on spliceosome assembly, rather than haploinsufficiency (F5, F16).

### 2. Variant Type
- The reported pathogenic spectrum consists of clustered predicted truncating/frameshift variants and specific splice-site variants in the 5' coding region (exon 2) (F1, F2, F5). 
- Recurrent missense variants affecting the RNA-recognition motif (RRM) or RS domains have not been identified as the causative mechanism for classic Ramond-Elliott syndrome (F16).

### 3. Patient Age
- **Evolution of Seizure Semiography**: Epileptic manifestations follow an age-dependent trajectory. Onset typically occurs in infancy as infantile spasms (HP:0012469) (50–54%), frequently transitioning during childhood to refractory generalized epilepsy and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome patterns (HP:0002373) (F3, F12, F14).
- **Microcephaly**: Often identified as congenital or progressive postnatal deceleration of head circumference ($-4$ SD in severely affected individuals) (F1, F11).

### 4. Patient Sex
- No statistically significant phenotypic divergence or penetrance difference by sex has been identified across the 13 affected individuals (F1, F2, F5).

## Literature Contradictions and Evaluation of Deprioritised Hypotheses

### 1. Direct Literature Contradiction: Early Feeding Difficulties
- **Ramond et al. (2023, PMID: 36549593)** reported that early feeding difficulties (HP:0011968) occurred in **7/12 (58.3%)** of individuals, presenting it as a common clinical feature co-occurring with microcephaly (F9, F11).
- **Shatokhina et al. (2023, DOI: 10.3390/ijms242115572)** explicitly documented that their confirmed patient with a *TRA2B* splice variant presented with infantile spasms, hypotonia, and severe delay, but **completely lacked feeding difficulties (0/1)** (F9, F12).
- **Resolution**: Early feeding difficulty is a **variable and incomplete** secondary manifestation rather than an obligate diagnostic criterion (F9, F11). Its absence cannot exclude a *TRA2B* diagnosis in a child presenting with microcephaly and infantile spasms (F9).

## Synthesis: Clinical Diagnostic Decision Framework and Open Questions

### Clinical Bedside Decision Framework
When evaluating an undiagnosed pediatric patient with severe global developmental delay, microcephaly, and hypotonia:

1. **Rule-Out via Major Malformations (High Negative Predictive Value)**: Check for ocular coloboma, microphthalmia, and cyanotic/complex congenital heart defects. If present, prioritize CHARGE syndrome or complex chromosomal/chromatin disorders over *TRA2B* (F7, F8).
2. **Assess Neurologic Co-Morbidities**: Evaluate for infantile spasms or early epileptic encephalopathy. If present, isolated primary microcephalies (*ASPM*, *WDR62*) are eliminated (F6).
3. **Assess Clinical Indistinguishability**: If the patient presents with microcephaly, early hypotonia, infantile spasms, and severe developmental delay, clinical bedside examination **cannot** distinguish *TRA2B* from *ADARB1* deficiency or other microcephalic DEEs (F6, F12).
4. **Molecular Confirmation**: Mandatory genetic confirmation must look specifically at the **5' coding region of *TRA2B* (exon 2 / RS1 domain)**. A standard loss-of-function variant downstream of the internal alternative start site or whole-gene deletion is unlikely to explain the classic Ramond-Elliott phenotype (F5, F16).

### Cross-Cutting Patterns and Open Questions
- **Incomplete Expressivity of Seizures and Feeding**: While the neurodevelopmental core triad (ID/DD, hypotonia, microcephaly) exhibits >80–100% penetrance, infantile spasms (~54%) and feeding failure (~54%) display variable expressivity (F1, F4, F9). Further research is required to determine whether background variations in neuronal ion channels or secondary downstream alternative splicing targets act as clinical modifiers.
- **Molecular Splicing Signatures vs Clinical Phenotyping**: Because bedside phenomics cannot resolve *TRA2B* from other microcephalic DEEs, developing blood- or fibroblast-derived transcriptomic RNA-splicing profiles (detecting aberrant exon-skipping events induced by the N-terminally truncated TRA2B protein) is an urgent translational priority to provide rapid molecular diagnostic confirmation.
