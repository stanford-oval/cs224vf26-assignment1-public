### Clinical Phenotypes and Cohort-Specific Frequencies in MMDS9B

Multiple mitochondrial dysfunctions syndrome 9B (MMDS9B, OMIM #620887) is caused by biallelic pathogenic variants in *FDXR*, encoding ferredoxin reductase—a mitochondrial iron-sulfur (Fe-S) cluster assembly electron-transfer partner (F1; F4). Identifying an individual with MMDS9B within a cohort presenting with overlapping neuro-ophthalmic, neuro-otologic, or neuromuscular deficits requires precise phenotyping across organ systems (F1; F3).

The core phenotypic hallmark of MMDS9B is a combined optic-acoustic neurodegeneration, present in over 85–90% of assessed individuals across independent cohorts (F1; F6). Secondary manifestations involve sensorimotor peripheral polyneuropathy, cerebellar ataxia, and early-onset rod-cone retinal degeneration (F1; F2; F6).

### Diagnostic Discriminators: Differentiating MMDS9B from Overlapping Syndromes

When evaluating a patient with complex neurosensory or neuromuscular decline, several primary differential diagnostic categories emerge (F3; F5). The following phenotypic features allow clinical discrimination:

#### 1. Differentiation from Primary Mitochondrial Optic Neuropathies (LHON and OPA1-DOA)
* **Overlapping Presentation:** Bilateral central visual loss, optic disc pallor, and visual field defects (F2; F5).
* **Discriminating Features:** 
  * *Retinal Dystrophy and Photoreceptor Dysfunction:* Leber hereditary optic neuropathy (LHON) and *OPA1*-related dominant optic atrophy (DOA) predominantly spare photoreceptors and retinal vasculature (F5). By contrast, FDXR deficiency routinely causes rod-cone retinal dystrophy, early-onset severe retinal dystrophy, or congenital amaurosis (F2; F5).
  * *Retinal Vascular Attenuation / Silver Wiring:* Prominent vessel attenuation appearing as thin white threads or ghost vessels ('silver wiring'), confirmed by vessel occlusion on fluorescein angiography, is present in 60–100% of ophthalmologically examined FDXR patients (3/5 in Wei et al., 2023; 11/11 in Yi et al., 2023) (F5). In a cohort of 826 families with hereditary optic neuropathy (nHON), concurrent retinal degeneration was seen in only 17.57%, whereas 78.79% had isolated optic atrophy (Zheng et al., 2025, [10.1093/brain/awae316](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awae316)) (F5).
* **Diagnostic Confidence:** **High**. The coexistence of profound retinal vessel attenuation and rod-cone retinal dystrophy alongside optic atrophy strongly argues against isolated LHON or *OPA1*-DOA (F2; F5).

#### 2. Differentiation from Upstream Multiple Mitochondrial Dysfunctions Syndromes (MMDS1–3)
* **Overlapping Presentation:** Autosomal recessive mitochondrial Fe-S cluster assembly defects affecting lipoate- and Fe-S-dependent enzymes (F3; F4).
* **Discriminating Features:**
  * Upstream syndromes—MMDS1 (*NFU1*), MMDS2 (*BOLA3*), and MMDS3 (*IBA57*)—classically manifest in neonates or young infants with fulminant lactic acidosis, early pulmonary hypertension, cystic cavitating leukodystrophy, and early death (F3; F4). 
  * In contrast, MMDS9B (*FDXR*) presents primarily as a progressive sensorial neuropathy (auditory neuropathy spectrum disorder [ANSD] and optic atrophy) during childhood, adolescence, or adulthood, without catastrophic infantile systemic collapse (F3; F4).
* **Diagnostic Confidence:** **High**. Lack of infantile pulmonary hypertension and absence of neonatal cystic leukodystrophy clearly separates classical MMDS9B from MMDS1–3 (F3; F4).

#### 3. Differentiation from Autoimmune Neuropathies (GBS and CIDP)
* **Overlapping Presentation:** Acute or subacute progressive motor-sensory weakness, hyporeflexia, and gait ataxia (F3; F7).
* **Discriminating Features:**
  * A distinct subset of juvenile or young-adult FDXR patients presents with acute/subacute flaccid quadriparesis or polyneuropathy closely mimicking Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS) or chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (CIDP) (Masnada et al., 2023; Tafakhori et al., 2026, [10.1186/s12883-026-04752-5](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12883-026-04752-5)) (F3; F7).
  * In the literature series analyzed by Masnada et al. (2023), 20% (2/10) of neuropathy cases were initially suspected of having GBS (F7). Ataxia is documented in 43.18% (19/44) and polyneuropathy in 22.72% (10/44) (F3; F6; F7).
  * Clues favoring MMDS9B include subclinical optic disc pallor, abnormal auditory brainstem responses (ABR) indicative of ANSD, and unresponsiveness to intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIg) or plasma exchange (F2; F7).
* **Diagnostic Confidence:** **Moderate-to-High** once dedicated neuro-ophthalmologic (OCT/funduscopy) and neuro-otologic (ABR/OAE) evaluations are performed (F2; F7).

### Negative Discriminators: Phenotypes Arguing Against MMDS9B

Certain clinical, biochemical, and neuroimaging findings are typical of other metabolic or mitochondrial diseases but are conspicuously absent or atypical in FDXR-associated MMDS9B. Their presence should argue against or prompt reconsideration of an MMDS9B diagnosis (F3; F4; F8; F9):

1. **Severe Neonatal Nonketotic Hyperglycinemia:** Marked neonatal glycine accumulation (massive plasma and CSF glycine elevations) is characteristic of primary glycine encephalopathy and MMDS1–3, but is absent in juvenile- and adult-onset FDXR deficiency (F4; F9).
2. **Infantile Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension:** Frequent and severe in MMDS1 (*NFU1*) and MMDS2 (*BOLA3*), but absent in the natural history of MMDS9B (F3; F4).
3. **Cystic Cavitating Leukodystrophy:** Widespread cystic degeneration of cerebral white matter typical of MMDS3 (*IBA57*) and *BOLA3* mutations is absent in typical MMDS9B neuroimaging (F3; F4).
4. **Persistent Marked Lactic Acidemia in Juvenile/Adult Presentations:** Elevated CSF and plasma lactate and severe microcephaly are restricted to the rare infantile Leigh-like encephalopathy presentation of FDXR deficiency; their occurrence in older children or adults without acute metabolic decompensation argues against typical MMDS9B (F8; F9).

### Phenotype Stratification by Variant Type, Position, Age, and Sex

* **Age of Onset Stratification:** 
  * *Infantile-onset (< 2 years):* Severe neurodegenerative or Leigh-like encephalopathy, congenital amaurosis, profound microcephaly, developmental arrest/regression, and early mortality (18% infant mortality across cohorts; Campbell et al., 2024) (F1; F2; F6; F8).
  * *Juvenile/Adult-onset (mean hearing loss onset ~11 years, range 2–25 years; Paul et al., 2017):* Predominantly manifests as progressive optic-acoustic sensory neuropathy (ANSD and optic atrophy), with variable lower-extremity spasticity, sensory ataxia (43.2%), or sensorimotor peripheral polyneuropathy (22.7%) (F2; F3; F6; F7).
* **Variant Type and Location:**
  * Biallelic null/truncating variants or severe missense mutations affecting critical electron-transfer domains (FAD- or NADPH-binding pockets) typically produce early infantile encephalopathy (F8; F12).
  * However, severe phenotypes are not exclusive to null alleles: patients harboring exclusively missense variants have presented with congenital amaurosis and early-onset severe retinal dystrophy in the first months of life (Yi et al., 2023) (F2; F12).
* **Sex-Specific and Extra-Neurological Findings:**
  * FDXR transfers electrons to ferredoxin (FDX1/FDX2), supporting not only Fe-S cluster biogenesis but also mitochondrial cytochrome P450 enzymes essential for steroidogenesis (CYP11A1) (F4; F12).
  * Biallelic variants can cause primary adrenal insufficiency and disorders of sex development (46,XX DSD / atypical genitalia) (Pignatti et al., 2024; Miller, 2021) (F12). Thus, phenotypic evaluation must look beyond purely neurological findings.

### Contested Hypotheses and Refuted Claims in the Literature

Several hypotheses in the published literature have been deprioritized, contested, or refuted based on conflicting evidence:

#### 1. Refuted Claim: Majority Frequency (70–85%) of Neuromuscular Symptoms
* *Hypothesis:* Gait ataxia, peripheral sensory-motor neuropathy, and hypotonia are present in 70–85% of pediatric and juvenile MMDS9B cases (F16).
* *Reason Refuted:* Systematic analysis of 44 literature cases by Masnada et al. (2023, [10.1007/s10072-023-06790-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10072-023-06790-0)) established that sensorimotor peripheral polyneuropathy occurs in only 22.72% (10/44) and gait ataxia in 43.18% (19/44) (F3; F6; F16). While optic atrophy is present in ~89–100%, broader neuromuscular involvement is present in less than half of affected individuals (F1; F6; F16).

#### 2. Contested Progression: Obligate "Ocular-First" Temporal Sequence
* *Hypothesis:* Sensory deficit progression strictly follows an ocular-first pattern, with vision loss manifesting in early childhood (< 5 years) and ANSD delayed until late childhood or adolescence (>= 10 years) (F10).
* *Contradiction:* While ocular ascertainment cohorts report earlier visual symptoms (Yi et al., 2023), Paul et al. (2017) and Cao et al. (2025) identified auditory neuropathy manifesting as early as 2 years of age (F2; F10). Apparent sequence differences largely reflect clinical ascertainment bias (ophthalmic vs neurotology clinics) rather than an obligate biological order (F10).

#### 3. Contested Neurotologic Mechanism: Uniform Cochlear Implant (CI) Efficacy in ANSD
* *Hypothesis:* Auditory neuropathy in MMDS9B is purely post-synaptic, allowing uniformly successful rehabilitation via cochlear implantation with modified pulse widths (F11).
* *Contradiction:* Kanemoto (2022, PMC9029697) and Shin et al. (2025, [10.1097/mao.0000000000004567](https://doi.org/10.1097/mao.0000000000004567)) demonstrated that while short-term gains can occur, patients with post-synaptic mitochondrial ANSD experience lower functional outcomes (CAP/SIR scores) and secondary long-term speech perception decline due to ongoing progressive retrocochlear degeneration (F11).

#### 4. Contested Retinal Specificity: 100% Diagnostic Separation by ERG from LHON/OPA1
* *Hypothesis:* Severe rod-cone ERG impairment is present in 100% of FDXR cases and completely absent in LHON and *OPA1*-DOA (F15).
* *Contradiction:* Chang et al. (2022, [10.1167/iovs.63.5.5](https://doi.org/10.1167/iovs.63.5.5)) demonstrated ERG abnormalities in 5/7 LHON and 3/6 *OPA1* patients (F15). Furthermore, Jurkute et al. (2021) and Zheng et al. (2025) showed that some FDXR patients present initially with isolated optic atrophy before electroretinographic photoreceptor degeneration becomes detectable (F5; F15).

#### 5. Contested Biochemical Marker: Strict Maintenance of Normal CSF:Plasma Glycine Ratios
* *Hypothesis:* A normal CSF-to-plasma glycine ratio reliably excludes primary nonketotic hyperglycinemia in all FDXR presentations (F14).
* *Contradiction:* OMIM entry #620423 documents elevated CSF-to-plasma glycine ratios in severe infantile MMDS cases with *FDXR* mutations, demonstrating that borderline-to-elevated ratios can occasionally occur during acute metabolic decompensation (F14).

#### 6. Contested Neuroimaging: Brain Lactate Peaks Limited to <= 30% of Patients
* *Hypothesis:* MR spectroscopy (MRS) cerebral lactate peaks are uncommon (<= 30%) and intermittent in MMDS9B (F13).
* *Contradiction:* Lam et al. (2026, [10.1007/s00247-026-06734-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00247-026-06734-8)) state that MRS will classically reveal a markedly elevated lactate peak in decompensated FDXR cases, and Vanlander et al. (2018) describe frequent cerebral lactate peaks in decompensated cohorts (F13).

### Synthesis of Cross-Cutting Patterns and Diagnostic Workflow

Integrating the clinical, biochemical, and genetic evidence highlights four actionable principles for clinicians evaluating suspected MMDS9B:

1. **Combined Sensory Tropism:** Biallelic *FDXR* variants have near-obligate penetrance for combined optic atrophy and auditory neuropathy (>85–90%), reflecting the heightened vulnerability of retinal ganglion cells and auditory spiral ganglion/cochlear nerve neurons to impaired Fe-S cluster maturation and ferroptosis (F1; F2; F4). In any patient presenting with unexplained optic atrophy, screening with auditory brainstem responses (ABR) is essential.
2. **Microvascular Biomarkers:** Unlike primary mitochondrial optic neuropathies (LHON, *OPA1*), MMDS9B uniquely exhibits severe retinal arteriolar attenuation ('silver wiring' and non-perfusion on angiography) in 60–100% of assessed eyes (F5). Targeted funduscopy and fluorescein angiography provide immediate discriminative value (F5).
3. **Atypical Inflammatory Mimicry:** Clinicians must be vigilant for juvenile- or adult-onset subacute sensorimotor quadriparesis or polyneuropathy mimicking GBS or CIDP (F3; F7). When an apparent inflammatory neuropathy fails to respond to IVIg or plasma exchange, funduscopic examination and audiological testing can reveal subclinical optic disc pallor or auditory neuropathy, pointing toward *FDXR* sequencing (F7).
4. **Absence of Fulminant Neonatal Multiorgan Collapse:** The preservation of baseline metabolic stability (absence of early fatal pulmonary hypertension, cystic cavitating leukodystrophy, or persistent severe nonketotic hyperglycinemia) distinguishes MMDS9B from upstream MMDS subtypes (MMDS1–3), reflecting sufficient residual electron transfer to sustain basal cellular metabolism outside sensory neural systems (F3; F4; F9).
