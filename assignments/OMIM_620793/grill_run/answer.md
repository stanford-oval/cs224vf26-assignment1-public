# Clinical Identification and Phenotypic Characterization of SNUPN-Related Limb-Girdle Muscular Dystrophy (LGMDR29)

## Introduction and Diagnostic Overview of LGMDR29 (SNUPN Deficiency)
Autosomal recessive limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 29 (LGMDR29; OMIM #620793) is an ultra-rare, pleiotropic neuromuscular disorder caused by biallelic variants in the *SNUPN* gene, which encodes snurportin-1 (F1, F5). Snurportin-1 functions as an essential nuclear transport adaptor protein that binds trimethylguanosine-capped spliceosomal small nuclear ribonucleoproteins (snRNPs) and interacts with importin-beta to mediate their trafficking into the nucleus for spliceosome assembly (F5, F11). 

Clinically, biallelic variants in *SNUPN* produce a broad spectrum spanning classical childhood-onset limb-girdle muscular dystrophy, syndromic presentations characterized by neurodevelopmental delay and cataracts, adult-onset proximo-distal myopathies with prominent axial and respiratory weakness mimicking sporadic inclusion body myositis (sIBM), and isolated spinocerebellar ataxia/atrophy (SCA) (F1, F3, F4, F7). Because patients often present with non-specific limb-girdle weakness, high serum creatine kinase (CK), or neurodegenerative signs, identifying LGMDR29 from phenotype alone requires careful evaluation of muscle imaging patterns, deep finger flexor sparing, and subclinical cerebellar changes (F3, F6, F7).

## Phenotypic Discrimination and Differential Diagnosis
To isolate an individual with *SNUPN* deficiency out of a cohort of patients presenting with proximal or proximo-distal weakness, clinicians must rely on specific discriminatory imaging and physical examination signatures (F6, F7).

### Discrimination from Sporadic Inclusion Body Myositis (sIBM)
* **Confused disorder:** Sporadic inclusion body myositis (sIBM).
* **Overlapping features:** Adult onset, proximo-distal weakness pattern, axial muscle involvement, myopathic features with rimmed vacuoles and protein aggregations on muscle biopsy (F2, F7, F8).
* **Discriminating clinical signs:** In sIBM, deep finger flexor weakness (flexor digitorum profundus) is a hallmark early sign present in the vast majority of patients (F7). In adult-onset *SNUPN*-related myopathy, deep finger flexor power is completely preserved (0/2 affected siblings showed deep finger flexor weakness) (N Muelas et al., 2026, 10.1002/acn3.70211) [F7].
* **Discriminating MRI signs:** Muscle MRI in sIBM characteristically spares the sartorius and gracilis muscles while predominantly targeting the quadriceps (rectus femoris, vasti) (FM Cox et al., 2011, 10.1093/rheumatology/ker001) [F7]. Conversely, in *SNUPN* deficiency, the sartorius and gracilis exhibit consistent, early, and selective fatty infiltration along with the vasti and paravertebral muscles across both childhood and adult cases (5/5 assessed patients) (P Iruzubieta et al., 2024; N Muelas et al., 2026) [F6, F7, F10].
* **Confidence of distinction:** **High** (supported by clinical testing and skeletal muscle MRI) [F7].

### Discrimination from Other Recessive Limb-Girdle Muscular Dystrophies
* **Confused disorders:** Calpainopathy (LGMDR1/LGMD2A), dysferlinopathy (LGMDR2/LGMD2B), sarcoglycanopathies (LGMDR3-R6), and collagen VI-related dystrophies (F8, F13).
* **Discriminating signs:** 
  1. *Axial muscle predilection:* Severe, early, and selective paravertebral muscle atrophy and fatty infiltration are present in LGMDR29 across both childhood-onset (5/5 patients) and adult-onset (2/2 patients) cases (F6, F10).
  2. *Subclinical cerebellar atrophy:* Demonstrating cerebellar atrophy on cranial MRI in a patient with muscular dystrophy strongly discriminates LGMDR29 from classical sarcoglycanopathies, dysferlinopathies, and calpainopathies (F1, F3, F4).
  3. *Histopathological myofibrillar alterations:* Presence of myofibrillar-like changes, Z-disc disorganization, and ectopic protein aggregates (e.g., myotilin) alongside standard dystrophic changes distinguishes LGMDR29 from pure sarcolemmal dystrophies (P Iruzubieta et al., 2024, PMID:38366623) [F2].
* **Confidence of distinction:** **Moderate to High** [F1, F6, F7].

### Discrimination from Autosomal Recessive Cerebellar Ataxias
* **Confused disorders:** Recessive spinocerebellar ataxias, Friedreich ataxia, and ataxia with oculomotor apraxia (F1, F4).
* **Discriminating signs:** Presence of elevated serum CK, subclinical dystrophic/myopathic abnormalities on electromyography or muscle biopsy, and selective fatty degeneration of lower limb muscles on skeletal MRI distinguish *SNUPN*-related ataxia from pure primary ataxias (M Okubo et al., 2026, 10.1093/brain/awaf348; N Muelas et al., 2026, 10.1002/acn3.70211) [F2, F4, F5].
* **Confidence of distinction:** **Moderate** [F4, F5].

## Pertinent Negative Findings (Features Documented as Absent)
Documenting findings that have been explicitly looked for and found absent in affected patients helps clinicians exclude LGMDR29 when present:

1. **Absence of Deep Finger Flexor Weakness:** Adult-onset patients with IBM-like features demonstrate normal strength in the deep finger flexors (flexor digitorum profundus), contrasting sharply with sIBM (N Muelas et al., 2026, 10.1002/acn3.70211) [F7]. The presence of marked deep finger flexor weakness strongly argues against *SNUPN* deficiency.
2. **Absence of Sparing of Sartorius and Gracilis:** While sartorius and gracilis sparing on MRI is standard in many dystrophies and inflammatory myopathies (such as sIBM), these muscles are selectively degenerated in *SNUPN* myopathy (5/5 assessed patients) (F7, F10). A thigh MRI showing completely spared sartorius and gracilis with severe vasti involvement argues against LGMDR29.
3. **Absence of Structural Congenital Brain Malformations:** Brain imaging in pure childhood-onset LGMDR29 showed no gross cerebral cortical dysplasias, cobblestone lissencephaly, or structural brainstem malformations, distinguishing it from dystroglycanopathies (P Iruzubieta et al., 2024) [F15].
4. **Absence of Early Primary Cardiomyopathy in Classical Cohorts:** Systematic evaluation of cardiac structure and function in established multi-patient cohorts (e.g., 18 pediatric patients across 15 families) showed an absence of dilated cardiomyopathy, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, or early cardiac conduction blocks (M Nashabat et al., 2024, 10.1038/s41467-024-45933-5) [F13, F14].

## Genotype-Phenotype Correlations and Demographic Variations

### Variant Type and Functional Impact
Across 12 reported *SNUPN* variants, the underlying pathogenic alleles are primarily hypomorphic missense variants rather than complete loss-of-function null mutations (F1, F5, F11). Snurportin-1 is an essential nuclear transport receptor; complete biallelic null alleles appear to be non-viable or incompatible with survival. Pathogenic missense variants disrupt the structural integrity of the protein, alter its binding affinity to importin-beta, or induce abnormal cytoplasmic protein aggregation and oligomerization, leading to defective nuclear import of U snRNPs and downstream spliceosomal dysregulation (F5, F11).

### Variant Position and Organ Manifestation
Variants do not segregate into mutually exclusive categories for isolated muscular dystrophy versus isolated spinocerebellar ataxia (F5). Functional studies show that phenotypic expression (cerebellar Purkinje cell loss vs. skeletal muscle degeneration) is governed by cell-type-specific thresholds of vulnerability to global splicing disruption and U1 snRNP stoichiometry, rather than absolute variant position (M Okubo et al., 2026, 10.1093/brain/awaf348) [F1, F5].

### Age and Sex Variations
* **Age of Onset:** Phenotypic presentation varies substantially by age of onset: childhood-onset cases typically present with proximal weakness, contractures, and difficulty walking or running, whereas adult-onset presentations manifest with proximo-distal, axial, and triceps brachii weakness accompanied by early restrictive respiratory insufficiency (F1, F6, F8, F10, F14). However, muscle MRI demonstrates that topographic selectivity (paravertebral, vasti, gracilis, and sartorius involvement) is fundamentally invariant to age of onset (F6, F10).
* **Sex:** No significant sex-specific divergence in clinical severity, penetrance, or topographic distribution of weakness has been documented; both males and females are affected equally in accordance with autosomal recessive inheritance (F1, F11).

## Contested Hypotheses and Literature Contradictions

Several findings across the literature show explicit contradictions or divergent interpretations:

### 1. Isolated Childhood-Onset LGMD vs. Multisystem Pediatric Syndrome
* **Contested issue:** Whether pediatric *SNUPN* deficiency is a classic muscle-restricted limb-girdle muscular dystrophy or a syndromic, multisystem neurodevelopmental disorder (F12, F15).
* **Evidence & Conflict:** Nashabat et al. (2024, 10.1038/s41467-024-45933-5) identified 18/18 affected children from 15 families displaying an atypical multisystem muscular dystrophy with prominent neurological co-morbidities and neurodevelopmental defects [F1, F12]. In direct contrast, Iruzubieta et al. (2024, PMID:38366623) reported 5 patients from 2 unrelated families with homozygous *SNUPN* p.Ile309Ser variants who presented with a classic childhood-onset limb-girdle muscular dystrophy with myofibrillar features, devoid of mandatory syndromic neurodevelopmental impairment or structural brain malformations [F1, F12, F15]. Muelas et al. (2026, 10.1002/acn3.70211) demonstrated that *SNUPN* disease represents a broad spectrum encompassing both pure myopathies and syndromic presentations [F1, F12].

### 2. Primary Myocardial Injury vs. Skeletal Muscle-Derived Biomarker Elevation
* **Contested issue:** Whether *SNUPN* deficiency causes primary cardiac damage / cardiomyopathy as an initial presenting manifestation (F9, F13, F17).
* **Evidence & Conflict:** Dai et al. (2026, 10.21203/rs.3.rs-8429398/v1) reported a 19-month-old infant presenting with isolated 'myocardial damage' triggered by influenza A preceding skeletal motor weakness, concluding that myocardial injury can be the herald manifestation of LGMDR29 [F8, F9, F17]. Conversely, this infant's echocardiogram showed preserved cardiac anatomy and systolic function [F9, F17]. In the established cohort of 18 pediatric patients (Nashabat et al., 2024), primary myocardial necrosis or cardiomyopathy was not observed [F9, F13, F17]. In muscular dystrophies, regenerating skeletal myofibers express fetal isoforms and release high levels of CK-MB and cross-reactive cardiac troponin (AM Chaulin, 2022, 10.14740/jocmr4664) [F9, F17]. Most clinical evidence indicates that cardiopulmonary compromise in LGMDR29 results from restrictive respiratory pump failure rather than primary cardiomyocyte cell death (F14).

### 3. Age-Restricted Weakness Topography
* **Contested issue:** Whether childhood-onset cases have strictly proximal weakness while adult cases have proximo-distal and axial weakness (F8, F10, F16).
* **Evidence & Conflict:** Initial clinical models proposed a dichotomy where early-onset disease was purely proximal and adult-onset disease was proximo-distal with axial involvement (F8, F16). However, detailed imaging by Iruzubieta et al. (2024) revealed that pediatric patients also exhibit prominent subclinical or clinical axial (paravertebral) and distal (peroneal and medial gastrocnemius) muscle involvement, confirming that the anatomical vulnerability pattern is shared across age groups [F6, F10, F16].

## Cross-Cutting Mechanistic Synthesis and Open Diagnostic Questions

### Splicing Vulnerability and Pleiotropic Phenotypes
The clinical dichotomy between isolated LGMDR29, adult-onset myopathy, and spinocerebellar ataxia reflects tissue-specific differences in baseline alternative splicing demand rather than discrete mutational hot spots (F1, F5). Purkinje cells in the cerebellum and high-metabolic skeletal myofibers possess exceptionally high rates of alternative splicing, rendering them preferentially vulnerable when snurportin-1-mediated U snRNP transport drops below critical thresholds (F5).

### Radiological Dissociation Preceding Functional Loss
A hallmark cross-cutting pattern in *SNUPN* deficiency is that structural degenerative changes precede overt clinical dysfunction (F3, F4, F6). Patients may demonstrate marked cerebellar atrophy on MRI while remaining cognitively intact and non-ataxic (F3, F4), or display advanced fatty infiltration of paravertebral, sartorius, and gracilis muscles on imaging before losing functional ambulation (F6, F7, F10).

### Practical Recommendations for Clinical Phenotyping
When evaluating a patient with suspected LGMDR29:
1. **Test Deep Finger Flexors:** Verify normal finger flexor strength to differentiate adult presentations from sIBM (F7).
2. **Obtain Lower-Limb and Axial Muscle MRI:** Look specifically for co-involvement of the sartorius, gracilis, vasti, and paravertebral muscles (F6, F7, F10).
3. **Perform Brain MRI:** Screen for subclinical or overt cerebellar atrophy even in the absence of clinical ataxia (F1, F3, F4).
4. **Evaluate Cardiopulmonary Status:** Prioritize spirometry to detect early restrictive respiratory pump failure, and interpret elevated serum troponins cautiously in the setting of active skeletal muscle necrosis (F9, F14, F17).
