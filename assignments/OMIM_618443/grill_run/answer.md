# Clinical Phenotypic Recognition and Differential Diagnosis of MAPK8IP3-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorder

Neurodevelopmental disorder with or without variable brain abnormalities (NEDBA; OMIM #618443) is caused by pathogenic heterozygous (and rarely compound heterozygous) variants in *MAPK8IP3*, which encodes JIP3 (c-Jun-amino-terminal kinase-interacting protein 3), an axonal cargo adapter essential for kinesin-1 and dynein-dynactin-mediated retrograde endolysosomal transport (F3, F4). Clinical diagnosis from phenotype alone requires distinguishing obligate syndromic neurodevelopmental features from secondary, low-penetrance manifestations and non-genetic motor mimics.

---

Across published molecular cohorts, neurodevelopmental delays and motor abnormalities form the foundational presentation, while craniofacial, structural neuroradiological, and ophthalmological findings occur at variable frequencies (F1, F5, F6, F7, F9, F10, F15).

## Discriminative Diagnosis: Distinguishing MAPK8IP3-Related Disorder from Mimics

When evaluating a patient presenting with early motor delays, spasticity, or tone instability, several key discriminators separate *MAPK8IP3*-related disease from common clinical mimickers (F2, F10, F11).

```
Patient Presenting with Spastic Motor Impairment & Tone Abnormalities
  |
  +---> Pure Lower Motor Neuron Weakness (Normal Cognition, Normal MRI)?
  |       --> Suggests classic 5q SMA or pure motor neuropathy
  |
  +---> Isolated Progressive Lower-Limb Spasticity (Normal Brain Imaging/Cognition)?
  |       --> Suggests Pure Hereditary Spastic Paraplegia (HSP)
  |
  +---> Static Motor Deficit (History of Perinatal Hypoxia/Ischemia)?
  |       --> Suggests Classical Cerebral Palsy (CP)
  |
  +---> Complex Triad: Obligate Cognitive/Language Delay + Mixed Tone + Thin Corpus Callosum
          --> High clinical confidence for MAPK8IP3-Related Disorder (OMIM #618443)
```

### 1. Complex Hereditary Spastic Paraplegias (e.g., SPG11, SPG15)
- **Overlapping Features**: Progressive lower limb spasticity, hyperreflexia, and corpus callosum thinning (F2, F11).
- **Discriminating Findings**: While complex HSPs exhibit neurodegeneration in adolescence or early adulthood, *MAPK8IP3* variants cause prominent congenital-to-early infantile developmental delay and intellectual impairment that precede or coincide with motor manifestations (F1, F2). Furthermore, *MAPK8IP3* individuals frequently demonstrate cerebellar hypoplasia or atrophy (8/13 in Platzer et al., 2019) and perisylvian polymicrogyria, which are atypical for classical HSP (F10, F11).
- **Diagnostic Confidence**: High for differentiating pure HSP; moderate-to-high for complex HSP when incorporating early infantile neurodevelopmental onset and structural posterior fossa anomalies (F2, F11).

### 2. Static Cerebral Palsy (Spastic Diplegia)
- **Overlapping Features**: Early hypotonia transitioning to spastic diplegia, motor milestone delays, and variable speech delays (F2, F15).
- **Discriminating Findings**: *MAPK8IP3* presentations are non-static; they frequently feature evolutionary neuroradiological findings including progressive cerebral/cerebellar volume loss or cortical malformations (e.g., polymicrogyria, hypoplasia of the corpus callosum) in the absence of hypoxic-ischemic perinatal events (F10, F11).
- **Diagnostic Confidence**: Moderate-to-high upon serial neuroimaging review and genetic testing (F10, F11).

### 3. Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA) and Lower Motor Neuron Disorders
- **Overlapping Features**: Profound infantile muscular hypotonia, diminished motor reflexes (F3, F15, F16).
- **Discriminating Findings**: Heterozygous *MAPK8IP3* mutations cause central corticospinal and cognitive involvement rather than isolated lower motor neuron death (F2). However, rare compound heterozygous variants have been described in severe neonatal hypotonia mimicking lethal SMA with >60% JIP3 protein reduction in muscle tissue (J Kárteszi et al., 2023, DOI: 10.1002/ajmg.a.63340) (F3, F16).
- **Diagnostic Confidence**: High for classic heterozygous de novo cases; requires molecular sequencing to resolve rare compound heterozygous presentations mimicking lethal SMA (F2, F16).

## Exclusionary and Atypical Findings Arguing Against Diagnosis

Certain clinical signs have been systematically assessed and found absent or incompatible with primary heterozygous *MAPK8IP3*-related disease, serving as negative criteria:

1. **Pure Hereditary Spastic Paraplegia / Isolated Spasticity Without Cognitive Involvement**: Individuals carrying heterozygous truncating or missense variants do not present with pure motor spasticity; intellectual disability or cognitive impairment is universally present across primary series (F1, F2). An isolated spastic paraplegia phenotype without cognitive or adaptive deficits argues strongly against this diagnosis (F2).
2. **Isolated Lower Motor Neuron Degeneration with Normal Supratentorial Brain Morphometry**: Heterozygous *MAPK8IP3* variants do not produce isolated anterior horn cell loss or spinal motor neuron degeneration in the absence of neurodevelopmental delay (F2, F16).
3. **Universal Early Severe Epileptic Encephalopathy**: Seizures occur in fewer than 25% of patients overall (e.g., 6/32 in Sudnawa et al., 2026; absent in 11/13 in Platzer et al., 2019) (F7). Intractable early-infantile epileptic encephalopathy without significant motor or corpus callosum anomalies argues against *MAPK8IP3* as the monogenic driver (F7, F10).
4. **Isolated Primary Ocular Retinal/Optic Nerve Degeneration**: Ophthalmological involvement is restricted to secondary central cortical visual impairment (CVI) in approximately 15% to 20% of cases (2/13 in Platzer et al., 2019) (F6). Primary photoreceptor dystrophy, optic atrophy, or anterior segment dysgenesis argues against the condition (F6).

## Phenotype Stratification by Variant Type, Position, Age, and Sex

```
MAPK8IP3 Genotypic & Structural Architecture
  [ N-term ] --- [ Leucine Zipper / JNK-Binding ] --- [ WD40 Domain ] --- [ C-term ]
                         |
                         +--> Recurrent Hotspot Missenses (e.g., p.Arg578Cys)
                         |      * Dominant-negative multimer poisoning
                         |      * Microcephaly (20-30%)
                         |      * Severe CC hypoplasia / cerebellar atrophy
                         |
                         +--> Truncating / Splice LOF Variants & WD40 Missenses
                                * Haploinsufficiency / loss-of-function
                                * Broad cognitive spectrum (mild learning to severe ID)
                                * Preservation of gross brain morphogenesis in some carriers
```

### 1. Variant Class: Missense vs. Truncating / Loss-of-Function (LOF)
- **Recurrent Missense Variants**: De novo recurrent missense mutations (such as p.Arg578Cys) act via dominant-negative interference with axonal organelle transport, elevating axonal lysosome density and disrupting retrograde clearance (F3, F4). These variants strongly correlate with severe structural neuroimaging abnormalities (corpus callosum hypoplasia, cerebellar atrophy) and microcephaly (F4, F5, F8).
- **Truncating and Splice-Site Variants**: Heterozygous predicted LOF variants (nonsense, frameshift, splice-site) produce partial loss of function or haploinsufficiency (F4, F8). While they still cause neurodevelopmental disorders encompassing cognitive impairment or intellectual disability (F2), they can preserve gross cortical morphogenesis and present with milder cognitive phenotypes (e.g., specific learning difficulties, mean DAS-II General Conceptual Ability of 62.5 across mixed cohorts) (F1, F4, F9).

### 2. Domain Position
- Recurrent missense alterations cluster tightly within conserved functional domains, specifically the **leucine zipper** and **JNK-binding domains** (F4, F12). Variants within these segments induce pronounced dominant-negative retrograde axonal transport defects (F3, F4).
- In contrast, missense variants within the **WD40 domain** have been shown in animal and cellular models to phenocopy loss-of-function alleles rather than classical dominant-negative hotspot mutations (CM Crowder et al., 2026, DOI: 10.64898/2026.07.20.739682.abstract) (F13).

### 3. Age-Dependent Phenotypic Evolution
- **Infancy**: Presenting features are dominated by generalized muscular hypotonia (documented in 9/13 patients by Platzer et al., 2019) and poor feeding, frequently accompanied by delayed attainment of early motor milestones (F1, F15).
- **Early Childhood to Adolescence**: Motor phenotypes often evolve; hypotonia may persist or evolve into spastic diplegia/quadriparesis with hyperreflexia (F2, F15). Secondary skeletal deformities emerge as consequences of chronic tone disturbance, including scoliosis (3/13 in Platzer et al., 2019) (F6). Cognitive impairments and severe expressive language deficits become fully manifest during this stage (F1, F9).

### 4. Sex Distribution
- No statistically significant sex-specific phenotypic divergence or sex bias has been established across reported cohorts; males and females are affected with equivalent clinical expressivity (F1, F4, F8).

## Controverted and Contested Findings in the Literature

Several phenotypic assertions and disease mechanisms have been contested or modified by newer clinical series and functional analyses:

### 1. Cognitive Impairment: Uniformly Severe vs. Broad Cognitive Spectrum
- *Initial Assertion*: Foundational reports suggested that *MAPK8IP3* pathogenic variants universally produce moderate-to-profound intellectual disability with absent or minimal expressive speech (F9).
- *Contesting Evidence*: Newer comprehensive evaluations establish a broader cognitive spectrum. Sudnawa et al. (2026, DOI: 10.1111/cge.70032) demonstrated a wide distribution of cognitive abilities (mean DAS-II General Conceptual Ability score 62.5 ± 26.5) (F1, F9). Furthermore, Yi et al. (2026, PMC13101558) identified an individual with an inherited canonical splice-site variant manifesting only mild specific learning difficulties, refuting the claim of invariant profound disability (F9, F14).

### 2. Structural Brain Malformation Penetrance (>85% vs. Variable / Incomplete)
- *Initial Assertion*: Early literature indicated that corpus callosum hypoplasia/dysgenesis and cerebellar hypoplasia are near-obligate (>85%) imaging hallmarks (F10).
- *Contesting Evidence*: The official OMIM designation recognizes NEDBA as presenting "with or without variable brain abnormalities" (OMIM #618443) (F10). In Platzer et al. (2019), structural abnormalities were consistently observed in recurrent missense mutations but were less consistent across truncating variants (F8, F10). Sudnawa et al. (2026) observed corpus callosum thinning in 62.1% (18/29) of evaluated individuals, demonstrating that gross structural dysgenesis is incomplete (F8, F17).

### 3. Absolute Dichotomy Between Missense Severity and LOF Mildness
- *Initial Assertion*: Recurrent de novo missense variants are severe and syndromic, whereas heterozygous loss-of-function variants are consistently milder or lack major neurodevelopmental pathology (F8, F13).
- *Contesting Evidence*: Yi et al. (2026, PMC13101558) documented an inherited truncating splice-site variant (p.Gly877Valfs*36) that resulted in severe global developmental delay, severe intellectual disability, and autism in an affected child, demonstrating that loss-of-function alleles can produce profound neurodevelopmental disease (F13, F14). Conversely, Crowder et al. (2026) demonstrated that specific WD40 missense variants functionally behave as loss-of-function alleles in vivo, indicating that clinical severity is governed by domain-specific biomechanics rather than a strict missense-versus-truncating categorization (F13).

### 4. Infantile Hypotonia Evolving Invariably into Spastic Diplegia
- *Initial Assertion*: Tone abnormalities follow a stereotypic biphasic sequence in >70–80% of cases, transitioning from infantile hypotonia to severe progressive spasticity (F15).
- *Contesting Evidence*: Detailed breakdown of the Platzer et al. (2019) cohort revealed that while tone abnormalities were noted in 84.6% (11/13), hypotonia alone was present in 9/13 and spasticity in 4/13; the specific evolution from hypotonia to spasticity was explicitly documented in only 2 of 13 patients (15.4%) (F15). Similarly, in Iwasawa et al. (2019), all 5 individuals had spastic diplegia, but early hypotonia was recorded in only 2 of 5 (F15).

### 5. Inheritance and Penetrance Modes
- *Initial Assertion*: *MAPK8IP3*-related disease is strictly an early-onset, fully penetrant autosomal dominant disorder caused almost exclusively by de novo mutations (F14, F16).
- *Contesting Evidence*: Familial pedigrees (Yi et al., 2026; Krenn et al., 2025, DOI: 10.1016/j.seizure.2025.08.027) describe variable expressivity, incomplete penetrance, and potential oligogenic modifiers in kindreds transmitting heterozygous truncating/splice-site alleles (F7, F14). Additionally, Kárteszi et al. (2023, DOI: 10.1002/ajmg.a.63340) revealed that biallelic/compound heterozygous mutations can manifest as an autosomal recessive lethal spinal muscular atrophy mimic (F3, F12, F16).
