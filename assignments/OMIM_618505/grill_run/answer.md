# Clinical Identification and Phenotypic Landscape of Stolerman Neurodevelopmental Syndrome (KDM6B / OMIM #618505)

## 1. Clinical Phenotypic Spectrum and Observed Frequencies

Stolerman neurodevelopmental syndrome (OMIM #618505, historically designated as neurodevelopmental disorder with coarse facies and mild distal skeletal abnormalities, NEDCFSA/NEDSST) is caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants in the lysine-specific demethylase gene *KDM6B* (also known as *JMJD3*) F1. Evaluation of cohorts reveals a predominantly non-specific neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by high clinical heterogeneity and variable expressivity, lacking an invariant pathognomonic physical sign F1, F10.

To identify affected individuals within cohorts presenting with overlapping neurodevelopmental delay, phenotypic findings must be evaluated across the published literature cohorts with specific numerators and denominators F1, F3, F5, F8, F10:

## 2. Diagnostic Discrimination and Commonly Overlapping Disorders

### Clinical Discriminative Capacity
From phenotype alone, identifying an individual with Stolerman neurodevelopmental syndrome among a cohort of patients with intellectual disability is challenging. Dysmorphic craniofacial features lack pathognomonic diagnostic value F1. The initial 100% penetrance of recognizable dysmorphic facial features in the 12-patient discovery cohort attenuated significantly as cohorts scaled, demonstrating that facial gestalt cannot serve as a reliable standalone inclusion criterion F1.

### Commonly Confused Disorders and Distinction Confidence

1. **Kabuki Syndrome 1 & 2 (OMIM #147920, #300867; *KMT2D*, *KDM6A*):**
   - *Overlapping Phenotype:* Both conditions involve histone H3K27 methylation/demethylation machinery and exhibit delayed development, hypotonia, and craniofacial anomalies F13, F14.
   - *Confidence of Clinical Distinction:* **Low to Moderate**. While Kabuki syndrome classically features elongated palpebral fissures with eversion of the lateral third of the lower eyelid and persistent fetal fingertip pads, literature reviewing chromatinopathies documents that lateral lower eyelid eversion and fingertip pads can occasionally overlap across histone methylation disorders, precluding absolute exclusion by physical exam alone F13. Definitive distinction requires molecular sequencing F10, F13.
2. **Coffin-Siris Syndrome (OMIM #135900; *ARID1B*, *SMARCA4*, etc.):**
   - *Overlapping Phenotype:* Coarse facial features, intellectual disability, and distal extremity anomalies F9, F11.
   - *Confidence of Clinical Distinction:* **Moderate**. Coffin-Siris syndrome typically presents with severe aplasia or hypoplasia of the fifth digit distal phalanx or nail (HP:0001792), whereas distal anomalies in *KDM6B* are infrequent (9%) and non-disruptive F2, F9, F10.
3. **Bohring-Opitz Syndrome (OMIM #605039; *ASXL1*):**
   - *Overlapping Phenotype:* Severe developmental delay, feeding issues, and microcephaly F2, F11.
   - *Confidence of Clinical Distinction:* **High**. Bohring-Opitz syndrome exhibits profound intrauterine dwarfism and the classic flexion BOS posture, which are absent in Stolerman syndrome F2, F11.
4. **Cerebral Folate Deficiency (CFD) / Epigenetic Syndromes (*FOLR1*, *CIC*, *DNMT3A*, *KMT2B*, *KMT2C*, *YY1*):**
   - *Overlapping Phenotype:* Motor delay, speech impairment, and secondary FOLR1 pathway downregulation F8.
   - *Confidence of Clinical Distinction:* **Very Low**. Folate receptor downregulation and serum autoantibodies are non-specific and overlap broadly across multiple chromatin and neurodevelopmental conditions F8.

## 3. Negative Discriminators: Features Arguing Against KDM6B Etiology

Certain structural and ophthalmic malformations have been evaluated and found consistently absent or exceptionally rare across assessed individuals with *KDM6B* variants. Their presence strongly argues against a diagnosis of Stolerman syndrome F2:

1. **Severe Congenital Limb Reduction Defects (HP:0009804):** Affected patients do not exhibit phocomelia, ectrodactyly, or intercalary limb reductions F2.
2. **Fifth Digit Distal Phalanx or Nail Aplasia/Hypoplasia (HP:0001792):** Severe hypoplasia or anonychia of the fifth digit characteristic of Coffin-Siris syndrome is absent in Stolerman syndrome cohorts F2, F9.
3. **Corneal Clouding / Opacity (HP:0007957):** Absence of anterior segment dysgenesis or structural corneal clouding differentiates Stolerman syndrome from storage diseases and severe anterior chamber cleavage disorders F2.
4. **Profound Prenatal Microcephaly with Intrauterine Dwarfism:** Stolerman syndrome is not characterized by severe primordial microcephalic dwarfism F2.
5. **Isolated Palpebral Eversion without Global Delay:** Although lower eyelid eversion has been reported in overlapping histone disorders, prominent isolated lateral eversion in the absence of global developmental delay is inconsistent with Stolerman syndrome F2, F13.

## 4. Phenotypic Correlations by Variant Type, Position, Age, and Sex

### Variant Type and Catalytic Domain Position
* **Absence of Severity Differences:** Empirical systematic cohort evaluation by Rots et al. (2023) refutes the hypothesis that truncating (nonsense, frameshift) variants produce a distinct or milder phenotype than missense variants clustered within or adjacent to the catalytic Jumonji C (JmjC) / zinc-finger domains F4. Both classes produce broadly overlapping neurodevelopmental delay, seizure frequencies, and structural features without statistically distinct clinical severity trajectories F4.
* **Hypomorphic and Non-Catalytic Missense Alleles:** Missense variants outside the catalytic domain have been reported in familial multi-generational linkage studies of non-syndromic autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) (Zhou et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2022) F6. However, whether these represent low-penetrance hypomorphic alleles or polygenic background risk remains unresolved, as de novo missense variants in clinical cohorts regularly present with full neurodevelopmental manifestations F6.

### Patient Age and Sex
* **Age Trajectory:** Infancy is frequently characterized by feeding difficulties or gastroesophageal reflux (51%) and hypotonia, which transition into motor/speech delay, behavioral anomalies (e.g., ASD, ADHD), and abnormal gait/ataxia during childhood and adolescence F3, F4, F10. Cerebellar heterotopia has been confirmed by neuroimaging in an 11-year-old child F1, F4.
* **Sex Distribution:** *KDM6B* is located on chromosome 17p13.1 (autosomal dominant). Clinical manifestations show no documented sex predilection or sex-specific phenotypic divergence F1, F10.

## 5. Contradictions and Nosological Discrepancies in the Literature

| Disputed Phenomenon | Primary Position / Citation | Contradicting Position / Citation | Resolution / State of the Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Diagnostic Facial and Distal Skeletal Manifestations** | OMIM entry #618505 defines the syndrome as having prominent "coarse facies and mild distal skeletal abnormalities" based on initial discovery cohorts (Stolerman et al., 2019) F9, F10. | Expanded cohort evaluation (Rots et al., 2023) demonstrates coarse facies in only **4%** (~3/73) and distal skeletal anomalies in only **9%** (~7/73) F9, F10. | **Empirical refutation of initial nosology.** Diagnostic naming established in discovery probands enshrined rare, atypical findings as cardinal traits, which dissolved upon cohort scaling F1, F9, F10. |
| **Palpebral Fissure Morphology** | Reviews of chromatinopathies (Rezazadeh et al., 2025) list elongated palpebral fissures (HP:0000587) as a feature of *KDM6B* alterations F7. | Systematic clinical evaluations (Rots et al., 2023; Top et al., 2023) identify downslanted palpebral fissures, epicanthus, or ptosis, rather than elongated fissures F7. | **Contested clinical descriptor.** Palpebral elongation is characteristic of Kabuki syndrome rather than *KDM6B*, where fissures are typically non-specifically sloped F7, F14. |
| **Exclusivity of Kabuki Syndrome Dysmorphology** | Some clinical frameworks posit that lateral lower eyelid eversion (HP:0000656) and persistent fetal fingertip pads (HP:0001212) definitively exclude *KDM6B* in favor of *KDM6A*/*KMT2D* F11, F13. | Comprehensive reviews of histone methylation disorders (Al Ojaimi et al., 2022, 2025) document overlapping lateral lower eyelid eversion and fetal fingertip pads in *KDM6B* patients F13. | **Contested exclusionary criterion.** Due to shared epigenetic pathway disruption, these minor stigmata cannot be treated as mutually exclusive discriminators F13. |
| **Cerebral Folate Deficiency Biomarker Specificity** | Han et al. (2022) identified *KDM6B* variants in 10.4% (5/48) of a CFD cohort and proposed FOLR1 downregulation / autoantibodies as a disease biomarker F8. | Luo et al. (2021) and Qin et al. (2023) show FOLR1 downregulation across disparate chromatin genes (*CIC*, *DNMT3A*, *KMT2B*, *KMT2C*, *YY1*), as well as circulating autoantibodies in non-genetic controls F8. | **Contested functional biomarker.** Folate pathway disruption is a convergent downstream secondary epiphenomenon rather than a *KDM6B*-specific diagnostic marker F8. |

## 6. Evaluation and Synthesis of Deprioritised Hypotheses

Several initial hypotheses regarding *KDM6B* pathophysiology were deprioritised based on literature analysis:

1. **Extraneurological Phenotypic Dilution (Refuted):** The hypothesis that feeding difficulties diluted from ~51% in discovery cohorts down to negligible rates in the expanded series of Rots et al. (2023) was refuted; Rots et al. explicitly documented feeding difficulties or gastroesophageal reflux in 51% of their expanded international cohort F3.
2. **Catalytic Domain Severity Divergence (Refuted):** The hypothesis that catalytic JmjC missense variants cause significantly more severe intellectual disability and seizures than truncating haploinsufficiency alleles was refuted by empirical systematic cohort comparisons showing uniform clinical distributions F4.
3. **Cohort Attribution of 2019 Discovery Series (Refuted):** Claims that Stolerman et al. (2019) evaluated 63 patients were refuted; Stolerman et al. (2019) was the original 12-patient series, whereas larger sample sizes (~73 probands) correspond to subsequent multicenter collaborations (Rots et al. / Top et al., 2023) F10.
4. **Absolute 0% Prevalence of Fetal Fingertip Pads and Eyelid Eversion (Refuted):** Hypotheses asserting verified 0/N frequencies for lateral lower eyelid eversion or fingertip pads in expanded supplemental tables were refuted due to lack of primary evidentiary support in the literature, alongside direct documentation of clinical overlap by Al Ojaimi et al. (2022, 2025) F13, F15, F16.
5. **100% Diagnostic Specificity Against Coffin-Siris Syndrome (Contested):** Assertions of 100% clinical specificity against Coffin-Siris syndrome based on absent fifth digit hypoplasia alone were contested due to variable expressivity in both conditions F9.
