# Clinical Identification and Phenotypic Landscape of *KLHL20*-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorder (OMIM #621390)

## Executive Summary & Feasibility of Phenotype-Only Identification

Neurodevelopmental disorder with early-onset seizures, facial dysmorphism, and behavioral abnormalities (NEDSFBA, OMIM #621390) is an autosomal dominant condition caused by heterozygous de novo missense variants in the *KLHL20* gene, located on chromosome 1q25 F1, F6. *KLHL20* encodes Kelch-like protein 20, a substrate adaptor for Cullin3 (CUL3)-RING E3 ubiquitin ligases that directs ubiquitination and plays critical roles in neurite outgrowth and synaptic homeostasis F5.

From clinical phenotypes alone, **a clinician cannot definitively identify an affected individual out of a group of patients with overlapping developmental, epileptic, or behavioral features without molecular testing** (F3; F4; F8). Although early-onset seizures, intellectual disability, and behavioral disturbances (autism spectrum disorder, hyperactivity) occur with high frequency, the craniofacial dysmorphic features are subtle and variable, lacking an identifiable syndromic "gestalt" (F3; F4). Furthermore, presentations can meet diagnostic criteria for broad non-syndromic developmental and epileptic encephalopathies (DEEs) F12. Clinical discrimination relies on recognizing a biphasic natural history (early infantile seizures accompanied or followed by emergent neurobehavioral traits) coupled with the notable absence of structural malformations or neurodegenerative decline (F2; F5).

Global developmental delay/intellectual disability is fully penetrant (14/14, 100%), ranging from mild to severe impairment (F1; F10). In contrast, seizures demonstrate incomplete penetrance: present in fewer than 80% (<11/14; documented in 9/14) of patients, with variable semiologies including isolated febrile seizures (2/14), febrile seizures progressing to epilepsy (2/14), and infantile spasms (F1; F6).

## Differential Diagnosis & Discriminating Clinical Features

### Potential Confusers
Clinicians typically consider *KLHL20*-related disorder within two differential categories (F8; F12):
1. **Classic Developmental and Epileptic Encephalopathies (DEEs)**: Conditions caused by pathogenic variants in *SCN1A*, *CDKL5*, *STXBP1*, or *KCNQ2*.
2. **Syndromic Autism and Intellectual Disability Spectrum**: Heterogeneous neurodevelopmental syndromes characterized by intellectual disability, behavioral outbursts, and subtle dysmorphisms.

### Discriminating Findings
- **Combination of Behavioral Pathology and Epilepsy**: While DEEs caused by *SCN1A* or *STXBP1* present predominantly with refractory early seizures, profound motor deficits, and non-dysmorphic features, *KLHL20*-related patients frequently demonstrate prominent behavioral alterations (hyperactivity, stereotypies, aggressive bursts, autistic traits) alongside seizures (F8; F12).
- **Subtle Facial Dysmorphisms**: The presence of subtle craniofacial dysmorphisms can distinguish *KLHL20* disorder from purely non-dysmorphic channelopathy-related DEEs F8. However, because no specific facial feature occurs in >70% of individuals and facial features are described as subtle, automated computer-aided 3D facial phenotyping or visual gestalt evaluation cannot reliably classify *KLHL20* patients apart from other syndromic autism cohorts (F3; F4; F11).
- **Confidence in Phenotypic Distinction**: **Low to Moderate**. Phenotypic discrimination alone remains insufficient to pinpoint *KLHL20* due to the high clinical overlap with other neurodevelopmental syndromes; definitive diagnosis necessitates molecular genomic testing (exome/genome sequencing) (F4; F8; F12).

## Exclusionary Phenotypes Arguing Against *KLHL20* Disorder

Because positive findings can be non-specific, exclusionary or negative findings provide essential diagnostic value (F2). The presence of any of the following features in an affected individual argues strongly against isolated *KLHL20*-related NEDSFBA (Sleyp et al., 2022, PMID:36214804; Kuroda et al., 2024, DOI:10.1002/ajmg.a.63600) (F2):

1. **Prominent Neurovisceral Storage Signs**: Coarse facial features, organomegaly (hepatosplenomegaly), or cellular vacuolation (F2).
2. **Obligate Congenital Structural Heart Defects**: Complex congenital cardiac malformations (e.g., tetralogy of Fallot, transposition of great arteries, hypoplastic left heart syndrome) are not core or obligate features of *KLHL20* disorder (F2).
3. **Rapid, Progressive Neurodegeneration and Early Lethality**: *KLHL20* leads to static or stable intellectual impairment without rapid motor regression, unremitting neurological breakdown, or early mortality (F2; F5).
4. **Focal Skeletal Limb-Reduction Deficiencies**: Phocomelia, ectrodactyly, or terminal limb transverse deficiencies (F2).
5. **Major Structural Brain Malformations or Microcephaly as Defining Features**: Cortical dysplasias, holoprosencephaly, or lissencephaly are absent as defining obligate characteristics in pure *KLHL20* cohorts (F2; F15).

If a patient exhibits these manifestations, clinicians should suspect secondary pathogenic variants, chromosomal anomalies, or alternative metabolic/syndromic diagnoses (F2).

## Phenotypic Modifiers: Variant Type, Position, Age, and Sex

### 1. Variant Type
Human disease associated with *KLHL20* is exclusively autosomal dominant and caused by heterozygous de novo missense variants (F1; F14; F15). There is no empirical evidence of pathogenic truncation, nonsense, frameshift, or biallelic loss-of-function variants in human NEDSFBA cohorts (F14; F15).

### 2. Variant Position
All reported pathogenic variants cluster tightly within the **Kelch-type beta-propeller domain** of KLHL20, which coordinates substrate binding (F6; F7; F16):
- **Recurrent Hotspot (p.Gly357Arg)**: Found in 11 of 14 discovery patients (c.1069G>A), this variant presents with high penetrance of behavioral abnormalities (ASD, hyperactivity) and intellectual disability, but incomplete penetrance of afebrile refractory epilepsy (F1; F6).
- **Non-Recurrent Kelch Missense Variants**: Found in 3 of 14 discovery patients. Statistical comparisons indicate that seizure penetrance, semiology (infantile spasms to febrile seizures), and age of onset do not significantly differ between p.Gly357Arg carriers and unique Kelch-domain missense carriers F7. However, 2 of 3 unique missense carriers met full criteria for severe DEE, indicating variable expressivity across the Kelch domain (F7; F12).
- **Variants Outside the Kelch Domain**: No pathogenic variants located in the BTB or BACK domains have been established in human cohorts (F14; F16).

### 3. Patient Age and Natural History
*KLHL20*-related disorder displays a **biphasic natural history** (F5):
- **Infancy and Early Childhood**: Characterized by paroxysmal neurological crises, hypotonia, early-onset seizures (often in the first year of life), febrile convulsions, and infantile spasms (F1; F5; F10).
- **Later Childhood and Adulthood**: Seizures frequently stabilize or become secondary, while behavioral disturbances (hyperactivity, impulsivity, autism spectrum traits, aggressive outbursts) and persistent intellectual disability become the dominant clinical concern (F5; F6). The disorder does not exhibit neurodegenerative motor regression (F2; F5).

### 4. Patient Sex
No sex-linked difference in penetrance, expressivity, or semiology has been documented; both males and females are affected consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance (F1; F7).

## Literature Contradictions and Deprioritised Hypotheses

Several hypotheses in the literature have been contested, refuted, or clarified by cohort evidence:

### 1. Distinctive Facial Gestalt vs. Subtle Craniofacial Findings (Contested)
- *Contested Claim*: Facial dysmorphism represents an obligate, robust gestalt that allows computational dense 3D facial imaging or bedside visual examination to discriminate *KLHL20* disorder from non-syndromic DEEs (F11).
- *Literature Evidence*: Sleyp et al. (2022; PMID:36214804) explicitly categorize facial dysmorphisms as "subtle" and heterogeneous, with no single feature exceeding 70% penetrance across the 14 patients (F3; F11). Facial phenotyping tools fail to reliably separate *KLHL20* from heterogeneous syndromic ASD cohorts without molecular testing (F4; F11).

### 2. Severity of Intellectual Disability and Feature Penetrance (Contested)
- *Contested Claim*: Severe-to-profound intellectual disability, broad nasal bridge, and thin upper lip vermilion occur at >75% penetrance in all affected individuals (F10).
- *Literature Evidence*: While global developmental delay/intellectual disability is 100% penetrant (14/14), documented severity ranges across mild, moderate, and severe spectra (Sleyp et al., 2022; ClinGen SGC-107240.2) (F1; F10). Specific dysmorphisms like broad nasal bridge or thin vermilion do not reach >75% consistency (F3; F10).

### 3. Mutational Mechanism: Hotspot Dinucleotide vs. Ascertainment Severity (Contested)
- *Contested Claim*: The predominance of c.1069G>A (p.Gly357Arg) reflects clinical ascertainment bias due to a more severe phenotype compared to other Kelch variants F9.
- *Literature Evidence*: Seizure semiology, penetrance, and intellectual impairment do not demonstrate significant divergence between p.Gly357Arg and non-recurrent Kelch variants F7. The recurrence is primarily explained by a hypermutable CpG dinucleotide transition, although intrinsic germline mutation rate models calibrated directly against ascertainment rates remain unformalized F9.

### 4. Inheritance and Domain Diversity (Refuted)
- *Refuted Claims*: Hypotheses postulating autosomal recessive biallelic loss-of-function variants, non-Kelch (BTB/BACK domain) truncation variants, or microcephaly/brain malformations as regular disease mechanisms (F14; F15; F16).
- *Literature Reality*: ClinGen curation and clinical series confirm *KLHL20* disorder is strictly autosomal dominant, driven by de novo missense variants confined exclusively to the Kelch beta-propeller domain (14/14 patients) (F14; F15; F16).

## Synthesis and Cross-Cutting Diagnostic Patterns

```
                          CLINICAL PRESENTATION
                                    │
        ┌───────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┐
        ▼                                                       ▼
[POSITIVE CORE CRITERIA]                               [EXCLUSIONARY SIGNS]
 • GDD/ID (14/14, 100%)                                 • Coarse facial / storage signs
 • Early seizures / spasms (<11/14, ~64%)               • Complex cardiac malformations
 • Biphasic evolution to ASD / hyperactivity            • Progressive neurodegenerative decline
 • Subtle, non-gestalt facial dysmorphisms              • Severe limb-reduction defects
        │                                                       │
        └───────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┘
                                    ▼
                     [GENETIC TESTING CONFIRMATION]
            Heterozygous de novo Kelch-domain missense
            (e.g., recurrent c.1069G>A [p.Gly357Arg])
```

1. **Nomenclature-Phenotype Mismatch**: The official OMIM designation ("Neurodevelopmental disorder with early-onset seizures, facial dysmorphism, and behavioral abnormalities") implies that facial dysmorphism is a prominent, pathognomonic hallmark. In reality, facial signs are non-penetrant, subtle, and lack a recognizable gestalt (F3; F4). Clinicians should not rule out the condition if a patient lacks striking facial dysmorphism.
2. **Biphasic Natural History**: The disease follows an age-dependent course: acute paroxysmal seizures dominate early infancy (including febrile seizures, epilepsy, and infantile spasms), transitioning into stable intellectual disability and prominent neurobehavioral morbidity (ASD, hyperactivity, impulsivity) in older childhood and adolescence (F5; F6).
3. **Discriminative Reliance on Negative Features**: Because the positive triad (developmental delay, seizures, behavioral issues) is shared by dozens of epileptic encephalopathies, diagnostic discrimination relies heavily on exclusionary findings (absence of neurovisceral storage, structural cardiac defects, progressive motor decline, or brain malformations) (F2; F8).
4. **Domain-Restricted Architecture**: Pathogenic variation clusters strictly within the Kelch-type beta-propeller domain without sub-domain phenotypic divergence, highlighting the critical role of substrate binding perturbation in *KLHL20* pathogenesis (F7; F16).
