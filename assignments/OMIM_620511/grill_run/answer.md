# Clinical Identification and Phenotypic Delineation of Fliedner-Zweier Syndrome (SCAF4-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorder)

Fliedner-Zweier syndrome (FZS; OMIM #620511) is an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental condition caused by heterozygous loss-of-function variants in *SCAF4* (SR-related CTD-associated factor 4), which encodes an RNA polymerase II CTD-binding factor critical for mRNA transcription termination and suppression of premature cleavage and polyadenylation [F1, F2]. While early ascertainment suggested a uniform, recognizable presentation, recent cohort expansions encompassing at least 68 to 69 cumulative individuals across international series have revealed significant variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance [F1, F2, F18].

---

## 1. Phenotypic Spectrum and Documented Clinical Frequencies

Identifying an individual with Fliedner-Zweier syndrome from clinical phenotype alone is exceptionally difficult because its core features overlap broadly with common neurodevelopmental presentations [F2, F5]. Across documented cohorts, prominent developmental delay and expressive speech impairment represent the most consistently penetrant features, even in individuals with preserved or borderline general intellectual abilities [F4, F5, F7].

## 2. Discriminative Features and Clinical Differential Diagnosis

Bedside differentiation of Fliedner-Zweier syndrome solely on clinical criteria is **not achievable with moderate or high clinical confidence**, and the confidence of bedside distinction without sequencing is very low [F2, F15]. Fliedner-Zweier syndrome exhibits extensive phenotypic pleiotropy: affected individuals present with non-specific developmental delay, a wide cognitive spectrum (from completely normal IQ or borderline intellectual disability to severe ID), variable speech delay, behavioral abnormalities (ADHD, autism spectrum traits), skeletal anomalies, and seizures [F1, F2, F4, F5].

### Confused Overlapping Disorders and Confidence of Distinction

1. **Spliceosomal and Transcription-Elongation Machinery Syndromes**:
   - *Overlapping entities*: *SRRM1*-related neurodevelopmental disorder (Altay et al., 2026, [10.1038/s41431-025-01966-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41431-025-01966-y)), *THOC6*-related neurodevelopmental disorder, *RNU4ATAC*-related microcephalic dwarfism/syndromes, and *SCAF8*-associated conditions [F2, F15].
   - *Overlapping findings*: Shared disruption of general mRNA transcription elongation and splicing creates converging clinical phenotypes involving developmental delay, speech limitations, muscular hypotonia, and non-specific skeletal anomalies [F2, F15].
   - *Confidence of bedside distinction*: **Very Low (<0.30)**. Transcriptome deregulations affect thousands of downstream targets (e.g., >9,000 deregulated genes and ~2,900 differentially spliced transcripts [F2]), producing convergent neurodevelopmental disruptions that cannot be separated clinically without exome or genome sequencing [F2, F15].
2. **Chromatin Remodeling Syndromes (e.g., Coffin-Siris Syndrome, *ARID1B* / *SMARCA4*)**:
   - *Overlapping findings*: Muscular hypotonia, intellectual impairment, expressive language delay, and subtle craniofacial dysmorphisms [F15].
   - *Discriminating clues*: Coffin-Siris and related SWI/SNFopathies frequently show fifth-digit nail/distal phalanx hypoplasia, pronounced coarse facial coarsening, and structural internal organ malformations, whereas FZS dysmorphism remains subtle and unclassifiable [F10, F15].
   - *Confidence of bedside distinction*: **Low to Moderate**. While digit hypoplasia points toward Coffin-Siris, genomic sequencing remains mandatory [F2, F15].

### Most Discriminating Features of Fliedner-Zweier Syndrome
When comparing an affected individual to patients with generic neurodevelopmental delay, the combination of features that raises suspicion includes:
- **Severe expressive language delay relative to general cognitive performance**: Speech deficits are consistently penetrant even in patients who preserve mild or near-normal non-verbal cognitive capacities [F4, F7].
- **Co-occurrence of axial and lower-extremity joint positional anomalies**: Recurrent skeletal signs specifically encompass scoliosis, developmental dysplasia of the hip, and clubfoot (talipes equinovarus) [F5, F12].
- **Pharmacoresponsive focal epilepsy**: When seizures occur (median onset around 4 years of age), they often present as focal seizures responsive to standard anti-seizure medications [F9, F13].

## 3. Exclusionary Criteria: Absent Features that Argue Against Diagnosis

Because positive findings are heterogeneous, negative criteria provide critical exclusionary value. Systematic evaluation of large cohorts (Schmid et al., 2025; Yan et al., 2025; n = 50 novel cases, 69 total reviewed) establishes that the following findings are **consistently absent** in Fliedner-Zweier syndrome; their presence argues strongly against the diagnosis [F6]:

1. **Severe Congenital Heart Defects**: Complex cardiac malformations requiring early surgical intervention (such as transposition of the great arteries, hypoplastic left heart syndrome, or tetralogy of Fallot) have not been observed as core features of *SCAF4* haploinsufficiency [F6].
2. **Profound Microcephaly or Macrocephaly at Birth**: Extreme congenital cranial volume anomalies (head circumference < -3 SD or > +3 SD at birth) are absent [F6].
3. **Limb Reduction Defects**: Congenital terminal or intercalary limb reductions (e.g., amelia, phocomelia, ectrodactyly) are completely absent; skeletal manifestations are restricted to joint positioning and axial alignment abnormalities (scoliosis, hip dysplasia, clubfoot) [F5, F6, F14].
4. **Anterior Chamber Dysgenesis / Corneal Clouding**: Severe structural ocular anterior segment malformations (e.g., Peters anomaly, congenital corneal clouding) are absent [F6].
5. **Progressive Neuroregression or Neurodegenerative Decline**: Fliedner-Zweier syndrome is a static neurodevelopmental delay/disability; progressive neurodegenerative loss of previously acquired motor, cognitive, or speech milestones strongly excludes FZS and points to lysosomal, mitochondrial, or Rett-like neurodegenerative entities [F6].
6. **Gross Structural Brain Malformations**: More than 70% of evaluated patients demonstrate entirely normal brain anatomy on neuroimaging (or minor, non-specific findings such as mild ventriculomegaly or corpus callosum hypoplasia); gross malformations of cortical development (e.g., lissencephaly, pachygyria, holoprosencephaly) argue strongly against *SCAF4* etiology [F3, F9].

## 4. Phenotype Differences by Variant Type, Position, Age, and Sex

### Variant Type: Truncating vs. Missense Alleles
- **Loss-of-Function (Truncating and Splice-Site)**: Pathogenic variants in *SCAF4* predominantly comprise nonsense, frameshift, and canonical splice-site variants that result in loss of function [F1, F2, F8]. In cohort analyses (Schmid et al., 2025), identified pathogenic variants comprised 25 truncating, 8 splice-site, and 5 missense alleles [F2].
- **Missense Variants**: All identified missense variants—including those residing within the CTD-interacting domain—are currently classified as variants of uncertain significance (VUS) [F2, F16]. No missense change has been conclusively validated by functional assays to cause classical Fliedner-Zweier syndrome [F16].

### Spatial Position and Nonsense-Mediated Decay (NMD) Escape
- **N-Terminal and Central Two-Thirds**: Confirmed pathogenic loss-of-function variants concentrate within the N-terminal and central two-thirds of the *SCAF4* coding sequence (e.g., within upstream exons through exon 14), which undergo degradation by nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD) [F8].
- **C-Terminal Truncations**: Truncating variants localized to the final exon or distal C-terminal region (distal to the CTD-interacting domain) escape NMD [F8]. These predicted escape variants (such as 10 observed last-exon truncating variants) fail to cause classical Fliedner-Zweier syndrome and behave as benign polymorphisms or alleles of uncertain significance [F8].

### Inheritance and Variable Expressivity
- **Inheritance Breakdown**: Initial discovery cohorts documented predominantly de novo alterations (8 de novo vs. 1 inherited in Fliedner et al., 2020) [F1, F11]. Expanded multicenter cohort matchmaking revealed an inheritance rate of over 25% (13 inherited vs. 23 de novo cases in Schmid et al., 2025) [F1, F11, F15].
- **Familial Expressivity**: Inherited variants demonstrate marked intra-familial variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance. Transmitting carrier parents frequently exhibit completely normal IQ, borderline intellectual disability, or subtle isolated speech delay compared to more noticeably affected probands [F1, F4, F11].

### Age and Sex Distribution
- **Age-Dependent Expression**: The neurodevelopmental phenotype evolves with age: infant presentations center on hypotonia and early motor delay, while preschool and school-age presentations (e.g., median seizure onset around 4 years of age; proband evaluations at 4.58 to 16 years) reveal expressive speech impairment, learning difficulties, ADHD, and autistic behaviors [F1, F2, F7, F10, F13].
- **Sex Distribution**: No significant sex bias in penetrance, inheritance, or clinical severity has been documented between male and female individuals [F18].

## 5. Literature Contradictions and Variable Reporting

Direct, irreconcilable contradictions between published studies do not exist; however, several important discrepancies in clinical penetrance and imaging findings have emerged between initial discovery reports and larger follow-up series [F3]:

1. **Penetrance of Intellectual Disability (100% vs. Incomplete Penetrance)**:
   - *Discovery Phase*: Fliedner et al. (2020; PMID: [33232675](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33232675/)) reported global developmental delay and/or intellectual disability in 13/13 (100%) of assessed individuals [F4, F10].
   - *Cohort Expansion*: Schmid et al. (2025; [10.1038/s41431-024-01760-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41431-024-01760-2)) demonstrated that intellectual disability exhibits incomplete penetrance, spanning a continuum from completely normal IQ to severe intellectual disability, with carrier parents and selected probands exhibiting preserved general cognitive capacity [F1, F4].
2. **Epilepsy and Seizure Penetrance**:
   - *Initial and Targeted Reports*: Initial reports indicated seizures in 20% to 30% of patients, whereas specialized epilepsy cohorts (Hu et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2024) documented 100% (3/3) seizure presence [F3, F13].
   - *Isolated Case Reports*: Recent single-case publications (e.g., Chen et al., 2025; Yan et al., 2025) describe confirmed de novo pathogenic nonsense variants with no reported seizures or epilepsy [F3, F17].
3. **Neuroimaging Spectrum on Brain MRI**:
   - *Reporting Variations*: While gross structural malformations are universally absent, reports vary regarding subtle findings. Initial series described non-specific mild ventriculomegaly or corpus callosum hypoplasia in a subset of patients, whereas multiple subsequent series report completely normal cranial MRI in the majority (e.g., 2/3 in Hu et al., 2024, and >70% across expanded cohorts) [F3, F9].

## 6. Analysis of Deprioritised and Contested Hypotheses

To ensure complete coverage of the current literature, hypotheses that were deprioritised due to being contested or refuted are reviewed below with the specific reasons they were set aside:

- **Proposed Pathognomonic Clinical Triad (DEPRIORITISED / Contested)**: Hypothesis [F15] suggested a defining clinical triad of infantile hypotonia, severe expressive speech delay, and mild facial dysmorphism. This was deprioritised because expanded multi-center series (Schmid et al., 2025; Yan et al., 2025) emphasize that seizures, skeletal anomalies (clubfoot, scoliosis, hip dysplasia), and behavioral issues are core recurrent features, and the high degree of phenotypic pleiotropy rules out any reliable pathognomonic triad [F5, F15].
- **Exclusivity and Specificity of Skeletal Findings (DEPRIORITISED / Contested)**: Hypotheses [F12] and [F14] proposed that skeletal anomalies are confined to axial/joint defects in 25–50% of patients and serve as specific bedside discriminators. These were deprioritised because several *SCAF4* patients manifest primarily with epilepsy and cognitive delay without skeletal findings, literature describes "various structural anomalies" without confirmed 25–50% bounds, and no comparative trials have verified their diagnostic specificity against other spliceosomal disorders [F12, F14].
- **Epilepsy Penetrance Fixed Below 40% (DEPRIORITISED / Contested)**: Hypothesis [F13] asserted seizures occur in <40% of unselected cohorts. While epilepsy cohorts show 100% penetrance and isolated case reports show 0%, expanded multi-center registries list epilepsy as a recurrent feature without confirming a rigid <40% threshold [F3, F13].
- **Domain-Specific Epilepsy Risk (DEPRIORITISED / Contested)**: Hypothesis [F17] suggested variants in functional domain-encoding exons cause significantly higher seizure risks. This was contested because Chen et al. (2025) identified an individual with an RRM-domain truncating variant who did not develop seizures, and Schmid et al. (2025) showed broad, incomplete seizure penetrance across variant positions [F17].
- **Causality of CTD-Interacting Missense Variants (DEPRIORITISED / Contested)**: Hypothesis [F16] suggested that missense variants within the CTD-interacting domain cause classical FZS. This was deprioritised because Schmid et al. (2025) classified all five evaluated missense variants (including three in the CTD domain) as variants of uncertain significance (VUS), with no functional transactivation or Pol II-binding assay confirming pathogenicity [F16].
- **Lack of Genotype-Phenotype Correlations Due to Cohort Size <20 (DEPRIORITISED / Refuted)**: Hypothesis [F18] claimed genotype-phenotype correlations could not be established because fewer than 20 cases had been published. This is directly refuted by Schmid et al. (2025), Chen et al. (2025), and Yan et al. (2025), which expanded the documented cohort to 68–69 individuals, established that missense variants remain VUS, and demonstrated clear spatial NMD-escape positional effects [F2, F8, F18].

## 7. Synthesis and Cross-Cutting Translational Patterns

1. **Ascertainment Bias and Penetrance Erosion across Generations**: As observed across monogenic neurodevelopmental syndromes, early discovery cohorts systematically overestimate penetrance (100% intellectual disability in initial 9 families) [F4, F10]. Broadened cohort matchmaking unmasks transmitting carrier parents with normal IQ or borderline impairment, demonstrating that *SCAF4* haploinsufficiency is subject to genetic modifiers or developmental buffering [F1, F4].
2. **Convergent Cellular Machinery Precludes Phenotypic Differentiation**: Because *SCAF4* acts in tandem with RNA polymerase II and general transcription-elongation complexes, its disruption mirrors other spliceosomal and transcription-processing disorders (*SRRM1*, *THOC6*, *RNU4ATAC*) [F2]. Differentiation at the bedside is not achievable (confidence <0.30); clinical diagnosis requires exome or genome sequencing [F2, F15].
3. **Discriminative Utility of Negative Features**: Given the non-specific nature of positive neurodevelopmental signs, negative exclusionary criteria (absence of complex congenital heart disease, absence of limb reduction, absence of neuroregression, and normal brain MRI anatomy in >70%) provide stronger diagnostic filtering power than positive features alone [F6, F9].
4. **Spatial Transcript Position and Quality Control**: The pathogenicity of *SCAF4* loss-of-function variants is strictly governed by transcript architecture: N-terminal and central truncations trigger nonsense-mediated mRNA decay, resulting in true haploinsufficiency, whereas C-terminal truncations escape NMD and fail to produce the core syndromic phenotype [F8].
5. **Selective Vulnerability of Language Circuits**: Expressive speech delay remains severely impaired even in patients with preserved general intelligence, suggesting specialized vulnerability of human corticostriatal speech circuits to transcription termination defects [F4, F7].
