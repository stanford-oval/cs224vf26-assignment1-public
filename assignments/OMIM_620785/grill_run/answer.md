# Phenotypic Identification of ACBD6-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorder with Progressive Movement Abnormalities (OMIM #620785)

## Introduction and Scope
Neurodevelopmental disorder with progressive movement abnormalities (NEDPM; OMIM #620785) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in *ACBD6* (acyl-CoA-binding domain-containing protein 6) (F1, F9). Clinically, the disorder is defined by global developmental delay and intellectual disability coupled with an evolving, complex spectrum of progressive movement abnormalities, notably early-onset dystonia and postural deformities (F1, F2). Because neurodevelopmental delay and hyperkinetic or hypokinetic movement disorders overlap extensively with other pediatric movement disorders, identifying affected individuals based on phenotype alone requires precise differentiation of invariant core features, key neuroimaging hallmarks, and consistently absent exclusionary signs.

## Reported Clinical Phenotypes and Cohort Frequencies

The comprehensive clinical spectrum of *ACBD6* deficiency has been systematically defined across two primary clinical cohorts:
1. The multinational discovery and characterization cohort by Kaiyrzhanov et al. (2024) [PMID: 37951597; DOI: 10.1093/brain/awad380; PMC10994533], comprising 45 affected individuals across 28 unrelated families (F1, F2, F7).
2. The initial familial report by Yeetong et al. (2022) [DOI: 10.1212/nxg.0000000000200046], comprising 2 affected adult siblings from a consanguineous pedigree (F7, F9).

## Most Discriminating Features and Key Differential Diagnoses

To identify an *ACBD6*-deficient patient from a cohort of individuals with overlapping developmental and extrapyramidal movement disorders, clinicians must rely on distinct combinations of motor semiology, non-neurological traits, and midline brain structural neuroimaging (F1, F6).

### 1. Neuroimaging Signatures vs. Pure Movement Semiology
Movement phenomenology alone is insufficient to isolate *ACBD6* deficiency because dystonia, choreoathetosis, and tremor overlap across childhood neurodevelopmental movement disorders (F1, F2, F6). Neuroimaging provides superior specificity:
- **Corpus callosum abnormalities** (hypoplasia or dysgenesis in 70%, ~31/45) (F6).
- **Anterior commissure hypoplasia or agenesis** (present in 66%, ~30/45) (F6).
- **Focal midbrain and vermian anomalies**: Short midbrain (38%, 11/45 or 11 evaluated) and small inferior cerebellar vermis (38%, 11/45 or 11 evaluated) (F12).

### 2. Differentials, Confused Conditions, and Confidence of Distinction

* **Neurodegeneration with Brain Iron Accumulation (NBIA)** (e.g., *PANK2*, *PLA2G6*):
  * *Overlap*: Progressive childhood dystonia, gait impairment, parkinsonian signs (F5, F6).
  * *Distinction*: Classical NBIA demonstrates iron accumulation on T2*/SWI (e.g., eye-of-the-tiger sign in PKAN) and lacks anterior commissure agenesis (F6, F15). Although clava hypertrophy occurs in ~24% of *ACBD6* patients (F16), it is also present in infantile neuroaxonal dystrophy (*PLA2G6*/NBIA) (Al, 2016, DOI: 10.1007/s00234-016-1726-6) [F16], meaning clava hypertrophy cannot distinguish *ACBD6* from INAD. Instead, anterior commissure and corpus callosum dysgenesis without pathological iron deposition distinguish *ACBD6* (F6).
  * *Confidence*: **High** when MRI sequences evaluating commissures and iron deposition are available.

* ***GNAO1*-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorder / Chorea**:
  * *Overlap*: Severe early-onset developmental delay, hypotonia, prominent hyperkinetic movement disorder (dystonia and chorea) (F1, F6, F13).
  * *Distinction*: *GNAO1* is typically driven by acute, life-threatening hyperkinetic choreic or dystonic crises (status dystonicus), whereas *ACBD6* exhibits a more insidious, chronic progressive dystonia (94%) with early postural deformities (97%), ataxia (85%), and structural commissural dysgenesis (corpus callosum 70%, anterior commissure 66%) (F1, F2, F3, F6).
  * *Confidence*: **High**.

* ***NKX2-1*-Related Choreoathetosis (Benign Hereditary Chorea)**:
  * *Overlap*: Chorea, ataxia, developmental motor delay (F1, F3, F6).
  * *Distinction*: *NKX2-1* typically presents with the "brain-lung-thyroid" triad (congenital hypothyroidism, respiratory distress syndrome, chorea) without profound intellectual disability or commissural agenesis (F1, F6). Dystonia is the dominant feature in *ACBD6* (94%), while classic chorea is far less prominent (F2).
  * *Confidence*: **High**.

* **Pontocerebellar Hypoplasia (PCH)**:
  * *Overlap*: Microcephaly, developmental delay, chorea/dystonia (F4, F12).
  * *Distinction*: PCH presents with severe, diffuse pontine and generalized cerebellar hemispheric atrophy. In contrast, *ACBD6* brainstem anomalies are strictly focal (short midbrain in 38% and small inferior vermis in 38%) without generalized pontocerebellar devastation (F12).
  * *Confidence*: **High**.

## Exclusionary and Absent Features (Red Flags Against Diagnosis)

Phenotypic features systematically evaluated and confirmed **absent** across *ACBD6* cohorts serve as strong counter-indicators that effectively exclude the diagnosis (F8):

1. **Acute Episodic Metabolic Crises (Ketoacidosis, Severe Lactic Acidosis, Hyperammonemia)**:
   Patients with *ACBD6* mutations do not experience recurrent metabolic decompensation, Reye-like episodes, or paroxysmal ketoacidosis during catabolic stress, unlike organic acidemias or mitochondrial defects (Kaiyrzhanov, 2023, DOI: 10.1093/brain/awad380; Zhao et al., 2025, DOI: 10.1038/s41525-025-00487-3) [F8, F9].
2. **Primary Sensory-Motor Peripheral Polyneuropathy**:
   Neurophysiological and clinical evaluations have confirmed the absence of severe primary sensory-motor peripheral polyneuropathy (F8). Peripheral nerve conduction studies are preserved.
3. **Extraneural Visceral Organomegaly in Infancy or Childhood**:
   Childhood-onset hepatomegaly or splenomegaly is absent in *ACBD6* cohorts (F8). (Adult-onset cirrhosis in two siblings was a late complication without childhood storage-disease hepatosplenomegaly) (F7, F8, F9).
4. **Obligate Microcephaly**:
   While microcephaly occurs in 39% (18/45) and congenital microcephaly in 33% (7/21 evaluated), over 60% of patients have a normal head circumference (F4). Therefore, the absence of microcephaly does not exclude *ACBD6*, but an assumption that microcephaly is mandatory is false.

## Genotype, Position, Age, and Sex Correlations

* **Variant Type and Position**:
  Pathogenic variants across *ACBD6* are overwhelmingly biallelic complete loss-of-function (LOF) alleles (nonsense, frameshift, canonical splice-site) (F1, F14). In the discovery cohort, 18 of 20 distinct mutations were LOF, alongside one missense and one in-frame insertion (Kaiyrzhanov et al., 2024, DOI: 10.1093/brain/awad380) [F14]. Because almost all patients harbor biallelic null alleles, there is **no allelic gradient or position-specific phenotype correlation** (F14). True hypomorphic missense variants causing an attenuated or isolated late-onset dystonia phenotype have been ruled out; exome screening of 1,924 late-onset isolated dystonia patients failed to identify *ACBD6* as a causative locus (Thomsen et al., 2025, DOI: 10.1002/acn3.70100) [F14].
* **Age-Dependent Evolution**:
  Phenotypic manifestations evolve predictably across the lifespan:
  * *Infancy / Early Childhood*: Moderate-to-severe global developmental delay (100%), early axial hypotonia, speech absence/impairment (98%), followed by early-onset progressive postural deformities (97%) and generalized/axial dystonia (90–94%) (F1, F2, F14).
  * *Late Childhood / Adolescence*: Upper limb tremor (63%), head tremor (59%), cerebellar ataxia (85%), and excessive weight gain / overweight (59%) (F2, F3, F7, F10).
  * *Adulthood*: Parkinsonism and hypokinesia manifest as late, secondary features in only ~32% (14/45) of patients (F5, F13). Multiorgan complications (cirrhosis, diabetes, renal failure) were seen in a single family in their early 30s (F7).
* **Patient Sex**:
  No differences in penetrance, severity, or semiology have been documented between male and female individuals.

## Contradictions and Deprioritised Hypotheses in the Literature

### 1. The Contradiction: Extraneural Multi-System Organ Failure vs. Pure Neurodevelopmental Syndrome
* **The Contradiction**:
  * Yeetong et al. (2022) [DOI: 10.1212/nxg.0000000000200046] reported two adult siblings with homozygous *ACBD6* mutations who developed adult-onset hepatic cirrhosis, type 2 diabetes mellitus, end-stage renal failure, and refractory pancytopenia, leading to death in their early 30s (F7, F9, F13).
  * In contrast, Kaiyrzhanov et al. (2024) [PMID: 37951597] evaluated 45 affected individuals from 28 families (including surviving individuals up to 50 years of age) and observed **0% (0/45)** incidence of hepatic cirrhosis, diabetes mellitus, end-stage renal disease, or pancytopenia (F7, F9, F11).
* **Resolution**:
  Extraneural multiorgan decompensation is not an obligate late-stage manifestation of single-gene *ACBD6* loss (F7, F11). While metabolic dysregulation frequently causes excessive weight gain/overweight in 59% (26/45) of individuals, progression to multi-organ failure reflects specific familial backgrounds, consanguinity modifiers, or rare secondary hits rather than the core *ACBD6* natural history (F7, F10, F11).

### 2. Deprioritised Hypotheses and Rationale for Deprioritisation

* **Deprioritised Hypothesis [F13] (Contested)**:
  * *Hypothesis*: The core discriminative profile is early delay evolving into a mixed dystonia-parkinsonism and choreoathetosis syndrome without metabolic or systemic failure, separating it from GNAO1, NKX2-1, and NBIA.
  * *Reason Deprioritised*: Contested by the literature on two grounds. First, parkinsonism/hypokinesia is only present in 32% (14/45) of individuals and thus cannot serve as an obligatory discriminative criterion (F5, F13). Second, Yeetong et al. (2022) established that systemic metabolic/organ failure *can* occur in specific backgrounds (F7, F13).
* **Deprioritised Hypothesis [F14] (Refuted)**:
  * *Hypothesis*: Truncating LOF variants cause early-onset severe disease, while hypomorphic missense variants in the acyl-CoA binding domain cause slower progression with late-onset dystonia.
  * *Reason Deprioritised*: Refuted. Empirical data from 45 patients show near-exclusive LOF variants (18/20 alleles) with uniform moderate-to-severe presentations, and large-scale sequencing of 1,924 late-onset dystonia cohorts failed to show ACBD6 involvement (F14).
* **Deprioritised Hypothesis [F15] (Refuted)**:
  * *Hypothesis*: The composite combination of anterior commissure hypoplasia/agenesis and corpus callosum dysgenesis occurs in >60% of cases and reliably differentiates ACBD6 from NBIA.
  * *Reason Deprioritised*: Refuted. While isolated rates are 70% for corpus callosum and 66% for anterior commissure, no study has reported their combined composite co-occurrence rate or validated this composite against NBIA cohorts (F15).
* **Deprioritised Hypothesis [F16] (Screen/Untested)**:
  * *Hypothesis*: Hypertrophy of the clava (gracile tubercle) is a pathognomonic marker occurring in 24% of ACBD6 patients and absent in differentials.
  * *Reason Deprioritised*: Refuted/Contradicted. Hypertrophy of the clava is an established neuroimaging hallmark of infantile neuroaxonal dystrophy (INAD / *PLA2G6*), where it is present in 8/8 evaluated cases, disproving that it is pathognomonic for ACBD6 (Al, 2016, DOI: 10.1007/s00234-016-1726-6) [F16].

### Clinical Summary
A clinician can confidently suspect *ACBD6* deficiency when an individual presents with **early-onset global developmental delay, severe expressive speech impairment, early postural deformities, and chronic progressive dystonia accompanied by ataxia**, in the presence of **corpus callosum and anterior commissure hypoplasia** on neuroimaging, in the **strict absence** of paroxysmal metabolic crises or peripheral polyneuropathy (F1, F2, F3, F6, F8).
