# Clinical Identification and Phenotypic Discrimination of Alsahan-Harris Syndrome (OMIM #621307)

## Clinical Spectrum and Exact Cohort Phenotype Frequencies

Alsahan-Harris syndrome (ALHAS; OMIM #621307) is an autosomal recessive developmental ciliopathy caused by biallelic pathogenic variants in *TBC1D32* (OMIM #615867) on chromosome 6q22 F1, F2. The disorder manifests along a broad developmental continuum, ranging from severe, life-limiting prenatal presentations with major central nervous system and craniofacial midline anomalies to surviving pediatric patients with neurodevelopmental, endocrinologic, and sensory impairments F1, F2, F3.

To identify an affected individual purely from phenotype, clinicians must evaluate specific multi-system clusters combining anterior pituitary insufficiency, oral-facial-digital dysmorphisms, polydactyly, and retinal disease F1, F3, F4. ## Key Phenotypic Discriminators and Differential Diagnosis

Clinical differentiation of Alsahan-Harris syndrome is challenging because its findings intersect with several established ciliopathies and non-ciliated developmental malformation syndromes F1, F3. The definitive discriminators and differential confidence levels are summarized below:

### 1. Classic Joubert Syndrome (JS) and Joubert Syndrome and Related Disorders (JSRD)
* **Overlapping Features:** Cerebellar vermis hypoplasia/dysplasia, midline brain malformations, retinal dystrophy, and nystagmus or ocular motor apraxia F3, F9, F14.
* **Distinguishing Features:** The primary diagnostic discriminator for Alsahan-Harris syndrome is the prominent presence of severe congenital anterior pituitary endocrinopathy (growth hormone deficiency or panhypopituitarism) alongside mesoaxial or postaxial polydactyly and oral mucosal anomalies (hyperplastic frenula, lobulated tongue, lingual hamartomas) F1, F3, F9.
* **Diagnostic Confidence:** **Moderate-to-High**. While hypothalamic-pituitary dysfunction and oral frenula can occasionally occur across the extended JSRD/OFD-VI spectrum, diffuse pituitary aplasia/hypoplasia paired with classic oral-facial-digital signs strongly tilts the diagnosis toward *TBC1D32* deficiency F3, F8, F9.

### 2. Oral-Facial-Digital Syndrome Type IX (OFD-IX)
* **Overlapping Features:** Retinal dysplasia/coloboma, oral frenula, lingual nodules, midline clefts, and polydactyly F1, F3, F9, F12.
* **Distinguishing Features:** OFD-IX (OMIM #258865) and Alsahan-Harris syndrome represent allelic manifestations of biallelic *TBC1D32* variants F12. The diagnosis of ALHAS is applied where life-limiting prenatal midline craniofacial/CNS malformations and severe structural pituitary aplasia dominate the presentation, whereas OFD-IX emphasizes the non-lethal oral, digital, and ocular triad F1, F2, F12.
* **Diagnostic Confidence:** **Moderate (Allelic Spectrum)**. Distinguishing between them relies on the severity of midline pituitary-hypothalamic structural absence and central nervous system lethality rather than mutually exclusive clinical boundaries F2, F12.

### 3. Pallister-Hall Syndrome (PHS; *GLI3*-related)
* **Overlapping Features:** Central polydactyly (mesoaxial polydactyly), hypothalamic/pituitary dysfunction, and midline facial defects F1, F3, F6.
* **Distinguishing Features:** Alsahan-Harris syndrome features genuine ciliary neurosensory involvement (retinopathy, oculomotor apraxia) and structural pituitary stalk interruption/pituitary aplasia without the pathognomonic non-ciliary anomalies of PHS F3, F4, F6. Conversely, Pallister-Hall syndrome is characterized by hypothalamic hamartomas, bifid epiglottis, and distal enteric aganglionosis (Hirschsprung disease) or imperforate anus F3, F6.
* **Diagnostic Confidence:** **High**. The absence of bifid epiglottis and anorectal/enteric malformations firmly excludes PHS in favor of ALHAS F6.

### 4. Meckel-Gruber Syndrome (MKS)
* **Overlapping Features:** Prenatally lethal presentation with occipital/central nervous system malformations and postaxial polydactyly F2, F5, F7.
* **Distinguishing Features:** Classical MKS is defined by bilateral diffuse cystic renal dysplasia and early neonatal lethality secondary to severe oligohydramnios-induced pulmonary hypoplasia F2, F5, F7. In contrast, fetuses with severe ALHAS demonstrate preservation of renal parenchymal architecture without diffuse multicystic renal dysplasia; perinatal mortality in ALHAS is driven by severe cranial/midline defects and primary cardiorespiratory compromise rather than renal-pulmonary sequence failure F2, F5.
* **Diagnostic Confidence:** **High**. Preserved renal parenchyma and normal amniotic fluid volume decisively differentiate lethal ALHAS from MKS F2, F5.

## Negative Discriminators: Features Characteristically Absent in Alsahan-Harris Syndrome

Specific congenital malformations have been systematically investigated and found absent in *TBC1D32*-associated ALHAS; their detection should prompt alternative syndromic diagnoses:

1. **Diffuse Multicystic Renal Dysplasia:** Classical large cystic kidneys leading to early severe oligohydramnios are characteristic of Meckel-Gruber syndrome but are preserved or absent in ALHAS fetuses (F2; F5; F7).
2. **Bifid Epiglottis:** A cardinal hallmark of Pallister-Hall syndrome (*GLI3*), which has not been observed in *TBC1D32* cohorts F6.
3. **Imperforate Anus and Distic Enteric Aganglionosis (Hirschsprung Disease):** Anorectal and posterior gut endodermal innervation defects are characteristic of GLI3-pathies (PHS) but are absent in ALHAS F6.
4. **Isolated Cranial Nerve III (Oculomotor Nerve) Palsy:** Although patients may present with cerebellar/ciliary oculomotor apraxia and nystagmus, isolated structural oculomotor nerve palsy is absent and should not be used as an ALHAS diagnostic marker F14.

## Phenotypic Variation Across Variant Type, Genomic Position, Age, and Sex

### Variant Type and Position (Breakdown of Binary Determinism)
Investigations of genotype-phenotype correlations in *TBC1D32* disease demonstrate an absence of strict binary determinism F12, F13:
* **Null vs. Hypomorphic Alleles:** Biallelic loss-of-function variants (frameshift, nonsense) and hypomorphic alleles (missense, splice-site) do not cleanly segregate into separate lethal versus non-lethal or aplastic versus hypoplastic pituitary categories F12, F13. Severe life-limiting fetal presentations (e.g., the 4 fetal probands in Harris et al., 2023) and surviving pediatric probands with combined pituitary hormone deficiency both occur across truncating and splice-site variant backgrounds F1, F2, F12, F13.
* **Pituitary Structural Variation:** Both complete anterior pituitary aplasia and structural hypoplasia occur across biallelic null and splice-site variants (e.g., aplasia in Finnish LoF siblings [2/2] vs. hypoplasia in Pakistani splice-variant probands [1/1]), reflecting variable expressivity along a shared ciliary and Sonic Hedgehog (SHH) signaling threshold rather than strict allele-class determinism F1, F13.

### Age-Dependent Ascertainment Bias
Clinical phenotype strongly correlates with gestational and post-natal age due to developmental timing and ascertainment feasibility F4, F14:
* **Prenatal / Fetal Presentation:** Prenatal cases present with hydrocephaly, encephalocele, microphthalmia, severe facial clefting, and polydactyly F1, F2, F5.
* **Post-Neonatal / Pediatric Presentation:** In survivors, neurosensory manifestations become ascertainable. Retinal dystrophy/retinopathy is documented in >75% of surviving children but cannot be assessed in fetal or early neonatal demise cohorts F4. Similarly, dynamic ocular motor apraxia and progressive endocrine growth failure only manifest during infancy and childhood F4, F14.

### Sex Correlation
No sex predilection or sex-specific phenotypic divergence has been documented; *TBC1D32* is an autosomal gene (6q22), and biallelic variants affect males and females with equal phenotypic severity F1, F2.

## Contested Hypotheses and Literature Discrepancies

Several hypotheses regarding *TBC1D32* disease have been contested or refuted in the recent literature and must be interpreted with caution:

* **Universality of Organ Dysplasia Exclusion vs. Fetal Lethality:** It was originally postulated that renal dysplasia and neonatal lethality are universally absent in ALHAS, acting as absolute negative criteria F7. However, Harris et al. (2023) documented 4 fetal probands among 14 affected individuals with severe prenatal phenotypes and life-limiting congenital anomalies, proving that lethal presentations do occur, albeit driven by cranial/midline defects rather than pulmonary hypoplasia from renal failure F2, F5, F7.
* **Pituitary Aplasia Penetrance Claims:** Deprioritised claims asserted that anterior pituitary aplasia or hypoplasia is present in >80% or 100% of all ALHAS cases and definitively distinguishes it from Joubert syndrome F8, F10, F11. Systematic reviews demonstrate that pituitary findings show variable structural penetrance (ranging from borderline hypoplasia to complete absence), while hypothalamic-pituitary endocrinopathies have also been documented in classic Joubert syndrome cohorts (e.g., NCBI 2019, DOI: 10.1136/bcr-2018-229016), precluding an absolute boundary F8, F11.
* **Oral Anomalies as Exclusive Joubert Discriminators:** The assertion that oral frenula and lingual nodules uniquely separate ALHAS from Joubert syndrome F9 is contested by evidence showing that hyperplastic oral frenulae and lingual hamartomas are established within the Joubert syndrome and related disorders spectrum (specifically OFD-VI; MA Parisi, 2009, DOI: 10.1002/ajmg.c.30229) F9.
* **Isolated Oculomotor Nerve Palsy:** The claim that isolated third cranial nerve palsy distinguishes ALHAS from Pallister-Hall syndrome F14 has been refuted; ALHAS exhibits ocular motor apraxia and nystagmus rather than isolated cranial nerve III palsy F14.
* **Requirement of Secondary Genetic Modifiers (e.g., SCLT1):** The hypothesis that structural renal phenotypes in *TBC1D32* require digenic inheritance or synergistic modifier alleles in transition zone genes such as *SCLT1* F15 was refuted. Biallelic *TBC1D32* mutations are fully sufficient to produce multi-organ ciliopathy phenotypes in an autosomal recessive manner F15.

## Synthesis and Open Diagnostic Questions

### Multi-Axis Syndromic Convergence
The clinical signature of Alsahan-Harris syndrome lies at the intersection of three embryonic axes: neuroendocrine Rathke's pouch development, oral-facial branchial patterning, and limb bud digit specification F1, F3, F9. While classic Joubert ciliopathies primarily disrupt cerebellar vermis and hindbrain architecture, and classic OFD syndromes focus on oral mucosal clefts and digit duplication, *TBC1D32* deficiency uniquely couples severe anterior pituitary stalk interruption or aplasia with oral-facial-digital dysmorphisms F1, F3, F10.

### Organ-Sparing Phenotypic Divergence
A key unresolved developmental question is why metanephric collecting duct ciliogenesis is relatively spared in *TBC1D32* deficiency compared to core ciliary transition zone disorders (such as *TMEM67*- or *CEP290*-associated Meckel-Gruber syndrome) F2, F7. This organ-sparing divergence provides a reliable negative radiological discriminator: preserved renal architecture in a fetus with severe occipital/midline brain malformations and polydactyly argues specifically for *TBC1D32* ciliopathy F2, F5.

### Recommended Clinical Decision Workup
When faced with an infant or fetus presenting with midline facial anomalies, polydactyly, or vermis hypoplasia, clinicians should:
1. **Screen the Endocrine Axis Early:** Perform urgent neuroendocrine testing (cortisol, free T4, GH/IGF-1) and high-resolution pituitary MRI targeting the hypothalamic-pituitary axis F1, F8, F10.
2. **Examine Oral Mucosa and Digits:** Inspect for accessory oral frenula, lobulated tongue, lingual hamartomas, and central (mesoaxial) polydactyly F1, F3, F9.
3. **Assess Retinal Integrity:** In live-born infants, conduct funduscopy and electroretinography to detect early ciliary retinopathy F1, F4.
4. **Apply Negative Discriminators:** Rule out Pallister-Hall syndrome by the absence of bifid epiglottis and imperforate anus, and rule out Meckel-Gruber syndrome by the absence of diffuse cystic renal enlargement F2, F5, F6.
