# Clinical Identification and Phenotypic Profile of Spinocerebellar Ataxia 52 (SCA52 / RAB3A)

Spinocerebellar ataxia 52 (SCA52; OMIM #621535) is an autosomal dominant neurodegenerative disorder caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants in the *RAB3A* gene (Ras-related protein Rab-3A), most notably the recurrent missense hotspot variant c.247C>T (p.Arg83Trp) in the switch II domain [F1, F2]. Identifying an individual with SCA52 within a heterogeneous cohort presenting with cerebellar ataxia or spastic paraplegia requires integrating core motor features, invariant neuroimaging hallmarks, and explicit pertinent negatives [F1, F6, F7].

---

## 2. Diagnostic Discriminators and Differential Diagnosis

### Most Discriminating Features
1. **Isolated Cerebellar Vermian Atrophy on Neuroimaging:** Highly penetrant (100% in symptomatic index cohorts) symmetrical cerebellar cortical and vermian atrophy without severe pontine flattening or primary spinal cord atrophy serves as the essential neuroimaging discriminator [F6, F8].
2. **Concomitant Pyramidal Signs (Ataxia-Spasticity Overlap):** Slowly progressive adult-onset cerebellar ataxia accompanied by mild gait spasticity and hyperreflexia distinguishes SCA52 from purely cortical spinocerebellar ataxias (e.g., SCA6) [F2, F3, F9].
3. **Cerebellar Outflow Tremor:** Action or postural tremors (e.g., head/neck titubation or upper limb action tremor) co-occurring with gait ataxia provide a recognizable diagnostic clue in specific pedigrees [F2, F9].

## 3. Pertinent Negative Findings and Exclusionary Criteria

Documented pertinent negatives function as critical filters to exclude SCA52 [F7]:

- **Absence of Extraneurological Organ Involvement (0/N in confirmed index cohorts):** Extraneurological systemic involvement is completely absent (0/2 families assessed; 0% penetrance) in genetically confirmed heterozygous *RAB3A* index cases [F7]. Specifically:
  - **No cardiomyopathy or cardiac dysfunction** (HP:0001638) [F7].
  - **No visual failure, optic atrophy (HP:0000648), or chorioretinopathy** (HP:0000532) [F7].
  - **No hepatic dysfunction** (HP:0001392) [F7].
- **Absence of Prominent Lower Motor Neuron Disease:** Marked muscle fasciculations, acute denervation, or flaccid weakness argue against SCA52 [F10].
- **Absence of Severe Progressive Spinal Cord Atrophy:** Neuroimaging demonstrating profound spinal cord flattening without cerebellar vermian atrophy argues strongly against SCA52, pointing instead toward classical primary spastic paraplegias [F6, F8].

## 4. Genotype-Phenotype Correlations and Demographic Modifiers

- **Variant Type and Hotspot Position:** Pathogenic variants in *RAB3A* are primarily heterozygous missense alterations affecting critical GTP-binding or switch domains [F4]. The recurrent c.247C>T (p.Arg83Trp) missense mutation located within the switch II region accounts for 90% (9/10) of reported ataxia families [F1, F2]. Non-R83W loss-of-function or de novo variants have been documented in 3 families who exhibited early neurodevelopmental phenotypes rather than classic adult-onset ataxia [F2, F6].
- **Age of Onset:** Symptomatic ataxia in recurrent p.Arg83Trp carriers typically presents in adulthood, most frequently during the fourth decade of life, demonstrating a slowly progressive degenerative trajectory [F1]. This contrasts with non-progressive congenital ataxia [F1, F2].
- **Sex Modifiers:** No significant differences in penetrance, disease severity, or clinical phenotype have been documented between male and female carriers [F4].

## 5. Contradictory Findings and Deprioritised Hypotheses

Several clinical assertions regarding SCA52 remain contested or have been refuted in the recent literature:

1. **Adult-Onset Degeneration vs. Infantile Ataxic Encephalopathy (Contested):** Early disease reports suggested that *RAB3A* variants primarily cause early-onset ataxia with infantile hypotonia (HP:0001252) and non-progressive encephalopathy [F10, F12]. However, Miyamoto et al. (2026) and Hengel et al. (2025) demonstrated that SCA52 is predominantly an adult-onset, gradually progressive cerebellar ataxia syndrome with vermian atrophy [F1, F2, F12]. Infantile hypotonia and cognitive delay are variable, non-obligate manifestations rather than invariant core features [F5, F12].
2. **Prevalence of Tremor (Contested):** Miyamoto et al. (2026) reported frequent action, hand, and neck tremors accompanying ataxia in p.Arg83Trp carriers [F2, F11]. Conversely, Hengel et al. (2025) characterized a cohort of 18 affected individuals across 10 families as having a predominantly pure cerebellar syndrome without prominent extrapyramidal or action tremor [F1, F11]. Hypotheses asserting an exact tremor penetrance between 50% and 80% lack published quantitative validation [F11].
3. **Allele Specificity of Spastic Paraplegia vs. Ataxia (Refuted):** The hypothesis that spastic paraplegia is driven strictly by non-p.Arg83Trp mutations while p.Arg83Trp causes only ataxia [F16] was refuted by Roller et al. (2026), who showed that the p.Arg83Trp hotspot mutation itself causes spasticity across the unified ataxia-spasticity continuum [F2, F16].
4. **Unsubstantiated Penetrance Thresholds (Refuted):** Hypotheses asserting specific quantitative penetrance figures—such as pyramidal tract spasticity occurring in ">60% of adult carriers" [F13] or neurodevelopmental delay occurring in "<40%" [F14]—were deprioritised because the primary literature describes these manifestations qualitatively as variable co-phenotypes without publishing formal segregation numerators and denominators for those thresholds [F13, F14]. Similarly, formal multi-center exclusion cohorts for polyneuropathy and optic atrophy [F15] have not been formally published beyond index pedigree characterization [F7, F15].

---

## 6. Cross-Cutting Diagnostic Patterns and Synthesis

Clinical identification of SCA52 is informed by three overarching biological and clinical patterns:

- **Dissolution of the Ataxia-HSP Boundary:** Heterozygous *RAB3A* mutations demonstrate that cerebellar ataxia and hereditary spastic paraplegia represent artificial diagnostic silos [F3]. *RAB3A* variants account for approximately 0.81% (10/1235) of patients in mixed ataxia/spastic paraplegia screening cohorts [F2, F3]. Spasticity does not exclude SCA52 [F3].
- **Invariant Vermian Imaging Signature:** While peripheral and motor neurological findings overlap heavily with complicated HSPs (such as SPG7 and SPG11), isolated cerebellar vermian atrophy is 100% penetrant in symptomatic SCA52 individuals and serves as the essential diagnostic trigger [F6, F8].
- **Negative Extraneurological Filter:** Because RAB3A mediates calcium-triggered exocytosis primarily in neurons, non-neural tissues are spared [F7]. The presence of cardiomyopathy, hepatic disease, retinopathy, or optic atrophy effectively excludes SCA52 [F7].
