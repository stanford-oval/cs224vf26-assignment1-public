# Clinical Identification and Phenotypic Differentiation of Stankiewicz-Isidor Syndrome (OMIM #617516)

Stankiewicz-Isidor syndrome (STISS; OMIM #617516) is a congenital neurodevelopmental proteasomopathy caused by heterozygous disruption or haploinsufficiency of *PSMD12*, which encodes the non-ATPase regulatory subunit 12 (RPN5) of the 26S proteasome lid (F1; F2). Identifying an affected individual purely from clinical manifestations within a cohort of syndromic neurodevelopmental patients requires navigating high phenotypic variability, subtle and evolving facial gestalts, and substantial overlap with other chromatinopathies and ubiquitin-proteasome system disorders.

## 2. Phenotypic Discrimination and Differential Diagnosis

No single positive dysmorphic sign possesses standalone diagnostic specificity for STISS; rather, discrimination hinges on specific structural combinations and distinguishing features from syndromic mimics (F2; F19).

### Primary Differential Diagnoses and Discriminative Confidence

1. **Cornelia de Lange Syndrome (CdLS):**
   - *Overlapping features:* Developmental delay, microcephaly, intellectual disability, and upper-limb malformations (F2; F3).
   - *Discriminating features:* CdLS displays dense, arched eyebrows with continuous synophrys, hirsutism/generalized hypertrichosis, and severe longitudinal terminal limb deficiency (F3). STISS lacks generalized hypertrichosis and severe limb reduction, presenting instead with mild preaxial or distal digital variations (such as hypoplastic thumbs or 2-3 toe syndactyly) (F2; F3).
   - *Diagnostic Confidence:* **High** (distinguishable by facial hair patterning and limb defect severity) (F2; F3).

2. **Rubinstein-Taybi Syndrome (RSTS):**
   - *Overlapping features:* Developmental delay, high arched palates, and digital variations (F3).
   - *Discriminating features:* RSTS uniquely presents with broad, angulated thumbs and persistent broad great halluces (HP:0001822 / HP:0001844) (F3). Digital abnormalities in STISS are restricted to hypoplasia, preaxial polydactyly, or mild webbing/syndactyly without broad angulation (F2; F3).
   - *Diagnostic Confidence:* **High** (F3).

3. **Holt-Oram Syndrome:**
   - *Overlapping features:* Congenital heart defects (e.g., septal defects) accompanied by preaxial upper limb/thumb abnormalities (F2; F8).
   - *Discriminating features:* Holt-Oram is non-syndromic with respect to cognition (neurodevelopment is normal), whereas STISS manifests complete penetrance of cognitive delay and variable neurodevelopmental deficits (F1; F2; F7).
   - *Diagnostic Confidence:* **High** (F1; F2).

4. **FBXO11-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorder:**
   - *Overlapping features:* Autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental delay, intellectual disability, behavioral anomalies, bulbous nasal tip, and thin upper lip vermilion (A Gregor et al., 2018, 10.1016/j.ajhg.2018.07.003) (F10; F19).
   - *Discriminating features:* Facial gestalts between these two proteasome/ubiquitin pathway disorders overlap extensively; palatal clefting/bifid uvula occurs at comparable frequencies (~15-16% in both) (F10). Renal fusion anomalies (horseshoe kidney) and preaxial digital duplication/hypoplasia point preferentially to *PSMD12*, but standalone facial discrimination is unreliable (F2; F5; F19).
   - *Diagnostic Confidence:* **Low to Moderate** (molecular confirmation is mandatory) (F10; F19).

5. **Autoinflammatory Cutaneous Lesions (Chilblains):**
   - When present, pernio-like lesions (chilblains) occur in ~8.3% (2/24) of STISS patients due to proteasome impairment and subsequent type I interferon pathway upregulation (B Isidor et al., 2022, PMID:34906456) (F4; F14). While too insensitive to rule in all STISS patients, its presence effectively differentiates STISS from non-proteasomal mimics (such as pure chromatinopathies) (F4; F14).

## 3. Exclusionary Criteria and Hard Negative Features

Because positive dysmorphic features show high variable expressivity, negative likelihood indicators are exceptionally powerful for ruling out STISS (F2; F3).

The following clinical signs have been systematically evaluated and documented as **absent** in confirmed *PSMD12* cohorts; their presence argues decisively against STISS:

1. **Severe Longitudinal or Transverse Limb Reduction Defects:** Phocomelia, ectrodactyly, oligodactyly, or complete absent forearm structures (radial ray aplasia beyond simple thumb hypoplasia) do not occur in STISS (S Bézieau et al., 2022; A Feresin et al., 2025, 10.1002/ajmg.a.63953) (F2; F3). Extremity findings in STISS are limited to non-reduction distal or preaxial anomalies (e.g., thumb hypoplasia, preaxial polydactyly, brachydactyly, or 2-3 toe syndactyly) (F2).
2. **Generalized Dense Hypertrichosis and Marked Synophrys:** True dense generalized hypertrichosis and severe, conjoined synophrys (classic CdLS pattern) are absent in STISS (B Isidor et al., 2022; A Feresin et al., 2025) (F3).
3. **Broad, Angulated Thumbs and Halluces:** Disproportionately broad, radially angulated terminal phalanges of the thumbs or great toes (classic Rubinstein-Taybi pattern) are absent (F3).
4. **Isolated Visceral Malformation Without Postnatal Neurodevelopmental Involvement:** Although horseshoe kidney can present as an apparently isolated fetal ultrasound finding prenatally (1/45 trio exome cases in QX Yu et al., 2026, 10.1002/pd.70086), true isolated, developmentally normal outcomes postnatally exclude STISS; affected infants invariably show developmental delay or dysmorphic markers upon maturation (F5).

## 4. Phenotypic Correlates: Variant Type, Locus Position, Age, and Sex

### Variant Type: Intragenic SNVs/Indels vs. Contiguous Microdeletions
- **Monogenic Sufficiency:** Intragenic, isolated loss-of-function variants (such as nonsense variants, frameshifts, or canonical splice-site disruptions) are completely sufficient to produce the full-blown STISS phenotype, confirming haploinsufficiency as the primary mechanism (F1). In a familial series harboring the inherited truncating mutation c.865C>T (p.Arg289*), all 6/6 affected carriers exhibited intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, and limb/extremity anomalies (A Feresin et al., 2025, 10.1002/ajmg.a.63953) (F1).
- **Contiguous Gene Deletions (17q24.2):** Larger microdeletions encompassing *PSMD12* and neighboring loci (such as *BPTF*, *KPNA2*, *PRKAR1A*, and *KCNJ2*) cause overlapping STISS-like neurodevelopmental disorders, but contiguous gene effects can modify clinical severity (F11; F13; F15).

### Variant Position along PSMD12
- No definite intragenic linear genotype-phenotype correlation exists across *PSMD12*. Loss-of-function variants distributed across early, middle (e.g., p.Arg289*), or late exons lead to nonsense-mediated decay or loss of 26S proteasome lid structural integration, yielding overlapping phenotypes (F1; F7).

### Age Progression and Natural History
- **Infancy and Early Childhood:** Characterized by hypotonia, global developmental delay, subtle midface hypoplasia, feeding difficulties, and identification of congenital heart defects (41.7%) or renal fusion anomalies (F5; F7; F8).
- **Adolescence and Adulthood:** Natural history studies reveal facial coarsening, severe post-pubertal cystic acne, and late-onset central obesity (A Feresin et al., 2025, 10.1002/ajmg.a.63953) (F12). These acquired traits can mask subtle pediatric dysmorphic features, complicating retroactive visual gestalt identification in adult clinics (F12).

### Biological Sex
- STISS is an autosomal dominant condition affecting males and females equally, with no observed divergence in penetrance or organ malformation rates between sexes (F1; F2; F7).

## 5. Contested Evidence, Deprioritised Hypotheses, and Literature Contradictions

Several assertions in early literature have been contradicted or deprioritised by expanded patient cohorts:

### Contradiction 1: Attribution of Microcephaly in 17q24.2 Deletions (*PSMD12* vs. *BPTF*)
- *Contradiction:* S Küry et al. (2017, 10.1016/j.ajhg.2017.01.003) initially reported microcephaly in most patients with isolated *PSMD12* disruptions (F11). However, P Stankiewicz et al. (2017, 10.1016/j.ajhg.2017.08.014) demonstrated that haploinsufficiency of the adjacent chromatin remodeler *BPTF* produces syndromic postnatal microcephaly in 7/9 individuals, proposing that severe microcephaly in 17q24 microdeletions is driven by *BPTF* rather than *PSMD12* (F11). In the largest series, B Isidor et al. (2022) found marked phenotypic heterogeneity among point mutations and larger CNVs, failing to demonstrate a statistically significant increase in microcephaly strictly attributable to concurrent *BPTF* deletion (F11; F15).

### Contradiction 2: Autoinflammatory Manifestations and Interferon Signature Specificity
- *Contested Finding:* B Isidor et al. (2022, PMID:34906456) identified chilblains in 2/24 (8.3%) individuals and framed type I interferon pathway induction as a defining systemic hallmark of STISS (F4; F14). In contrast, A Feresin et al. (2025, 10.1002/ajmg.a.63953) identified zero clinical autoinflammatory manifestations (0/6) across two families and cautioned that type I interferon signature alterations are non-specific and lack diagnostic sensitivity (F4; F14).

### Contradiction 3: Visual Gestalt Retention vs. Adult Facial Coarsening
- *Contested Finding:* B Isidor et al. (2022) asserted that STISS presents with structural signs (enophthalmia, microtia, and preaxial changes) distinct enough to allow clinical recognition across ages (F12). Conversely, A Feresin et al. (2025) reported that evolving adult features—specifically facial coarsening, severe post-pubertal acne, and progressive obesity—obscure infantile craniofacial gestalts, hindering clinical diagnosis in adulthood without genomic testing (F12).

### Refuted Dysmorphic Frequency Claims (Deprioritised Findings F16, F17, F18, F19)
- **Constellation of Prominent Forehead, High-Arched Eyebrows, Bulbous Nose, and Thin Upper Lip >80% (F16):** Refuted. Available cohort datasets demonstrate marked intra- and interfamilial variability; no unified facial quartet reaches >80% across published series (B Isidor et al., 2022; A Feresin et al., 2025) (F16).
- **Digital Anomalies in 50–70% of Patients (F17):** Refuted. While mild digital variations occur, specific recurrent anomalies like 2-3 toe syndactyly were documented in only 5 of 24 individuals (20.8%) by S Bézieau et al. (2022), and their non-specific nature provides poor discriminatory power against syndromes like Silver-Russell or Dubowitz (F17).
- **Broad Forehead and High-Arched Eyebrows Sensitivity >75% (F18):** Refuted. Eyebrow findings are variable (often lateral flaring rather than high-arched), and systematic penetrance does not reach 75% in molecularly confirmed cohorts (F18).
- **Bulbous Nasal Tip and Thin Vermilion Specificity >85% (F19):** Refuted. These mid- and lower-face characteristics overlap directly with other neurodevelopmental syndromes, including *FBXO11* deficiency (A Gregor et al., 2018, 10.1016/j.ajhg.2018.07.003), preventing them from serving as standalone discriminators (F10; F19).

## 6. Cross-Cutting Synthesis and Clinical Diagnostic Strategy

When evaluating a patient with an undiagnosed neurodevelopmental disorder for Stankiewicz-Isidor syndrome, clinicians should utilize a hierarchical diagnostic framework:

1. **Screen for Core Neurodevelopmental Invariance:** Developmental delay/intellectual disability is obligate (~100% penetrance across cohorts) (F1; F7). An individual with entirely normal cognitive development does not have classic STISS.
2. **Apply Hard Exclusion Criteria:** Immediately evaluate the patient for severe transverse/longitudinal limb reduction (phocomelia, ectrodactyly), marked synophrys with generalized hypertrichosis, or broad angulated thumbs/halluces (F2; F3). The presence of any of these signs argues strongly against *PSMD12* disruption and indicates alternative diagnoses such as Cornelia de Lange or Rubinstein-Taybi syndromes (F3).
3. **Assess Visceral and Digital Anchors:** Identify visceral malformations (cardiac defects present in ~41.7% [10/24]; horseshoe kidney present in prenatal series) alongside non-reduction preaxial or distal digital variations (hypoplastic thumbs, preaxial polydactyly, or 2-3 toe syndactyly in ~20.8% [5/24]) (F2; F5; F8; F17).
4. **Factor in Age-Dependent Masking:** In adolescents and adults, look beyond facial gestalts: check for evolving features including severe acne and obesity that develop alongside baseline intellectual disability (F12).
5. **Molecular and Biochemical Confirmation:** Because facial dysmorphisms overlap significantly with E3-ubiquitin ligase disorders (such as *FBXO11*), definitive diagnosis requires identifying a heterozygous loss-of-function *PSMD12* variant or 17q24.2 microdeletion via exome/genome sequencing or chromosomal microarray (F1; F10; F19).
