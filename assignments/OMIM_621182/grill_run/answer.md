# Clinical Phenotypic Identification of *NAV3*-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorder (OMIM #621182)

Neurodevelopmental disorder with poor or absent speech, dysmorphic facies, and behavioral abnormalities (NEDSFB; OMIM #621182) is an ultra-rare Mendelian condition caused by pathogenic alterations in the *NAV3* gene (encoding neuron navigator 3) on chromosome 12q21 (F1). Identifying affected individuals from phenotype alone within a heterogeneous group of neurodevelopmental disorder (NDD) patients requires an evidence base of core frequencies, discriminating anchors, exclusionary pertinent negatives, and an understanding of conflicting clinical literature (F2, F4, F5, F6).

---

## Clinical Phenotypic Spectrum and Cohort-Specific Frequencies

Because the published literature remains constrained by small-sample ascertainment bias, exact frequencies must be evaluated strictly within their reported cohorts (F2). Across foundational cohorts, clinical manifestations center on severe developmental delays, expressive language deficits, microcephaly, behavioral disruptions, and minor craniofacial dysmorphic traits (F1, F4).

## Differential Diagnosis and Discriminating Clinical Findings

When evaluating a patient presenting with generalized intellectual disability, microcephaly, and absent speech, clinical distinction relies on specific positive markers and phenotypic boundaries:

1. **Unselected Non-Syndromic Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD):**
   - *Differentiating Features:* In large-scale sequencing of unselected ASD cohorts, *NAV3* pathogenic mutations are exceedingly rare (<0.5%; e.g., only 4 partial or whole gene *NAV3* mutations detected in 42,607 cases by Zhou et al., 2022, PMID: 35982159; 0 molecularly confirmed *NAV3* diagnoses in 174 Hungarian ASD cases by Bencsik et al.) (F3). Patients with *NAV3* alterations display severe syndromic intellectual disability, true microcephaly (HP:0000252), and dysmorphic features rather than isolated non-syndromic autism (F3, F4).
   - *Confidence in Distinction:* **High**. Primary non-syndromic ASD gene panels have negligible yield for *NAV3*, confirming it is a syndromic entity (F3).

2. ***SCN3A*-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorder:**
   - *Differentiating Features:* *SCN3A* pathogenic variants classically produce severe, treatment-resistant early-onset epileptic encephalopathy (present in 16/21 patients; Zaman et al., 2020, DOI: 10.1002/ana.25809) and prominent structural malformations of cortical development such as polymicrogyria (present in 15/19 patients; Zaman et al., 2020) (F5). Conversely, *NAV3*-related disorder lacks malformations of cortical development, and early-onset refractory epileptic encephalopathy is absent (F5).
   - *Confidence in Distinction:* **High**. The presence of cortical dysgenesis or intractable infantile seizures strongly favors *SCN3A* over *NAV3* (F5).

3. ***ASTN1*-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorder:**
   - *Differentiating Features:* Biallelic variants in *ASTN1* produce complex neurodevelopmental phenotypes (Levine et al., 2026, https://www.cell.com/ajhg/abstract/S0002-9297(25)00482-3) characterized by distinct structural neuroradiological abnormalities and variable motor manifestations, whereas *NAV3* presents with microcephaly, prominent absent speech, and behavioral disruptions in the absence of progressive motor deterioration or primary neuronal migration defects (F5).
   - *Confidence in Distinction:* **Moderate**. Distinction requires brain MRI and molecular sequencing as both involve neuronal morphogenesis and adhesion pathways (F1, F5).

4. **Classic Angelman Syndrome and *FOXP2*-Related Speech Apraxia:**
   - While theoretically considered in the differential of absent speech and behavioral excitation, formal clinical criteria differentiating *NAV3* syndrome from Angelman syndrome or *FOXP2* speech apraxia have not been validated in comparative clinical trials, and small cohort sizes preclude definitive statistical separation based purely on bedside gestalt (F16).
   - *Confidence in Distinction:* **Low to Unsubstantiated** by clinical trials; molecular confirmation is required (F16).

## Pertinent Negative Boundaries: Features Arguing Against *NAV3* Disorder

Given the non-specific nature of global developmental delay, invariant exclusionary findings (pertinent negatives) provide higher diagnostic specificity than positive core symptoms (F5):

- **Absence of Progressive Neurodegeneration or Regression:** Affected individuals display static neurodevelopmental delay. Progressive loss of acquired motor or cognitive milestones is consistently absent (0/11; Ghaffar et al., 2024; Kakar et al., 2025; Umair et al., 2025) (F5). Any neurodegenerative course argues decisively against *NAV3* (F5).
- **Absence of Major Cortical Malformations:** Comprehensive neuroimaging in molecularly confirmed cohorts does not reveal lissencephaly, pachygyria, or holoprosencephaly (0/11 in Ghaffar et al., 2024; 0/5 in Kakar et al., 2025) (F5). Brain structure is largely preserved aside from secondary microcephaly (F4, F5).
- **Absence of Early-Onset Intractable Epileptic Encephalopathy:** Unlike epileptic channelopathies (e.g., *SCN3A*), infantile spasms and intractable epileptic encephalopathies have not been observed in *NAV3* cohorts (0/5 in Kakar et al., 2025) (F5, F6).
- **Absence of Major Congenital Visceral or Cardiac Malformations:** No structural cardiac defects, congenital diaphragmatic hernias, or visceral organ dysplasias have been consistently linked to *NAV3* alterations (F5).

## Genotype-Phenotype, Domain, and Demographic Correlations

Clinical and genetic stratification reveals several critical properties of *NAV3*-related disease:

- **Inheritance Mode and Zygosity:** The primary delineation in OMIM #621182 (NEDSFB / Umair-Alfadhel syndrome) is autosomal recessive (F1). Biallelic variants—predominantly homozygous nonsense, frameshift, and essential splice-site mutations (such as c.243+1G>T, p.Gln2109Ter, p.Arg2193Ter, and p.Val870SerfsTer12)—constitute the clear majority of characterized families (e.g., 5 patients across 3 consanguineous families in Kakar et al., 2025; single probands in Umair et al., 2024 and 2025) (F1, F2, F10, F11). Healthy heterozygous carrier parents demonstrate that 50% loss of *NAV3* expression is insufficient to trigger the full syndromic entity, refuting simple haploinsufficiency as the universal mechanism (F11, F13).
- **Variant Position and Functional Domains:** Pathogenic missense and truncating variants do not strictly restrict to the conserved AAA ATPase or calponin homology (CH) domains (F17). For example, pathogenic missense mutations have been documented in the coiled-coil domain (p.Ser1681Arg) and other functional segments (p.Ser1144Pro), refuting strict domain clustering (F17). Zebrafish functional models show rescue by wild-type *NAV3* mRNA but failure of mutant rescue, corroborating loss-of-function mechanics (F17).
- **Age and Sex Correlations:** No sex predilection or sex-specific phenotypic divergence has been established; males and females are equitably affected in consanguineous pedigrees (F13). Phenotypes manifest from early infancy as delayed developmental milestones, motor delay, microcephaly, and emergent speech failure as the child matures into toddlerhood (F2, F4).

## Literature Contradictions and Contested Clinical Hypotheses

Careful review of the primary literature reveals several conflicting observations:

1. **Seizures and Epilepsy:** Kakar et al. (2025, DOI: 10.1007/s00439-024-02718-6) reported zero patients with seizures among 5 evaluated individuals across 3 families (0/5) (F6). Conversely, Umair et al. (2024, DOI: 10.1002/mgg3.2473; 2025, DOI: 10.2174/0109298673367919250626005640) documented febrile seizures in their index probands (1/1) (F6). It remains unresolved whether seizures represent an infrequent core feature or an incidental finding (F6).
2. **Facial Dysmorphism Gestalt:** While Ghaffar et al. (2024) and Kakar et al. (2025) note variable craniofacial dysmorphic traits (e.g., prominent forehead, dysplastic ears, thin upper lip), clinical curation frameworks (such as PanelApp) have questioned whether these findings constitute a distinct, clinically recognizable facial gestalt or non-specific minor anomalies common to generalized microcephalic syndromes (F4, F6, F7).
3. **Mechanisms of Speech Failure (Central Apraxia vs. Vocal Cord Dysfunction):** Umair et al. (2025) evaluated a patient harboring a homozygous missense variant (p.Ser1144Pro) and reported flexible laryngoscopy and electromyography findings demonstrating vocal cord pathology, suggesting speech failure might involve peripheral neuromotor or laryngeal dysfunction (F14). However, Kakar et al. (2025) phenotyped five patients with biallelic truncating variants who exhibited profound speech absence without vocal cord paresis or laryngeal anomalies, aligning with central neurodevelopmental expressive failure (F14).
4. **Spectrum of Speech Impairment:** Whereas Ghaffar et al. (2024) emphasize near-complete absence of spoken language, Umair et al. (2025) noted non-fluent, ambiguous speech in their proband, contesting whether the condition uniformly produces complete congenital aphonia (F9, F12, F15).
5. **Microcephaly Invariance Across Zygosity:** While Ghaffar et al. (2024) observed microcephaly in 11/11 individuals regardless of variant type, whether microcephaly is universally present across all potential hypomorphic or monoallelic variants remains contested in expanded registry submissions (F8, F15).

## Synthesis of Cross-Cutting Patterns and Clinical Decision-Making

Clinicians evaluating candidate patients should synthesize the following principles:

- **Ascertainment Bias and Panel Selection:** Because foundational cohorts were recruited through consanguineous pedigrees presenting with profound disease (F1, F2), penetrance figures for microcephaly and absent speech are artificially near-complete (F4). When sequencing broader cohorts, *NAV3* should be prioritized in targeted syndromic intellectual disability panels featuring microcephaly and absent speech, rather than unselected non-syndromic autism panels where diagnostic yields are negligible (F3).
- **Diagnostic Exclusion Tree:** Clinicians should use reverse exclusionary screening: the presence of progressive neurodegeneration, classic brain malformations (lissencephaly/pachygyria), or early refractory epileptic encephalopathy rules out *NAV3* disorder with high confidence (F5).
- **Multidisciplinary Speech Assessment:** Given reports of laryngeal electrophysiological abnormalities alongside central speech deficits, comprehensive phenotyping of speechless patients with *NAV3* variants should include both formal cognitive/linguistic evaluations and laryngoscopic airway examinations (F12, F14).
