# Clinical Identification of KDM2B-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorder (OMIM #621474)

## Executive Summary: Clinical Identification of KDM2B-Related Disorder (OMIM #621474)

Neurodevelopmental disorder with congenital cardiac defects and variable renal and ocular abnormalities (NEDCRO; OMIM #621474) is an autosomal dominant condition caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants in the *KDM2B* gene (chromosome 12q24.31) [F4, F6]. Identifying an affected individual purely from clinical examination poses a significant diagnostic challenge because its core triad—developmental delay, structural heart defects, and ocular or nephrourological malformations—substantially overlaps with classical chromatinopathies such as CHARGE syndrome, Kabuki syndrome, and Ritscher-Schinzel syndrome [F3, F11, F14]. 

Bedside differentiation requires an appreciation of domain-specific penetrance: individuals harboring missense variants within the specialized CxxC zinc-finger DNA-binding domain manifest a highly penetrant syndromic presentation characterized by developmental delay (up to 100%), high rates of congenital heart disease (74% to 78%), and variable ophthalmological and renal findings (25% to 50%) [F2, F4, F8, F9]. In contrast, individuals with non-CxxC truncating loss-of-function (pLOF) variants or catalytic JmjC domain variants display incomplete penetrance (<50%) for the visceral organ malformation triad, frequently presenting as non-syndromic neurodevelopmental impairment [F5, F6]. Furthermore, the absence of severe limb reduction defects, ectrodactyly, and coarse facial storage features serves as a vital clinical exclusionary boundary [F1].

## Most Discriminating Features and Differential Diagnosis

### Confused Clinical Conditions
The phenotypic constellation of neurodevelopmental delay, congenital heart disease, ocular coloboma, and structural renal defects in *KDM2B* variants strongly mimics several well-known syndromic conditions [F3, F11, F14]:
1. **CHARGE Syndrome (OMIM #214800; *CHD7*)**: Characterized by coloboma, heart defects, choanal atresia, growth retardation, genital anomalies, and ear abnormalities [F3, F14].
2. **Kabuki Syndrome (OMIM #147920 / #300867; *KMT2D*, *KDM6A*)**: Characterized by developmental delay, cardiac anomalies, persistent fetal finger pads, renal defects, and distinctive facial gestalt [F11, F14].
3. **Ritscher-Schinzel Syndrome (Cranio-Cerebello-Cardiac Syndrome; *WASHC5*, *CCDC22*)**: Presents with cerebellar malformations, congenital heart disease, and cranial dysmorphism [F11].

### Discriminating Features and Diagnostic Confidence Assessment
- **Cardiovascular Malformations in CxxC Variants**: The presence of congenital heart defects (atrial septal defect, ventricular septal defect, or valvular anomalies) in approximately 74%–78% (14/19 to 15/19; 14/18) of individuals with CxxC zinc finger missense variants is a central identifying pillar [F2, F8, F10]. While common in CHARGE and Kabuki syndromes, its co-occurrence with subtle rather than classical dysmorphic facial features provides an important discriminator [F14, F17].
- **High Frequency of Refractive and Functional Eye Anomalies vs Structural Coloboma**: While structural ocular coloboma occurs in an intermediate subset (30%–50%) across pooled cohorts [F9], severe ocular dysgenesis (microphthalmia, anophthalmia, coloboma) is not universally present and was found in 0/3 consecutive CxxC patients in a detailed clinical series [F18]. Instead, refractive errors (such as myopic astigmatism) were documented in 3/3 individuals [F15, F18]. In contrast, ocular coloboma is present in ~72% (13/18) of CHARGE syndrome cohorts [F3]. The presence of bilateral myopic astigmatism with preserved ocular integrity leans away from classical CHARGE syndrome [F3, F18].
- **Distinct Facial Gestalt via Computational Photogrammetry**: Although bedside human dysmorphology assessment historically labeled *KDM2B* facial features as subtle or non-pathognomonic, computational facial image analysis (GestaltMatcher) successfully discriminates *KDM2B* CxxC-domain individuals from other syndromic entities [F14, F17].
- **Confidence Level**: **Moderate on purely physical grounds; High when augmented with molecular/epigenetic biomarkers**. Differentiating *KDM2B*-related disorder from CHARGE or Kabuki syndrome purely by physical examination carries only moderate confidence because of phenotypic convergence across chromatinopathies [F14, F17]. High diagnostic confidence requires confirmation via molecular sequencing or peripheral blood DNA methylation episignature profiling [F11, F17].

## Systematically Assessed Negative Findings (Exclusion Criteria)

To exclude a diagnosis of *KDM2B*-related disorder when assessing a patient with overlapping congenital anomalies, clinicians should evaluate several pertinent negative findings:

1. **Absence of Severe Limb Reduction Defects and Ectrodactyly**: Severe limb reduction abnormalities, split-hand/split-foot malformations (ectrodactyly), and phocomelia have been evaluated and consistently found absent across all clinical cohorts (Van Jaarsveld et al., 2023; van Oirsouw et al., 2025; Gomes et al., 2026) [F1]. Their presence effectively excludes *KDM2B*-related disorder [F1].
2. **Absence of Coarse Facial Features**: Coarse facial features characteristic of lysosomal storage disorders or certain severe metabolic chromatin disorders are consistently absent in *KDM2B* probands, who exhibit subtle facial dysmorphisms [F1, F14].
3. **Critical Caveat Regarding Ear Anomalies and Choanal Atresia**: In CHARGE syndrome, semicircular canal hypoplasia/aplasia is present in 100% (18/18) of imaged patients and choanal atresia in 28% (5/18) [F3]. However, the apparent absence of choanal atresia and semicircular canal hypoplasia **cannot** currently serve as an exclusion criterion in favor of *KDM2B* [F3]. Published *KDM2B* cohorts have not systematically documented high-resolution temporal bone computed tomography (CT) or specialized otolaryngologic examinations [F3, F14]. Thus, true anatomic sparing of the inner ear remains unverified in published literature [F3].

## Phenotypic Stratification: Variant Type, Protein Domain, Age, and Sex

### Variant Type and Domain-Specific Penetrance
The phenotypic landscape of *KDM2B* is heavily determined by the structural domain targeted by the variant [F5, F8, F15]:
- **CxxC Zinc-Finger Domain Missense Variants (amino acids ~600–650)**:
  - Demonstrate full penetrance for neurodevelopmental delay / intellectual disability [F6].
  - Display high cardiac penetrance: congenital heart defects occur in 74%–78% (14/19 to 15/19 in van Oirsouw et al., 2025; 14/18 in van Oirsouw et al., 2026; 2/3 in Gomes et al., 2026) [F2, F8].
  - Frequently manifest the complete syndromic triad of neurodevelopmental, cardiac, and renal/ocular anomalies [F5, F8].
  - Mechanistically, CxxC variants preserve stable protein expression while impairing DNA-binding capacity at non-methylated CpG islands, causing a dominant-negative or neomorphic disruption of Polycomb Repressive Complex 1.1 recruitment [F15].
- **Non-CxxC Protein Truncating / Loss-of-Function (pLOF) Variants**:
  - Truncating variants (nonsense, frameshift, canonical splice site) span the *KDM2B* locus and result in haploinsufficiency [F15].
  - Exhibit incomplete penetrance (<50%) for the multi-organ malformation triad, commonly presenting as isolated or non-syndromic neurodevelopmental delay [F5].
  - CHD occurs in an intermediate subset (60%, 3/5) [F8].
- **Catalytic JmjC Domain Missense Variants**:
  - Exhibit incomplete penetrance, variable expressivity, and non-penetrant carriers [F5, F6]. Cardiac penetrance is markedly reduced compared to CxxC variants [F8, F12].
- **Contiguous 12q24.31 Microdeletions**:
  - Implicate *KDM2B* alongside flanking genes (including *SETD1B*) [F6, F7].
  - Cardiac malformations occur at a significantly lower frequency (25%, 3/12) than in CxxC single-nucleotide variants [F8, F10].

### Age and Sex Stratification
- **Age**: In initial delineations, neurodevelopmental delay and intellectual disability were documented in individuals evaluated after 2 years of age [F4, F12]. However, the apparent 100% obligate cognitive penetrance in discovery cohorts is heavily influenced by ascertainment bias toward pediatric probands referred for unexplained syndromic developmental delays [F7]. Broader testing has identified non-penetrant adult carriers among non-CxxC variants [F6].
- **Sex**: No significant sex-specific divergence in phenotypic spectrum or penetrance has been demonstrated; expressivity is dictated by domain location rather than patient sex [F5, F6, F8].

## Evaluation of Contested Hypotheses and Literature Contradictions

Several hypotheses in the literature have been contested or refuted upon closer examination of expanded cohorts:

### 1. Refuted: >75% Penetrance of Severe Structural Eye Malformations in CxxC Variants
- **Contested Claim**: Missense variants in the CxxC domain cause severe structural ocular malformations (coloboma, microphthalmia, anophthalmia) in >75% of patients [F16, F18].
- **Evidence & Resolution**: Refuted [F18]. In an unselected CxxC series, Gomes et al. (2026) reported 0/3 (0%) individuals with severe structural eye malformations, whereas all 3 exhibited refractive errors (myopic astigmatism) [F18]. Studies claiming a very high structural eye defect frequency (e.g., Ceroni et al., 2026) ascertained 2 *KDM2B* CxxC cases by screening a cohort of 706 families pre-selected specifically for developmental eye anomalies, introducing profound ascertainment bias [F5, F9, F18]. Mouse models of general loss-of-function also develop coloboma (~40%) and microphthalmia, demonstrating that severe structural eye defects are not unique to CxxC missense variants [F16].

### 2. Contested: Obligate 100% Penetrance of Neurodevelopmental Impairment
- **Contested Claim**: Intellectual disability and developmental delay are obligate (100%) across all individuals with *KDM2B* pathogenic variants [F4, F10, F12].
- **Evidence & Resolution**: Contested [F10, F12]. While initial discovery cohorts reported 21/21 (100%) affected individuals (van Jaarsveld et al., 2023) [F4], aggregated data from 68 patients (van Oirsouw et al., 2026) reveal that 100% penetrance is confined to CxxC domain variants [F6]. Non-CxxC pLOF and JmjC domain variants display incomplete penetrance with documented asymptomatic or non-penetrant carriers [F5, F6, F7].

### 3. Contested: Structural Renal Anomalies Occur in 33% to >45% of CxxC Cases
- **Contested Claim**: Structural nephrourological anomalies occur in at least 33% (1/3) to 45% of CxxC variant carriers [F13].
- **Evidence & Resolution**: Contested [F13]. While Gomes et al. observed 1/3 (33.3%), systematic evaluation of the larger van Oirsouw et al. CxxC cohort (n=19) revealed unilateral renal agenesis in 5/19 patients (26.3%), demonstrating that the true lower bound of structural renal malformations falls below 33% [F13].

### 4. Contested: Absence of a Discrete Facial Gestalt Precludes Clinical Separation
- **Contested Claim**: A clinical diagnosis cannot be suspected from facial features because *KDM2B* lacks a discrete facial gestalt [F14, F17].
- **Evidence & Resolution**: Contested [F17]. While subjective bedside assessment finds the facial dysmorphisms subtle, automated deep-learning facial image analysis (GestaltMatcher) demonstrated that individuals with CxxC domain variants share a recognizable facial gestalt that objectively separates them from healthy controls and overlapping syndromes [F17].

### 5. Contested: Uniform Episignature Diagnostic Classifier (>95% Universal Sensitivity)
- **Contested Claim**: A single peripheral blood DNA methylation episignature distinguishes all *KDM2B* variants with >95% sensitivity and specificity [F11].
- **Evidence & Resolution**: Contested [F11]. Epigenetic profiling has revealed divergence into two distinct sub-episignatures (CxxC zinc finger missense variants vs loss-of-function/haploinsufficiency variants) [F11]. Furthermore, partial signature overlaps with other chromatinopathies and lower sensitivity in hypomorphic or mosaic cases complicate the assertion of a single, uniform >95% clinical episignature classifier [F11].

## Cross-Cutting Synthesis and Clinical Diagnostic Strategy

### Bedside Diagnostic Synthesis
To pick an affected individual out of a cohort of patients with overlapping neurodevelopmental, cardiac, and ocular anomalies, clinicians should apply a multi-tiered decision framework:

1. **Screen for Core Inclusions and Exclusions**:
   - **Inclusion**: Developmental delay paired with congenital heart defects (particularly septal defects; present in ~78% of CxxC variants) and refractive visual errors (e.g., myopic astigmatism) or coloboma [F2, F8, F9, F18].
   - **Exclusion**: Severe limb reduction defects (ectrodactyly) or coarse storage-like facies strongly argue against *KDM2B* [F1].
2. **Look Beyond Severe Coloboma**:
   - Do not rely solely on major structural eye defects to suspect *KDM2B*. Mandate comprehensive cycloplegic refraction; non-structural refractive errors are frequent endophenotypes [F9, F18].
3. **Recognize Domain-Driven Phenotypic Divergence**:
   - If a patient has severe multisystem involvement (cardiac + renal + ocular + developmental delay), suspect a variant in the CxxC zinc finger domain (amino acids ~600–650) [F2, F5, F8].
   - If a family exhibits non-syndromic neurodevelopmental delay with incomplete penetrance, evaluate for non-CxxC truncating or JmjC variants [F5, F6].
4. **Molecular and Computational Verification**:
   - Because subjective facial features are subtle and clinical mimicry with CHARGE/Kabuki syndromes is extensive [F3, F14, F17], definitive diagnosis requires gene sequencing (identifying *KDM2B* variants) supported by computer-aided facial gestalt analysis (GestaltMatcher) or domain-specific peripheral blood DNA methylation episignature testing [F11, F17].
