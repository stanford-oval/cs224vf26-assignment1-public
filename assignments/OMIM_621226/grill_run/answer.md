# Clinical Identification of Spastic Ataxia 11 (TUBA4A / SPAX11) by Phenotype

## Executive Summary & Clinical Diagnostic Overview
Spastic ataxia 11, autosomal dominant (SPAX11; OMIM #621226) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder caused by monoallelic pathogenic variants in *TUBA4A*, which encodes an alpha-tubulin subunit (F1, F3). Clinically, SPAX11 occupies a transitional space between dual-tract spastic ataxias, pure hereditary spastic paraplegias (HSPs), and tubulinopathy syndromes (F1, F5). Identifying an affected individual from their phenotype alone requires recognizing a core triad of lower extremity spasticity, progressive cerebellar gait ataxia, and high-penetrance oculomotor abnormalities (saccadic smooth pursuit, gaze-evoked nystagmus), in the absence of severe early cognitive decline, optic atrophy, or isolated lower motor neuron degeneration (F1, F2, F6, F9).

## Phenotypic Profile and Cohort-Level Frequencies
The primary human evidence base for SPAX11 derives from two major cohort series: the discovery study of the recurrent p.Glu415Lys variant by Torella et al. (2023) and the series of de novo and inherited monoallelic *TUBA4A* variants reported by Benkirane et al. (2024) (F1, F4, F8, F9).

## Discriminating Features and Differential Diagnosis
To pick an individual with SPAX11 out of a group of patients with overlapping motor symptoms, clinicians must evaluate the specific combination of dual-tract signs and oculomotor markers against three main clinical differential categories (F1, F6):

### 1. Distinction from Pure Hereditary Spastic Paraplegias (HSPs, e.g., SPG4 / SPAST)
- **Discriminating Findings**: The presence of early broken smooth pursuit, gaze-evoked nystagmus, appendicular ataxia, and cerebellar volume loss readily discriminates SPAX11 from pure HSPs (F1, F6).
- **Confidence**: **High**. Pure HSPs typically manifest isolated progressive lower limb spasticity and corticospinal signs without prominent early oculomotor disruption or cerebellar ataxia (F1, F6).

### 2. Distinction from SPG7 / Paraplegin-Related Disorders
- **Overlapping Features**: SPG7 frequently produces a combined spastic ataxia syndrome with cerebellar atrophy and pyramidal tract involvement (F1).
- **Discriminating Findings**: Loss of paraplegin in SPG7 often exhibits prominent optic nerve pallor, progressive external ophthalmoplegia, or isolated late spasticity (Coarelli et al., 2019; F1). However, the central spastic-ataxic syndrome of SPAX11 lacks distinguishing physical signs from complex SPG7 presentations.
- **Confidence**: **Low**. Phenotypic features alone cannot reliably separate SPAX11 from SPG7 without targeted genetic analysis (F1).

### 3. Distinction from Autosomal Dominant Spinocerebellar Ataxias (SCA1, SCA2, SCA3, SCA28 / AFG3L2) and TUBB4A Disorders
- **Overlapping Features**: AFG3L2-associated SCA28/SPAX5 and TUBB4A-related disorders feature cerebellar ataxia combined with lower extremity hyperreflexia and nystagmus (F1, F6). 
- **Discriminating Findings**: The clinical presentation of horizontal nystagmus, broken pursuit, and lower-limb spasticity in SPAX11 phenocopies AFG3L2 (mean onset ~26.5 years) and intermediate TUBB4A presentations (Coarelli et al., 2025; Gavazzi et al., 2026; F6).
- **Confidence**: **Low**. Reliable differentiation from complex SCAs and *TUBB4A* tubulinopathies cannot be achieved purely on clinical examination (F1, F6).

## Exclusionary Diagnostic Boundaries: Findings Found Absent
Specific negative findings have been consistently documented across reported human series (Torella et al., 2023; Benkirane et al., 2024) and animal models (Hines et al., 2026; Johari et al., 2026). The presence of any of the following features strongly argues **against** SPAX11 (F2):

1. **Severe Early-Onset Dementia or Frontotemporal Decline**: Unlike multisystem tauopathies or C9orf72/FTD syndromes, cognitive preservation is standard in early to mid-stage SPAX11 (F2).
2. **Prominent Optic Atrophy, Retinal Dystrophy, or Pigmentary Retinopathy**: Unlike SCA7 (maculopathy) or mitochondrial ataxias, retinal integrity is preserved in SPAX11 (F2).
3. **Isolated Lower Motor Neuron Denervation without Ataxia**: Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis type 22 (ALS22; caused by distinct destabilizing *TUBA4A* variants) presents with classic upper and lower motor neuron loss without cerebellar ataxia (F2, F3). Isolated motor neuron disease without cerebellar involvement points to ALS22 rather than SPAX11 (F2).
4. **Early Childhood-Onset Severe Demyelinating Neuropathy**: Peripheral involvement in SPAX11 is strictly mild, axonal, sensorimotor, and emerges in adulthood rather than early childhood (F10).

## Genotype-Phenotype Correlations: Variant Type, Position, Age, and Sex
*TUBA4A* displays profound pleiotropy across distinct structural domains (F3, F5):

- **Variant Position and Mutation Mechanism**: Destabilizing or dominant-negative variants targeting specific monomer domains lead to upper and lower motor neuron disease (ALS22), whereas missense substitutions perturbing microtubule dynamics result in SPAX11 (F3). Specific core variants also bridge the spectrum into skeletal myo-tubulinopathies presenting with myofibrillar disruption, neuromuscular junction transmission failure (repetitive nerve stimulation decrements of -14% to -48%), or adult-onset myopathy (Hines et al., 2026; Johari et al., 2026; Milone et al., 2026; F5).
- **De Novo vs. Inherited Status**: In the Benkirane et al. (2024) series of 12 spastic ataxia probands (identified from a screening cohort of 448 ataxia probands; odds ratio 57.08 in the 100,000 Genomes Project inherited ataxia cohort), 5 confirmed de novo cases and multiple inherited kindreds were reported (F1, F3, F4, F7). Both de novo and inherited monoallelic variants present with the same core spastic-ataxic phenotype, without systematic divergence in core presentation (F3, F15).
- **Age and Sex**: SPAX11 exhibits variable age of onset, presenting either in the first two decades of life or delayed into mid-to-late adulthood, with incomplete penetrance documented within extended families (e.g., Torella et al., 2023; F3, F4, F8). There is no reported clinical divergence or skew by biological sex (F3, F4).

## Contested and Refuted Hypotheses in the Literature
Several hypotheses regarding neuroimaging penetrance and progression dynamics have been scrutinized and deprioritised:

### 1. Obligate Cerebellar Atrophy Penetrance (Contested)
- **Prior Hypothesis**: Cerebellar atrophy on MRI is an obligate feature present in 80–100% of affected adults with SPAX11 (F11).
- **Literature Contradiction**: While Benkirane et al. (2024) reported cerebellar atrophy in 7 of 11 evaluated patients, Torella et al. (2023) demonstrated that across 12 affected individuals harboring the recurrent p.Glu415Lys mutation, brain MRI revealed slight or completely absent cerebellar atrophy, with only questionable upper vermian volume loss in some individuals (F4, F11, F14, F16). Therefore, macrostructural cerebellar atrophy is not an obligate diagnostic criterion (F4, F11).

### 2. Accelerated Atrophy Driven Strictly by De Novo Catalytic-Core Variants (Contested)
- **Prior Hypothesis**: Catalytic-core de novo variants trigger rapid pancerebellar atrophy within <5 years of onset compared to indolent C-terminal variants (F12).
- **Reason for Deprioritisation**: Johari et al. (2026) identified de novo core mutations that present with mild cerebellar atrophy or predominant skeletal myo-tubulinopathy, and no standardized semi-quantitative MRI scoring cohorts exist comparing duration-matched groups (F12).

### 3. Dysarthria Correlation with >10-Year Disease Duration (Refuted)
- **Prior Hypothesis**: Cerebellar dysarthria emerges strictly after >10 years of clinical motor disease (F13).
- **Refutation**: In the kindreds described by Torella et al. (2023), dysarthria is present in fewer than half (<50%) of affected individuals and does not follow a strict 10-year latency threshold (F8, F9, F13).

### 4. Duration-Dependent Linear Progression and Vermis-to-Hemisphere Latency on MRI (Refuted)
- **Prior Hypotheses**: Cerebellar atrophy severity follows a linear duration model (absent <5 years, severe after 10–15 years) (F14), and vermian atrophy precedes hemispheric atrophy by a stereotyped 5-year latency (F16).
- **Refutation**: Neither human pedigree series (Torella et al., 2023; Benkirane et al., 2024) nor animal models (Hines et al., 2026) show a linear time-locked threshold or stereotyped 5-year anatomical sequence; individuals with long-standing motor ataxia can retain preserved cerebellar volumes (F4, F14, F16).

### 5. Increased Dysarthria in De Novo Variants Compared to Inherited Variants (Refuted)
- **Prior Hypothesis**: De novo variants cause a significantly higher incidence of early-onset dysarthria than inherited alleles (e.g., p.Glu415Lys) (F15).
- **Refutation**: Comparison of de novo probands in Benkirane et al. (2024) and familial cases in Torella et al. (2023) demonstrates shared phenotypic features without statistically significant divergence in dysarthria onset or prevalence (F15).

## Cross-Cutting Diagnostic Patterns and Synthesis
1. **Decoupling of Fine-Motor Ocular Pathways from Bulbar and Macroscopic Parenchymal Loss**: In *TUBA4A*-mediated tubulinopathies, vestibulocerebellar and oculomotor relay pathways are exquisitely sensitive to microtubule destabilization, resulting in near-complete penetrance of broken smooth pursuit and nystagmus (>85%) before significant cerebellar volume loss or dysarthria emerges (F4, F7, F8, F9). Purkinje neuron synaptic dysfunction precedes macroscopic tissue loss (F4, F7).
2. **Asymmetric Discriminative Power**: Phenotypic evaluation easily separates SPAX11 from pure single-tract syndromes like pure HSPs, but dual-tract complex spastic ataxias (SPG7, SCA28/AFG3L2, TUBB4A) converge on the same corticospinal and spinocerebellar degeneration patterns (F1, F6). Definitive confirmation in this group requires genomic sequencing (F1, F6).
3. **Exclusionary Clinical Boundaries**: When evaluating an unclassified spastic-ataxic patient, the presence of early dementia, retinopathy, or childhood demyelinating neuropathy effectively excludes SPAX11, triaging workups toward alternative genetic panels (F2, F10).
