# Clinical Identification and Phenotypic Profile of SPTAN1-Associated Distal Hereditary Motor Neuronopathy 11 (dHMN11 / OMIM #620528)

## Clinical Presentation and Phenotypic Profile of SPTAN1-Associated dHMN11

Autosomal dominant distal hereditary motor neuronopathy 11 (dHMN11; OMIM #620528) is caused by heterozygous loss-of-function (LoF) variants in *SPTAN1*, which encodes alpha-II spectrin (D Beijer et al., 2019; OMIM, 2021, PMID:33578420) [F1]. Clinically, dHMN11 is characterized by a length-dependent, lower-limb-predominant distal motor deficit presenting with progressive bilateral distal muscle weakness and atrophy, secondary foot deformities, and depressed or absent ankle reflexes (MF Dohrn et al., 2020; HL Dong et al., 2021) [F1, F8]. 

Recent clinical, electrophysiological, and pathological delineations demonstrate that heterozygous *SPTAN1* haploinsufficiency does not invariably produce a strictly isolated axonopathy of the lower motor neuron; rather, it often manifests as a pleiotropic neuromuscular condition bridging distal motor neuronopathy and early-onset distal myopathy (J De Winter et al., 2025; L Van de Vondel et al., 2026, DOI: 10.1038/s41431-025-01938-2; C Laurini et al., 2026) [F4, F6]. Identifying an affected individual among patients presenting with generic distal lower-limb weakness requires rigorous integration of clinical findings, neurophysiological parameters, deep tendon reflex distribution, and pertinent negative findings [F2, F3, F5, F8].

## Discriminating Clinical and Neurophysiological Features Versus Diagnostic Mimics

When evaluating a patient with distal lower limb amyotrophy and foot deformities, dHMN11 must be distinguished from several common neurogenetic mimics (C Laurini et al., 2026; MF Dohrn et al., 2020) [F3, F4]:

### 1. Charcot-Marie-Tooth Disease Type 2 (Axonal CMT2)
* **Overlap:** Slowly progressive bilateral peroneal weakness, foot drop, distal muscle wasting, pes cavus, and depressed or absent ankle jerks (HL Dong et al., 2021) [F3, F8, F11].
* **Discriminating Features:** Absolute preservation of sensory neurophysiology. In dHMN11, sensory nerve action potential (SNAP) amplitudes and sensory conduction velocities across sural, superficial peroneal, and median/ulnar nerves remain completely normal (absence of sensory neuropathy; HP:0000107 / HP:0003474), while motor nerve conduction studies demonstrate reduced compound muscle action potentials (CMAPs; HP:0003444) without significant demyelinating conduction slowing (D Beijer et al., 2019; HL Dong et al., 2021) [F3, F5]. CMT2 routinely presents with reduced or unrecordable SNAPs and distal sensory deficits (vibration, light touch, pinprick) [F5].
* **Diagnostic Confidence:** **Very High**. Preserved SNAPs paired with pure motor axonal impairment is the established diagnostic requirement defining dHMN and separating it from CMT2 (D Beijer et al., 2019; MF Dohrn et al., 2020) [F3, F5].

### 2. Distal Hereditary Motor Neuropathies with Upper Limb Predominance (dHMN-V / GARS1 / BSCL2)
* **Overlap:** Distal motor weakness without sensory involvement.
* **Discriminating Features:** Classical dHMN11 presents predominantly with early lower limb involvement (tibialis anterior, peronei, extensor digitorum brevis) and delayed motor milestones, whereas dHMN type V initiates primarily in the thenar and first dorsal interosseous muscles of the hands (D Beijer et al., 2019; C Wu, 2022) [F8, F10].
* **Diagnostic Confidence:** **Moderate to High** [F8, F10].

### 3. Isolated Distal Myopathies (e.g., Welander, Udd, Nonaka, or Miyoshi Myopathy)
* **Overlap:** Distal lower-limb weakness, foot drop, and selective fatty infiltration on muscle MRI [F4, F9, F11].
* **Discriminating Features:** Neurogenic EMG abnormalities (fibrillation potentials, positive sharp waves, high-amplitude long-duration polyphasic motor unit action potentials) coexist with myopathic changes (short-duration, low-amplitude motor units) in dHMN11/SPTAN1 disease, whereas classic distal myopathies show normal neurography and lack chronic neurogenic denervation (J De Winter et al., 2024, DOI: 10.1101/2024.09.23.24313872; C Laurini et al., 2026) [F4]. Additionally, dHMN11 displays a dissociated reflex arc (ankle jerks lost, patellar jerks preserved or brisk), whereas myopathies typically spare or symmetrically dampen reflexes without an isolated distal reflex cutoff (HL Dong et al., 2021) [F8].
* **Diagnostic Confidence:** **Moderate** (as muscle biopsy in *SPTAN1* LoF carriers can reveal overt myopathic/dystrophic features in up to 70–100% of biopsied patients) (J De Winter et al., 2025; C Laurini et al., 2026) [F1, F4].

### 4. Severe SPTAN1-Associated Encephalopathies (DEE5 / EIEE5, OMIM #613477)
* **Overlap:** Mutations occurring in the same gene (*SPTAN1*).
* **Discriminating Features:** Total divergence of clinical syndrome. dHMN11 patients exhibit completely normal intellectual development, intact language, normal cerebral architecture on brain MRI, and an absolute absence of infantile epilepsy or epileptic spasms (D Beijer et al., 2019; MF Dohrn et al., 2020; D Owrang et al., 2025) [F1, F2].
* **Diagnostic Confidence:** **Definitive** [F2].

## Pertinent Negative Findings (Exclusionary Features)

In a clinical evaluation, the presence of any of the following features strongly argues **against** a diagnosis of dHMN11:

1. **Intractable Epilepsy or Infantile Seizures (HP:0001250):** Evaluated and confirmed absent in all 13 reported dHMN11 individuals across 4 families with nonsense variants, as well as in all 20 individuals evaluated across the broader *SPTAN1* LoF cohort (D Beijer et al., 2019; HL Dong et al., 2021; J De Winter et al., 2025) [F1, F2].
2. **Intellectual Disability or Global Developmental Delay (HP:0001249 / HP:0001263):** Affected individuals achieve normal intellectual milestones and display no cognitive impairment (HL Dong et al., 2021; MF Dohrn et al., 2020) [F2].
3. **Objective Clinically Prominent Sensory Loss (HP:0000107 / HP:0003474):** Testing of pinprick, temperature, vibration, and proprioception reveals normal sensory perception in classical dHMN11 kindreds. Documented stocking-glove sensory loss reclassifies the presentation as axonal CMT2 (D Beijer et al., 2020, DOI: 10.1093/brain/awaa311; C Laurini et al., 2026) [F3, F5].
4. **Abnormal Sensory Neurophysiology:** Preservation of SNAP amplitudes and conduction velocities is uniform; reduced or absent SNAPs exclude pure dHMN11 (D Beijer et al., 2019; HL Dong et al., 2021) [F3, F5].
5. **Diffuse Pan-Areflexia:** Unlike polyneuropathies producing generalized areflexia, dHMN11 spares or exaggerates proximal reflexes; loss of patellar reflexes alongside ankle jerks is uncharacteristic (HL Dong et al., 2021) [F8].

## Phenotypic Variations by Variant Type, Position, Age, and Sex

### 1. Variant Type and Functional Consequence
* **Loss-of-Function vs. Structural In-Frame Variants:** dHMN11 is caused exclusively by heterozygous loss-of-function variants (premature stop codons, frameshift indels, whole-gene microdeletions) leading to haploinsufficiency via nonsense-mediated decay (NMD) (D Beijer et al., 2019; search.clinicalgenome.org, 2019; OMIM, 2021) [F1, F4]. Conversely, *de novo* in-frame mutations, missense variants, or duplications clustering in the alpha/beta-spectrin dimerization domain (spectrin repeats 20–21 / EF-hand regions) exert a toxic dominant-negative effect that disrupts spectrin heterodimerization, triggering catastrophic early infantile epileptic encephalopathy 5 (DEE5) with progressive brain atrophy and hypomyelination (D Beijer et al., 2019; J Tohyama et al., 2015; S Syrbe et al., 2017) [F1, F15].
* **Upper Motor Neuron Phenotype from Specific Missense Sites:** Specific monoallelic missense variants outside the dimerization domain—such as the recurrent p.Arg19Trp substitution at the N-terminal interlinking interface—manifest as pure hereditary spastic paraplegia (SPG91) or cerebellar ataxia rather than dHMN11 (L Van de Vondel et al., 2022, DOI: 10.1002/mds.28959; 朱子豪 et al., 2026) [F1, F15].

### 2. Incomplete Penetrance
* Heterozygous *SPTAN1* LoF alleles display documented incomplete penetrance (D Beijer et al., 2019; L Van de Vondel et al., 2026, PMID:40999194) [F6, F7]. In the 9q34 microdeletion family described by Van de Vondel et al. (2026), 4 of 6 carriers were clinically affected, yielding an estimated clinical penetrance of **66.7%** (non-penetrance rate of 33.3%) [F6, F7]. Similarly, Beijer et al. (2019) noted asymptomatic adult obligate carriers in 2 of 3 evaluated families [F7].

### 3. Age of Onset
* Initial onset ranges from early childhood (delayed walking at ~20 months) to juvenile and young-adult onset (second to fourth decade) with insidious foot drop or gait instability (D Beijer et al., 2019; HL Dong et al., 2021; J De Winter et al., 2025) [F1, F11].

### 4. Sex Correlations
* No significant sex predilection or phenotypic skewing by sex has been identified; autosomal dominant inheritance affects males and females with equal severity across reported cohorts (D Beijer et al., 2019; C Wu, 2022) [F1, F16].

## Contested and Contradictory Literature Findings

Several hypotheses and phenotypic characterizations regarding *SPTAN1*-related motor disease have been debated or contradicted across studies:

### 1. Pure Distal Motor Neuropathy vs. Mixed Neuro-Myopathic Entity
* **Contradiction:** Earlier studies classified *SPTAN1* LoF mutations strictly as causing distal hereditary motor neuropathy type 11 (dHMN11), assuming a pure lower motor neuron axonopathy (D Beijer et al., 2019; OMIM, 2021) [F1, F13].
* **Resolution / Evidence:** Subsequent multicenter analyses demonstrated that heterozygous LoF variants cause concurrent primary myopathy, showing short-duration MUPs on electromyography, necrotic/regenerating fibers and dystrophic disruption on muscle biopsy (7/10 to 7/7 patients), and marked fatty infiltration on muscle MRI (10/10 patients) (J De Winter et al., 2025; L Van de Vondel et al., 2026; C Laurini et al., 2026) [F1, F4, F10]. dHMN11 is now increasingly recognized as part of a continuous neuro-myopathic disease spectrum [F4, F6].

### 2. Specificity of Lower Limb Muscle MRI Patterns
* **Contested Hypothesis:** Payá et al. (2026, DOI: 10.1111/ene.70456) proposed that a reticular pattern of fat infiltration progressing to "muscle islands" (present in 54.8% of dHMN vs. 32% of distal myopathies) reliably differentiates dHMN from primary distal myopathies [F9].
* **Resolution / Evidence:** This distinction remains contested because broader radiological series (Y Tai et al., 2025, DOI: 10.1148/rg.240190) show that reticulated/honeycomb patterns also appear across distinct myopathic disorders. Moreover, no prospective imaging cohort isolating *SPTAN1* dHMN11 from other dHMN etiologies (e.g., *HSPB1*, *BICD2*) has been conducted [F9].

### 3. Strict Dichotomy of Loss-of-Function (Neuromuscular) vs. Missense (Central Encephalopathy)
* **Contested Hypothesis:** Early models posited that all *SPTAN1* LoF variants cause late-onset neuromuscular disease, whereas all missense/in-frame mutations cause early infantile epileptic encephalopathy (DEE5) [F12, F15].
* **Resolution / Evidence:** This strict dichotomy is refuted: Lu et al. (2025, DOI: 10.1111/cge.14732) reported heterozygous LoF alleles in patients presenting with ataxia and intellectual disability, while Van de Vondel et al. (2022) identified dominant missense variants (p.Arg19Trp) causing spastic paraplegia without cognitive decline, indicating that phenotype depends on specific protein domain vulnerability as well as mutational mechanism [F15].

### 4. Postural Hand Tremor as a Phenotypic Marker
* **Refuted Finding:** It had been proposed that postural hand tremor occurs in fewer than 25% of dHMN11 patients and only reflects secondary intrinsic hand muscle denervation [F16].
* **Resolution / Evidence:** Systemic cohort analysis shows that postural tremor is reported sporadically across general dHMN populations without specific documentation, statistical validation, or mechanistic association in *SPTAN1* cohorts (C Wu, 2022; M Wilson et al., 2025, DOI: 10.1089/cap.2024.0100) [F16].

## Diagnostic Synthesis and Mechanistic Patterns

To identify an affected individual with dHMN11 from among a group of patients with overlapping distal neuromuscular weakness, clinicians can apply three cross-cutting physiological criteria:

1. **Sensory-Sparing Neurophysiology (Absolute Filter):** Normal sensory nerve conduction velocities and normal SNAP amplitudes in the presence of reduced distal motor CMAPs definitively separates dHMN11 from axonal CMT2 (D Beijer et al., 2019; HL Dong et al., 2021) [F3, F5].
2. **Dissociated Craniocaudal Reflex Gradient:** Extinction of the Achilles tendon reflex (ankle areflexia) paired with preserved or brisk patellar reflexes (patellar hyperreflexia, +++) reflects length-dependent axonal degeneration that exhausts the longest lower motor neuron units (S1) while sparing L3–L4 spinal segments or unmasking subclinical corticospinal release (HL Dong et al., 2021; MF Dohrn et al., 2020) [F8]. This rules out generalized myopathic or peripheral pan-areflexia.
3. **Absence of Central Symptoms with Familial Incomplete Penetrance:** Normal cognition, normal cranial imaging, and absence of seizures rule out DEE5, while recognizing that approximately 20% to 35% of adult mutation carriers in the family will be clinically asymptomatic (L Van de Vondel et al., 2026; J De Winter et al., 2025) [F2, F7].
