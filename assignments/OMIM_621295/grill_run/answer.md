# Phenotypic Identification of Cerebral Arteriopathy, Autosomal Recessive, with Subcortical Infarcts and Leukoencephalopathy 1 (OMIM #621295)

## 1. Executive Summary and Diagnostic Definition

Cerebral arteriopathy, autosomal recessive, with subcortical infarcts and leukoencephalopathy 1 (OMIM #621295), historically termed CARASIL1 or recessive NOTCH3-related leukodystrophy/arteriopathy, is a severe hereditary cerebral small vessel disease caused by biallelic alterations in the *NOTCH3* gene (F1, F2). Historically confused with both autosomal dominant CADASIL (OMIM #125310) and *HTRA1*-related autosomal recessive CARASIL (OMIM #600142), phenotypic differentiation requires understanding a fundamental molecular bifurcation: the documented global biallelic *NOTCH3* population divides strictly into two distinct chronological and phenotypic syndromes rather than a clinical continuum (F1, F2).

1. **Biallelic Loss-of-Function (LoF) / Null Leukoencephalopathy**: Patients harboring biallelic nonsense, frameshift, splice-site, or null/cysteine-sparing mutations present in infancy or early childhood (9–14 months of age) with global developmental delay, early pyramidal signs, seizures, early-onset childhood ischemic strokes, and severe periatrial leukomalacia-like white matter volume loss mimicking periventricular leukomalacia (PVL) (F1, F4, F5).
2. **Biallelic Cysteine-Altering Missense CADASIL-Spectrum Arteriopathy**: Patients carrying biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) classic cysteine-altering missense variants in the epidermal growth factor-like repeat (EGFr) domains manifest in adulthood (3rd to 5th decades) with recurrent subcortical lacunar infarctions, cognitive decline, and confluent anterior temporal pole hyperintensities (F1, F4, F16).

Clinicians can reliably identify the recessive loss-of-function phenotype (OMIM #621295) by its infantile onset, unique periatrial white matter atrophy on MRI, and the systematic absence of extraneurological signs (alopecia, spondylosis) and early anterior temporal pole hyperintensity (F3, F5, F8).

## 4. Obligate Negative Findings: Features Ruling Out OMIM #621295

In genetic small vessel diseases, "negative discriminators"—features consistently evaluated and shown to be absent—provide critical diagnostic exclusion value (F3, F8).

1. **Absence of Extraneurological Ectodermal and Skeletal Signs**:
   - Premature alopecia (`HP:0002297`) has been systematically investigated and confirmed absent in 0/50 biallelic *NOTCH3* patients (F3). Its presence strongly indicates *HTRA1*-related CARASIL (F3).
   - Severe early-onset spondylosis deformans or severe lumbar disc degeneration (`HP:0003419`) is absent in all evaluated *NOTCH3* cases (0/50), arguing definitively against *HTRA1*-associated CARASIL (F3).
2. **Absence of Early Anterior Temporal Pole Hyperintensity**:
   - Sparing of the anterior temporal pole T2/FLAIR hyperintensity is a hallmark across pediatric and adolescent biallelic LoF cohorts (3/3 in Stellingwerff et al., 1/1 in Tasharrofi et al., and across 26 LoF cases) (F4, F8, F10).
   - The presence of prominent, selective anterior temporal pole subcortical hyperintensity in a young child strongly argues against biallelic LoF *NOTCH3* disease and suggests CADASIL-like cysteine-involving pathology or alternative leukodystrophies (F8, F9, F14).
3. **Absence of Obligate Cutaneous Signs (Livedo as a Non-Criterion)**:
   - While cutaneous livedo reticularis or racemosa (`HP:0000965`) occurs in certain systemic arteriopathies (e.g., *ADA2* deficiency), it has a sensitivity below 35% across biallelic *NOTCH3* LoF cases (absent in 2/2 siblings assessed by Al-Amrani) (F7). Therefore, the absence of cutaneous livedo cannot be used to rule out OMIM #621295 (F7).

## 5. Phenotypic Stratification by Variant Type, Position, Age, and Sex

The clinical presentation in biallelic *NOTCH3* arteriopathy is strongly governed by variant type, variant position, and age, but displays no evidence of sex predilection (F1, F2).

### Stratification by Variant Type (Loss-of-Function vs. Cysteine-Involving Missense)
The global documented cohort of 50 patients with biallelic *NOTCH3* mutations segregates into two non-overlapping clinical entities (F1, F2):
- **Biallelic Loss-of-Function Cohort (26/50 patients, 52%)**: Harbors homozygous or compound heterozygous nonsense, frameshift deletions (e.g., c.2984delC), or splice-site null mutations (F1, F2, F5). They exhibit early infantile/pediatric onset (9–14 months), developmental arrest, seizures, spasticity, childhood ischemic strokes, intracranial calcifications, and periatrial volume loss (F1, F5, F6).
- **Biallelic Cysteine-Altering Missense Cohort (24/50 patients, 48%)**: Harbors biallelic missense mutations altering cysteine residues within the EGFr domains (F1, F2, F4). These patients exhibit an adult-onset CADASIL phenotype presenting in the 3rd to 5th decades with lacunar strokes, subcortical dementia, and anterior temporal hyperintensities without early periatrial volume loss (F1, F2, F4).

### Stratification by Variant Position
- Biallelic null/LoF variants are distributed across the gene, including early exons (e.g., exon 1) as well as distal C-terminal and signaling regions (e.g., exons 32 and 33) (F4).
- In contrast, classic adult-onset CADASIL mutations are clustered in EGFr-encoding exons 2–24, where cysteine gain or loss drives granular osmiophilic material (GOM) deposition (F1, F4). In rare cases of homozygous cysteine-sparing variants in EGFr19 (e.g., c.3373G>A [p.Glu1125Lys]), distinct adult presentations featuring vestibular migraine, epilepsy, and high microbleed density have been described (F15).

### Stratification by Age and Sex
- **Age**: Age of onset is strictly bifurcated by variant class. Biallelic LoF presents consistently between 9 and 14 months of age (or under 2 years), progressing throughout childhood (F1, F2, F9). Cysteine-involving missense homozygotes or compound heterozygotes present in adulthood (mean age in 4th decade, e.g., 35 years) (F1, F2, F16).
- **Sex**: No sex differences in penetrance, severity, or lesion topography have been observed in biallelic *NOTCH3* cohorts; males and females are equally affected across reported sibships and case series (F1, F2, F3).

## 6. Literature Contradictions and Contested Findings

Several hypotheses and phenotypic assertions in the literature have been contested or contradicted by emerging case series and population-scale studies. These findings are detailed below along with the rationale for their deprioritization.

### Contradiction 1: Anterior Temporal Pole Infiltration vs. Strict Sparing
- **Contested Hypothesis [F10, F14, F17]**: Anterior temporal pole T2/FLAIR hyperintensity is strictly absent in biallelic LoF *NOTCH3* mutations across all disease stages.
- **Contradictory Reports**: Stellingwerff et al. (2022) (3/3 patients) and Tasharrofi et al. (2024) (1/1 patient) documented complete sparing of the anterior temporal pole in biallelic LoF cases, proposing it as an invariant negative discriminator (F8, F10, F17). Conversely, Ouskri et al. (2025) reported temporal white matter hyperintensities in a 12-year-old Moroccan child with a homozygous LoF frameshift variant (F10, F14, F17).
- **Resolution & Synthesis [F9]**: Apparent temporal lobe abnormalities in older LoF patients represent diffuse, end-stage parenchymal loss and generalized cerebral deafferentation rather than the focal, selective early anterior temporal pole subcortical white matter hyperintensities pathognomonic of CADASIL (F9).

### Contradiction 2: Phenotypic Penetrance in Heterozygous Carrier Relatives
- **Contested Hypothesis [F11, F12]**: Heterozygous carriers of *NOTCH3* LoF/null variants are entirely asymptomatic, lack vascular phenotypes, and do not suffer premature ischemic strokes.
- **Contradictory Reports**: Iruzubieta et al. (2024), Tasharrofi et al. (2024), and Stellingwerff et al. (2022) reported that monoallelic parents and relatives are asymptomatic or show only mild, age-related white matter changes (F11, F12). In direct contrast, Al-Amrani (2023) and Pippucci et al. (2015, DOI: 10.15252/emmm.201404399) reported premature strokes (at ages 28 and 39) in heterozygous null carriers, alongside vascular smooth muscle degeneration and decreased NOTCH3 immunostaining (F11). Furthermore, clinically ascertained *NOTCH3* LoF cohorts reveal stroke in 15% (11/69) and lacunes in 38% (12/32) of monoallelic carriers (Neurology, DOI: 10.1212/WNL.0000000000214021), and UK Biobank neuroimaging demonstrates microstructural white matter injury (PSMD Δ0.19 × 10⁻⁴, p = 0.017) in monoallelic LoF carriers (F12).
- **Resolution**: While *NOTCH3* LoF behaves as a severe recessive disease regarding infantile encephalopathy, monoallelic LoF is not completely non-penetrant and confers an elevated risk of late-onset cerebral small vessel disease and lacunar infarction (F11, F12).

### Contradiction 3: Seizure Penetrance in Pediatric Biallelic LoF
- **Contested Hypothesis [F13]**: Epileptic seizures and spastic pyramidal tract signs are penetrant in >75% of pediatric biallelic null cases.
- **Contradictory Reports**: Al-Amrani (2023) identified seizures in 2/2 siblings, and Ouskri et al. (2025) noted spastic tetraparesis in 1/1 patient (F13). However, Stellingwerff et al. (2022) demonstrated that spasticity and seizures are variable rather than universal across all pediatric early-onset cases, showing that an epidemiological threshold of >75% across all null cohorts remains unconfirmed (F13).

### Contradiction 4: Temporal Lesions in Homozygous Cysteine-Sparing Missense Variants
- **Contested Hypothesis [F15]**: Recessive homozygous cysteine-sparing variants uniformly present with confluent temporal pole hyperintensities.
- **Contradictory Reports**: Luo et al. (2026, DOI: 10.1007/s10048-026-00899-y) described confluent Fazekas 3 temporal hyperintensities and high microbleed density (2.15 vs. 0.4 counts/cm³, p = 0.008) in a family with an EGFr19 cysteine-sparing variant (F15). In contrast, systematic cohort reviews (Cao et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2006) demonstrate that cysteine-sparing *NOTCH3* variants show a significantly lower prevalence of anterior temporal pole hyperintensities compared to classic cysteine-altering CADASIL (p = 0.02) (F15).

## 7. Diagnostic Decision Algorithm and Clinical Synthesis

To identify a patient with *NOTCH3* biallelic loss-of-function arteriopathy (OMIM #621295) among patients presenting with early-onset small vessel disease, clinicians should follow a four-tier decision framework:

### Clinical Decision Rules
1. **Gating Neuroimaging Criterion**: The presence of symmetrical periatrial white matter volume loss mimicking periventricular leukomalacia (PVL) with deep microcalcifications in an infant or young child with motor arrest and stroke is the primary neuroimaging signature (F4, F5, F6).
2. **Exclusionary Criteria (Negative Gates)**:
   - Check scalp and spine: Presence of early diffuse alopecia or premature severe lumbar disc degeneration excludes *NOTCH3* and establishes *HTRA1*-related CARASIL (F3).
   - Check temporal poles on T2/FLAIR: Sparing of the anterior temporal poles strongly favors biallelic LoF *NOTCH3* over classic CADASIL, whereas confluent early temporal pole hyperintensity points to cysteine-altering *NOTCH3* variants (F4, F8).
3. **Non-Gating Cutaneous Signs**: Do not exclude OMIM #621295 if livedo reticularis/racemosa is absent, as cutaneous vascular penetrance is low (<35%) (F7).

Through this synthesis of chronological onset, lesion topography, and obligate negative signs, clinicians can accurately isolate patients with OMIM #621295 prior to genetic confirmation.
