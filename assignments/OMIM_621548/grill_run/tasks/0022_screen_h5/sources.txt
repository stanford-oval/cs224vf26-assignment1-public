Ophthalmoscopic examination features included macular edema, mild intraretinal pigment migration, and widespread atrophy in advanced disease.     abstract: <h4>Purpose</h4>To help differentiate CLN3 (Batten) disease, a devastating childhood metabolic disorder, from the similarly presenting early-onset Stargardt disease (STGD1). Early clinical identification of children with CLN3 disease is essential for adequate referral, counselling and rehabilitation.<h4>Methods</h4>Medical chart review of 38 children who were referred to a specialized ophthalmological centre because of rapid vision loss. The patients were subsequently diagnosed with either CLN3 disease (18 patients) or early-onset STGD1 (20 patients).<h4>Results</h4>Both children who were later diagnosed with CLN3 disease, as children who were later diagnosed with early-onset STGD1, initially presented with visual acuity (VA) loss due to macular dystrophy at 5-10 years of age. VA in CLN3 disease decreased significantly faster than in STGD1 (p = 0.01). Colour vision was often already severely affected in CLN3 disease while unaffected or only mildly affected in STGD1. Optic disc pallor on fundoscopy and an abnormal nerve fibre layer on optical coherence tomography were common in CLN3 disease compared to generally unaffected in STGD1. In CLN3 disease, dark-adapted (DA) full-field electroretinogram (ERG) responses were either absent or electronegative. In early-onset STGD1, DA ERG responses were generally unaffected. None of the STGD1 patients had an electronegative ERG.<h4>Conclusion</h4>Already upon presentation at the ophthalmologist, the retina in CLN3 disease is more extensively and more severely affected compared to the retina in early-onset STGD1. This results in more rapid VA loss, severe colour vision abnormalities and abnormal DA ERG responses as the main differentiating early clinical features of CLN3 disease.

    abstract: <h4>Clinical characteristics</h4>The spectrum of CLN3-related disorder comprises three CLN3-related phenotypes. Classic CLN3 disease is characterized by severe loss of vision due to retinal dystrophy typically beginning between ages four and seven years followed shortly thereafter by progressive neurologic findings that include cognitive decline, neurobehavioral/psychiatric problems, epilepsy, and parkinsonism. Life span is generally around 20 to 30 years. Protracted CLN3 disease is characterized by vision loss that largely overlaps with that of classic CLN3 disease. The cerebral manifestations have a milder and more slowly progressive course than those of classic CLN3 disease. Individuals may retain functional independence into their twenties or thirties. Life expectancy is extended, with some individuals living into their fifth or sixth decade. However, progression is inevitable, and supportive care needs increase over time. Isolated CLN3-related retinal degeneration due to retinal dystrophy is characterized by vision loss that first manifests in early adulthood initially as difficulty with dark adaptation followed by decreasing central visual acuity. Most of the few individuals reported to date do not exhibit additional manifestations; however, neurologic findings were described in one individual.<h4>Diagnosis/testing</h4>The diagnosis of a CLN3-related disorder is established in a proband with suggestive findings and biallelic pathogenic variants in CLN3 identified by molecular genetic testing.<h4>Management</h4>Treatment of manifestations: For individuals with classic and protracted CLN3 disease, multidisciplinary care by specialists in ophthalmology and low vision services, developmental delay / intellectual disability, mental health specialists, neurology to manage seizures and extrapyramidal manifestations, physical therapy and occupational therapy to support activities of daily living, speech-language pathology to address swallowing and communication issues, and cardiology to manage cardiac conduction abnormalities and autophagic vacuolar myopathy; transitional care or continued management by pediatric team as appropriate; family and social work support. For individuals with isolated CLN3-related retinal degeneration, treatment of refractive errors and monitoring for macular edema; low vision services; family and social work support.
