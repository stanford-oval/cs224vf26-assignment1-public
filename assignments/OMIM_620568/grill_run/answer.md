# Clinical Delineation and Phenotypic Identification of Cornelia de Lange Syndrome 6 (BRD4)

## Executive Summary & Clinical Delineation Framework

Cornelia de Lange syndrome 6 (CdLS6; OMIM #620568) is an autosomal dominant cohesin-pathway disorder (cohesinopathy) caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants or haploinsufficiency in the *BRD4* gene (F1). Historically, Cornelia de Lange syndrome (CdLS) was recognized by a distinct, severe clinical constellation—frequently driven by pathogenic alterations in *NIPBL* (>60% of cases)—encompassing hallmark craniofacial dysmorphism, profound developmental delay, and severe transverse limb reduction defects (F3, F5). 

By contrast, individuals harboring pathogenic *BRD4* alterations present with an "atypical" or "non-classic" CdLS phenotype (F1, F4). Although these individuals exhibit overlapping craniofacial traits (e.g., synophrys, arched eyebrows, thin upper vermilion) and variable neurodevelopmental delays, they consistently lack the severe structural transverse limb reductions and life-threatening malformations seen in classical CdLS (F3, F5). Clinically identifying an individual with CdLS6 purely from phenotype within a cohort of syndromic intellectual disability requires recognizing subtle, non-classic cohesinopathy facial traits while systematically screening for the complete absence of major limb amputations (F4, F5).

## Granular Clinical Phenotypes and Cohort Frequencies

To date, more than 20 clinically and molecularly confirmed individuals harboring *BRD4* variants have been published worldwide (F1). The foundational discovery cohort was delineated by Olley et al. (2018; PMID 29379197; note that PMID 30054598 represents a subsequent author correction containing no original phenotypic data) (F2), followed by a 14-individual international delineation by Jouret et al. (2022; DOI: 10.1111/cge.14141) (F1, F4) and comprehensive reviews by Alesi et al. (2019; DOI: 10.1111/ahg.12289) and Lonardo et al. (2025; DOI: 10.3390/children12111440; PMC12650802) (F1, F5).

## Discriminating Features and Differential Diagnosis

When evaluating a patient with neurodevelopmental delay and dysmorphism, CdLS6 must be distinguished both from other cohesinopathies and from non-cohesin syndromic intellectual disabilities (F4).

### 1. Distinction from Classical CdLS (*NIPBL*)
* **Key Discriminating Features:** Classical CdLS (Kline et al., 2018; Deardorff et al., 2020) is characterized by severe pre- and postnatal growth failure (<5th percentile), profound developmental disability, life-threatening diaphragmatic or cardiac defects, and severe upper-limb reduction defects (F3, F4, F15). CdLS6 presents with mild-to-moderate developmental delay, inconsistent or milder postnatal growth delay, subtle CdLS-like facies, and preservation of limb integrity (F4, F5).
* **Confidence of Distinction:** **High**. A patient exhibiting severe structural upper-limb truncations or severe classical CdLS facies can be clinically prioritized away from *BRD4* toward *NIPBL* (F3, F4).

### 2. Distinction from Non-Canonical Cohesinopathies (*RAD21*, *SMC3*)
* **Key Discriminating Features:** Pathogenic variants in *RAD21* and *SMC3* also cause atypical/non-classic CdLS presentations characterized by mild intellectual impairment, attenuated craniofacial dysmorphism, and minimal limb involvement (F4).
* **Confidence of Distinction:** **Low**. Differentiating *BRD4*-CdLS6 from *RAD21*- or *SMC3*-related CdLS on clinical dysmorphology alone is unreliable due to extensive phenotypic convergence; molecular confirmation via exome/genome sequencing is required (F4).

### 3. Distinction from Non-Cohesin Neurodevelopmental Syndromes (*Coffin-Siris*, *Rubinstein-Taybi*)
* **Key Discriminating Features:** Coffin-Siris syndrome (characterized by hypoplastic nails/fifth digits, hypertrichosis, and coarse facial features) and Rubinstein-Taybi syndrome (broad thumbs/halluces, beaked nose) share microcephaly and cognitive delay. The strongest clinical indicators pointing toward CdLS6 rather than Coffin-Siris or Rubinstein-Taybi are the signature cohesinopathy facial profile: synophrys (HP:0000664), neatly arched eyebrows (HP:0002553), and a long, smooth philtrum with a thin vermilion border of the upper lip (F4, F6).
* **Confidence of Distinction:** **Moderate**. While craniofacial gestalt software (e.g., Face2Gene) and clinical evaluation detect these differences, overlapping mild presentations still require sequencing validation (F4, F6).

## Exclusionary Findings: Phenotypes Absent in CdLS6

A critical strategy for clinical differential diagnosis is identifying phenotypes that have been systematically evaluated and documented as **absent (0/N penetrance)** in confirmed *BRD4* cohorts (F3, F5). Their presence in a patient actively argues against a diagnosis of CdLS6:

1. **Severe Congenital Structural Limb Reduction Defects:**
   * Transverse limb amputations / acheiria (complete absence of hands) (F3, F5)
   * Oligodactyly (HP:0000583; missing digits) (F5)
   * Phocomelia (HP:0003022) or severe radial/ulnar ray hypoplasia/aplasia (F3, F4, F5)
   * *Clinical Rule:* Severe transverse limb defects have a 0% penetrance across all published isolated *BRD4* point mutations and contiguous 19p13.12 microdeletion cohorts; limb anomalies in CdLS6 are strictly restricted to minor terminal findings such as fifth-finger clinodactyly (HP:0030084) and small hands/feet (HP:0200055) (F5).
2. **Severe Diaphragmatic Hernia and Fatal Classical Visceral Malformations:**
   * Major congenital diaphragmatic defects characteristic of lethal/severe classic CdLS are absent or extraordinarily rare in surviving *BRD4* patients (F4).

## Phenotypic Modifiers: Variant Type, Position, Age, and Sex

### Phenotype by Age
CdLS6 exhibits an age-dependent progressive craniofacial coarsening (F6):
* **Pediatric Presentation:** Young children frequently display classic, mild CdLS features, including neatly arched eyebrows, synophrys, and a thin upper lip vermilion (F6).
* **Adult Presentation:** As patients age, connective tissue changes lead to facial coarsening, causing the adult facial gestalt to transition away from the canonical delicate pediatric CdLS appearance into a distinct, broader, non-classic cohesinopathy facies (F6).

### Phenotype by Variant Type and Position
* **Missense vs. Truncating / Haploinsufficiency Variants:** Early studies posited that missense variants in the bromodomains (BD1/BD2) or extraterminal (ET) domain might yield a more typical CdLS presentation, whereas truncating loss-of-function variants or 19p13.12 microdeletions cause atypical CdLS (F9, F10). However, subsequent international cohorts (Jouret et al., 2022; Lonardo et al., 2025; Alesi et al., 2019) have demonstrated that heterozygous loss of function, contiguous gene microdeletions, and missense alterations all converge on an atypical/non-classic CdLS spectrum governed by *BRD4* haploinsufficiency (F1, F9, F10, F15).
* **Phenotype by Sex:** No significant sex-specific phenotypic divergence has been characterized; males and females present with comparable clinical severity across published cohorts (F12).

## Cross-Cutting Patterns and Translational Synthesis

1. **Attenuated Presentation via Cohesin-Loading Modulation:** Unlike the structural cohesin ring components (SMC1A, SMC3, RAD21) or its core chromatin-loader (NIPBL), BRD4 acts as an auxiliary chromatin reader and transcriptional regulator that recruits NIPBL to active promoter/enhancer domains. This regulatory rather than structural role explains why *BRD4* haploinsufficiency maintains adequate limb bud patterning thresholds, resulting in a strict zero penetrance of transverse reduction defects (F3, F5).
2. **Dynamic Ontogenic Gestalt:** The age-dependent facial evolution observed in CdLS6 (F6) underscores that diagnostic clinical scoring frameworks cannot treat facial gestalt as static. Computational facial analysis algorithms (such as Face2Gene) achieve high top-syndrome accuracy when accounting for age stratification, capturing the transition from subtle arched eyebrows in infants to progressive facial coarsening in adults (F6).
3. **Clinical Diagnostic Algorithm:** In a clinical setting, an individual exhibiting mild-to-moderate intellectual disability, arched eyebrows, synophrys, and a thin upper lip vermilion, **in the verified absence of oligodactyly or limb reduction defects**, should be prioritized for comprehensive genomic sequencing covering *BRD4*, *RAD21*, and *SMC3* (F3, F4, F5).
