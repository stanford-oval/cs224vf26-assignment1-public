# Clinical Phenotypic Identification and Differential Diagnosis of Spastic Paraplegia 93 (NFU1 Deficiency, OMIM #620938)

## Executive Summary & Clinical Phenotypic Profile

Spastic paraplegia 93, autosomal recessive (SPG93, OMIM #620938), represents the milder, chronically survivable end of the disease spectrum caused by biallelic mutations in *NFU1* on chromosome 2p13 (F2). While severe biallelic loss of function in *NFU1* typically produces Multiple Mitochondrial Dysfunctions Syndrome 1 (MMDS1, OMIM #608100)—a lethal infantile encephalopathy characterized by fatal pulmonary hypertension and metabolic lactic acidosis—SPG93 manifests as a progressive spastic paraplegia phenotype surviving past infancy and into adulthood (F2; F3). Clinicians identifying patients based on phenotypic evaluation alone must distinguish early-stage presentations (which frequently mimic spastic cerebral palsy or pure hereditary spastic paraplegia) from mature presentations, which evolve into a complex neurodegenerative syndrome featuring spastic paraparesis, cerebellar ataxia, dysarthria, and axonal/sensorimotor polyneuropathy (F4; F5; F6).

## Differential Diagnosis and Discriminating Clinical Biomarkers

When a clinician assesses a patient presenting with spasticity or spastic paraparesis, SPG93 must be differentiated across multiple clinical categories:

### 1. Canonical MMDS1 (NFU1 Deficiency)
- **Discriminating Findings**: Early-onset fatal course (<15 months), refractory lactic acidosis, and pulmonary arterial hypertension are hallmarks of MMDS1 (F3). Patients with SPG93 survive into later childhood, adolescence, or adulthood (reaching up to the third decade) and lack severe pulmonary vasculopathy (F2; F3; F6).
- **Confidence of Distinction**: **High**. The absence of acute lethal cardio-pulmonary failure provides a definitive clinical boundary separating SPG93 from classical MMDS1 (F3).

### 2. Common Classical Hereditary Spastic Paraplegias (e.g., SPG4, SPG7, SPG11)
- **Discriminating Findings**: While early-stage SPG93 can mimic pure HSP, 100% (16/16) of SPG93 patients exhibit cerebral white matter neuroimaging abnormalities on brain MRI from early stages (F5). Classical pure HSPs (such as *SPAST*-related SPG4) typically display normal brain parenchyma or isolated thin corpus callosum without widespread leukoencephalopathy (F5).
- **Biochemical Biomarkers**: SPG93 is an inborn error of Fe-S cluster biogenesis affecting lipoate synthase (*LIAS*) and the glycine cleavage system (GCS), frequently causing body-fluid glycine elevations (hyperglycinemia) and mild lactic acidosis (F1; F7). These biochemical features are absent in pure classical spastic paraplegias.
- **Confidence of Distinction**: **Moderate to High** when brain MRI and plasma amino acid profiles are evaluated simultaneously.

### 3. Variant Nonketotic Hyperglycinemia (NKH) and Other Fe-S/Lipoic Acid Defects (*GLRX5*, *BOLA3*, *LIAS*)
- **Discriminating Findings**: Defects in *GLRX5*, *BOLA3*, *LIAS*, and *NFU1* cause overlapping clinical phenotypes involving spastic paraparesis, cavitating leukoencephalopathy, and elevated CSF/plasma glycine (F1). Biochemical screening and neuroimaging alone **cannot** reliably separate SPG93 from *GLRX5* or *BOLA3* deficiencies (F1).
- **Confidence of Distinction**: **Low based on phenotype alone**. Direct molecular genomic sequencing is strictly required for intra-pathway differentiation (F1).

### 4. Spastic Cerebral Palsy
- **Discriminating Findings**: Children with early-onset SPG93 presenting in toddlerhood (4.5 to 27 months) with spastic diplegia and motor delays are frequently misdiagnosed as having static perinatal cerebral palsy (F4; F11; F12). Normal perinatal history, progressive deterioration rather than a static course, presence of cerebral white matter lesions (F5), and emergence of cerebellar ataxia or neuropathy in later childhood distinguish SPG93 from static cerebral palsy (F4; F6).
- **Confidence of Distinction**: **Moderate**, requiring longitudinal follow-up and neuroimaging (F4; F5).

## Obligate Exclusionary Findings: Features Arguing Against SPG93

Certain clinical signs have been systematically evaluated and documented as absent in individuals with SPG93. Their presence strongly argues against a diagnosis of SPG93, indicating alternative diagnoses (such as MMDS1 or unrelated complex HSP variants):

1. **Severe Infantile Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (HP:0002092)**: Systemic pulmonary vascular remodeling leading to early right ventricular failure is the clinical hallmark of classical infantile MMDS1, resulting in death before 15 to 16 months (F3). It has been looked for and confirmed absent in chronic SPG93 patients surviving past early childhood (F3).
2. **Intractable Severe Metabolic Lactic Acidosis (HP:0003128)**: Catastrophic systemic lactic acidosis requiring acute intensive care resuscitation is characteristic of infantile MMDS1 but is consistently absent in the milder SPG93 spastic paraparesis presentation (F3).
3. **Normal Brain Neuroimaging**: In the defined SPG93 cohort, 100% of affected patients (16/16) demonstrated abnormal white matter morphology on MRI (F5). A completely normal brain MRI in a patient with chronic spastic paraplegia strongly argues against SPG93 and favors pure classical HSPs such as SPG4 (F5).

## Phenotypic Modifiers: Variant Type, Allelic Position, Age, and Sex

### Variant Type and Position
- While severe biallelic loss-of-function variants in *NFU1* or homozygous canonical mutations (such as p.Gly208Cys) typically cause fatal MMDS1, milder hypomorphic missense variants (e.g., p.Gly189Arg, p.Leu99Val, p.Val241Leu) are identified in patients with SPG93 (F8; F14). These missense substitutions preserve residual Fe-S cluster maturation activity.
- However, a strict genotype-phenotype correlation is challenged by documented **intrafamilial phenotypic discordance** (F8). T.A. Uzunhan et al. (2020) described siblings harboring the identical homozygous *NFU1* variant c.565G>A (p.Gly189Arg): one sibling experienced fatal MMDS1 at 16 months of age, whereas the other survived with a milder spastic paraparesis presentation evaluated at 27 months (F3; F8).

### Age-Dependent Phenotypic Evolution
- SPG93 displays marked temporal progression (F4; F6; F12). Presentation frequently begins in early toddlerhood as spastic diplegia or lower-extremity hyperreflexia (F4; F11). Over longitudinal observation, it evolves from an apparently isolated motor spasticity into a complex multisystem neurodegenerative syndrome involving cerebellar signs (ataxia, dysarthria) and distal sensorimotor axonal polyneuropathy in the second and third decades of life (F6).

### Sex Correlation
- Across reported cohorts, clinical presentations and progression affect both males and females equally; no sex-dependent difference in penetrance or phenotypic severity has been identified (F2; F4; F6).

## Contradictions and Conflicting Literature in NFU1-Related Disease

1. **Cavitating vs. Non-Cavitating Leukoencephalopathy on Neuroimaging**:
   - *Initial View*: Early literature suggested that SPG93 was defined by mild, non-cavitating periventricular white matter hyperintensities without cystic changes, cleanly separating it from the severe cavitating leukodystrophy of MMDS1 (D. Tonduti et al., 2015, DOI: 10.1186/s13023-015-0237-6) (F4; F13).
   - *Contradiction*: T.A. Uzunhan et al. (2020) demonstrated frontal cystic cavitation and parenchymal degeneration in a child presenting with spastic paraparesis carrying homozygous *NFU1* mutations, confirming that cavitation is an age- and state-dependent feature rather than an obligate exclusionary divider (F4; F13).

2. **Prevalence and Discriminative Value of Optic Atrophy**:
   - *Conflict*: Certain analyses proposed that optic atrophy is absent or rare (<20%) in SPG93, serving as a discriminator against mitochondrial HSPs such as *SPG7* (F15).
   - *Counter-evidence*: Multiple reviews of mitochondrial Fe-S protein maturation disorders (F. Tort et al., 2016, DOI: 10.1007/s10545-016-9975-4; E. Lebigot et al., 2021, DOI: 10.3390/biomedicines9080989) catalog optic atrophy as a recurrent feature within the *NFU1* spastic spectrum, demonstrating that optic atrophy cannot be used to rule out SPG93 (F15).

3. **Reliability of Hyperglycinemia as an Obligate Biomarker**:
   - *Conflict*: Initial reports posited body-fluid hyperglycinemia as a 100% obligate clinical marker of *NFU1* spastic paraplegia (F7).
   - *Contradiction*: Broader reviews of Fe-S cluster biogenesis disorders indicate that elevated body-fluid glycine and lactate are variable across clinical draws and metabolic states, meaning normoglycinemia during quiescent phases does not exclude SPG93 (A. Selvanathan et al., 2022) (F7).

## Deprioritised Hypotheses and Methodological Re-evaluations

Several working hypotheses in the pooled research were deprioritised due to conflicting data, lack of quantitative cohort evidence, or refutation:

- **100% Sensitivity of Hyperglycinemia for SPG93 Discrimination (Deprioritised/Contested)**: Set aside because total biochemically evaluated SPG93 cohorts remain small (n < 15), and systemic reviews show variable body-fluid glycine levels (F7).
- **Strict Genotype-Phenotype Correlation by Allele (Deprioritised/Contested)**: The assertion that hypomorphic missense variants strictly dictate SPG93 while null variants dictate MMDS1 is refuted by discordant siblings sharing the exact homozygous p.Gly189Arg mutation with divergent fatal MMDS1 vs. spastic paraparesis presentations (F8; F14).
- **Quantitative Cerebellar Sign Prevalence of 60%–75% (10/16 to 12/16) (Deprioritised/Refuted)**: Set aside because published literature does not provide cohort-wide granular numerical breakdowns supporting this specific statistical frequency, despite confirming qualitative ataxia and dysarthria (F16).
- **Quantitative Polyneuropathy Cutoff (≥50% over age 10, 0% under age 5) (Deprioritised/Refuted)**: The literature lacks a systematic cohort of 16 patients over age 10 evaluated electrophysiologically; polyneuropathy cannot be validated at this exact quantitative threshold (F17).
- **Strict Age of Onset in Late Childhood / Adulthood (Deprioritised/Contested)**: Set aside because initial symptoms consistently appear in early infancy or toddlerhood (4.5 to 27 months) mimicking spastic diplegic cerebral palsy before late complex progression (F11).
- **Total Defined SPG93 Cohort Size of Exactly 13 Individuals (Deprioritised/Refuted)**: Refuted by foundational data from R. Kaiyrzhanov et al. (2022) and OMIM #620938, which define the cohort as 19 individuals across 10 families (16 exhibiting the spastic paraplegia continuum and 3 with severe infantile hypotonia/encephalopathy) (F2; F18).

## Cross-Cutting Mechanistic Patterns and Open Research Directions

1. **Intrafamilial Discordance and Modifier Loci**: The dramatic discordance between siblings carrying identical homozygous *NFU1* variants demonstrates that clinical expressivity is modulated by non-allelic factors. Multi-omic profiling (whole-genome sequencing for modifier loci and RNA-sequencing of respiratory chain pathways) is required to determine why some patients escape fatal MMDS1 to develop SPG93 (F3; F8).
2. **Obligate Negative Phenotypic Boundaries**: Differentiating SPG93 from its allelic counterpart (MMDS1) does not rely on distinct pyramidal signs, but on the obligate absence of lethal pulmonary arterial hypertension and catastrophic metabolic acidosis (F3).
3. **Biochemical and Neuroimaging Pathway Convergence**: Because *NFU1*, *BOLA3*, *GLRX5*, and *LIAS* converge on lipoic acid cofactor synthesis and glycine cleavage lipoylation, they generate identical neuroimaging lesions and body-fluid glycine elevations, precluding etiology-specific differentiation without genomic sequencing (F1).
4. **Longitudinal Phenotypic Evolution**: SPG93 typically begins as an apparently isolated, static spastic diplegia in early toddlerhood, but slowly unmasks cerebellar degeneration and peripheral axonal polyneuropathy as bioenergetic failure accumulates across extended axonal lengths (F4; F5; F6).
