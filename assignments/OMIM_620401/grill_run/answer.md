## 1. Executive Summary and Cohort Demographics

Intellectual developmental disorder, autosomal recessive 84 (MRT84; OMIM #620401) is an autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorder caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in *SGSM3* (Small G Protein Signaling Modulator 3) (F1, F4). To date, the documented global cohort across dedicated primary Mendelian discovery publications consists of 15 molecularly confirmed homozygous individuals from 9 independent kindreds across two primary series (F1, F2, F18):

1. **The Ashkenazi Jewish Founder Cohort:** Described by Birnbaum et al. (2024, PMID:37833060), comprising 13 affected individuals from 8 unrelated Ashkenazi Jewish families harboring a shared homozygous founder loss-of-function allele (F1, F2). This variant has an estimated population carrier frequency of approximately 1 in 52 (1.92%–2.3%) in gnomAD, corresponding to a predicted disease incidence of ~1 in 10,800 births in this demographic (F1, F3).
2. **The Consanguineous Turkish Kindred:** Described by Turkyilmaz et al. (2025, DOI:10.1111/cge.14631), comprising 2 affected adult siblings (aged 36 and 17 years) harboring a homozygous stop-gain variant, `p.Arg526Ter` (F1, F13).

Together, the Ashkenazi Jewish founder variant accounts for >70% of known affected individuals worldwide (F1). Phenotypically, MRT84 is characterized primarily by mild-to-moderate intellectual developmental delay, expressive speech acquisition delay with eventual preserved verbal communication, and short stature (F5, F6, F7, F13).

## 3. Differential Diagnosis and Discriminating Features

When evaluating a patient with mild-to-moderate intellectual developmental delay and short stature, distinguishing MRT84 from phenotypically overlapping autosomal recessive intellectual developmental disorders (ARID) requires identifying key discriminating traits and exclusionary findings (F4, F5, F6).

### Overlapping Clinical Entities
Commonly confused ARID disorders include conditions associated with:
- *CC2D1A* (MRT3)
- *TUSC3* (MRT7)
- *TRAPPC9* (MRT13)
- Recessive metabolic/neurodegenerative encephalopathies
- Syndromic ciliopathies

### Primary Discriminating Criteria
1. **Mild-to-Moderate Cognitive Impairment with Preserved Communicative Speech (Moderate-to-High Confidence):** Unlike *TRAPPC9* or severe syndromic ARID where severe expressive aphasia or permanent nonverbal status is standard, *SGSM3*-deficient patients manifest expressive speech delay with developmental catch-up, attaining communicative spoken speech by mid-childhood (e.g., around age 5; documented developmental quotient around 84) (F4, F6, F7, F9).
2. **Cardinal Invariant Short Stature (High Confidence):** Short stature (HP:0004322) is consistently linked with the biallelic *SGSM3* loss-of-function phenotype across distinct genetic backgrounds (both the Turkish `p.Arg526Ter` kindred and the Ashkenazi Jewish founder cohort) (F5, F10, F13). In contrast, many nonsyndromic ARID entities (e.g., *CC2D1A*, *TUSC3*) typically maintain normal linear growth.
3. **Absence of Coarse Facies and Severe Microcephaly (High Confidence):** Patients with *TRAPPC9* frequently exhibit progressive post-natal microcephaly, obesity, and distinct coarse facial features, which are conspicuously absent in MRT84 (F4).

## 4. Pertinent Negative Findings as Exclusionary Criteria

Systematic clinical evaluations across published cohorts have established invariant negative systemic and neurological findings (F4, F8). The presence of any of the following features argues decisively **against** a diagnosis of *SGSM3*-related MRT84 (F4, F8):

- **Progressive Neurodegenerative Regression:** In contrast to metabolic encephalopathies or neuronal ceroid lipofuscinoses, *SGSM3* deficiency does not manifest progressive neurological decline or developmental regression (F4, F8, F9).
- **Progressive Spastic Paraplegia:** Pyramidal signs, lower-extremity hyperreflexia, and spastic paraparesis have been evaluated and found absent (F4).
- **Retinitis Pigmentosa and Retinal Dystrophy:** Ophthalmologic manifestations of progressive retinal degeneration typical of ciliopathies or metabolic storage disorders are absent (F4).
- **Major Congenital Heart Defects:** Structural intracardiac defects (e.g., septal defects, conotruncal malformations) have not been observed (F8).
- **Renal Anomalies:** Structural renal dysplasia, cysts, or malformations are absent (F8).
- **Sensorineural Hearing Loss:** Auditory brainstem and audiologic evaluations demonstrate normal hearing (F8).
- **Progressive Microcephaly or Progressive Cerebral/Cerebellar Atrophy:** Structural neuroimaging does not demonstrate neurodegenerative brain volume loss or acquired progressive microcephaly (F4).
- **Obligate Mutism / Anarthria (HP:0001344):** Complete failure to acquire functional expressive speech is absent in older children and adults (F6, F7, F15).

## 5. Phenotypic Modifiers: Variant Type, Position, Age, and Sex

### Variant Type and Position
- All molecularly proven MRT84 cases reported to date harbor biallelic truncating loss-of-function variants (nonsense or frameshift alleles), specifically the Ashkenazi Jewish founder frameshift allele and the Turkish homozygous stop-gain `p.Arg526Ter` allele (F1, F13, F17).
- There is currently **no evidence** that phenotypic severity varies between different truncating alleles or by position along the *SGSM3* transcript (F17). Both the Ashkenazi founder variant and the C-terminal `p.Arg526Ter` variant result in an overlapping phenotype of mild-to-moderate intellectual developmental delay and short stature (F5, F13, F17).
- No pathogenic missense variants located in conserved domains (e.g., the RUN domain or TBC-like domain) have been clinically established in patients to demonstrate distinct genotype-phenotype correlations (F17).

### Age-Dependent Dynamics
- **Speech Trajectory:** Language impairment is markedly age-dependent. In preschool years, patients exhibit prominent expressive speech delay (HP:0000750) (F6, F9). However, longitudinal data show developmental catch-up, with intelligible communicative speech acquired around age 5 and maintained through adolescence and adulthood (e.g., in Turkish siblings assessed at ages 17 and 36 years) (F6, F9, F13).
- **Stability:** Motor and cognitive skills remain stable without late-onset neurodegenerative regression in adult patients (F4, F8, F9, F13).

### Sex-Specific Effects
- Affected individuals have been documented across both sexes in the multi-kindred Ashkenazi Jewish cohort (8 families) and the Turkish family (siblings) (F1, F2, F13).
- No differences in disease penetrance, expressive language capability, or degree of short stature have been observed between male and female individuals (F1, F5, F13).

## 6. Literature Discrepancies and Conflicting Nosology

The principal contradiction in the literature centers on whether *SGSM3* deficiency represents a **syndromic** or **nonsyndromic** intellectual disability entity (F11, F13, F14):

1. **Syndromic Classification:** Birnbaum et al. (2024, PMID:37833060) defined the condition as a syndromic intellectual disability disorder, citing mild, recurrent dysmorphic facial features, mild muscular hypotonia, and behavioral/emotional challenges across their 13 Ashkenazi Jewish patients (F11, F14, F16).
2. **Nonsyndromic Classification:** In contrast, Turkyilmaz et al. (2025, DOI:10.1111/cge.14631) classified *SGSM3* as a strong candidate gene for a *nonsyndromic* intellectual disability phenotype, reporting two homozygous `p.Arg526Ter` siblings with intellectual developmental delay and short stature who lacked facial dysmorphism, structural malformations, hypotonia, or stereotypic movements (F11, F13, F14, F16).

This discrepancy appears to reflect subjective nosological interpretation of minor physical anomalies and subclinical facial variants rather than true biological divergence (F11). Additionally, a minor literature discrepancy arose regarding short stature: while one early assumption suggested short stature might be restricted to the Turkish pedigree, data from Birnbaum et al. confirmed that short stature is a shared, prevalent manifestation in the Ashkenazi Jewish founder cohort as well (F5, F10).

## 7. Deprioritised and Refuted Hypotheses Review

Several literature hypotheses were deprioritised, contested, or refuted during data curation:

- **Hypothesis [F10] (DEPRIORITISED / Screened):** *Short stature is present in Turkyilmaz et al. (2/2) but absent in Birnbaum et al.*  
  *Reason for deprioritisation:* Directly contradicted by Birnbaum et al. (2024), who documented short stature as an observed, prevalent phenotype in the Ashkenazi Jewish cohort (F5, F10).
- **Hypothesis [F12] (DEPRIORITISED / Contested):** *Severe delay in motor and language milestones is universally present (100%) in all cohorts.*  
  *Reason for deprioritisation:* Contested because motor milestone acquisition is not universally delayed across all patients (one patient had no motor delay), and overall developmental delay is consistently characterized as mild-to-moderate rather than uniformly severe (F12).
- **Hypothesis [F15] (DEPRIORITISED / Contested):** *Severe expressive speech impairment or complete speech absence (HP:0001344) approaches 100% penetrance.*  
  *Reason for deprioritisation:* Contested because affected individuals develop communicative verbal language by mid-childhood (e.g., speech onset around age 5, DQ ~84), ruling out obligate mutism (F6, F7, F15).
- **Hypothesis [F16] (DEPRIORITISED / Refuted):** *Hyperactive behavior, restlessness, and stereotypic movements (HP:0000752, HP:0000733) occur in >60% of affected individuals.*  
  *Reason for refutation:* No published cohort provides quantitative evidence supporting a >60% prevalence. Furthermore, Turkyilmaz et al. (2025) identified two affected adult siblings completely lacking hyperactivity, restlessness, or stereotypic movements (F16).
- **Hypothesis [F17] (DEPRIORITISED / Refuted):** *Truncating variants cause a classic severe presentation whereas missense variants in the RUN or TBC-like domains cause subtle phenotypic variations.*  
  *Reason for refutation:* All documented patients with truncating loss-of-function variants actually present with mild-to-moderate intellectual disability rather than a severe encephalopathy. Furthermore, no pathogenic missense alleles in functional domains have been validated in human cohorts (F17).
- **Hypothesis [F18] (DEPRIORITISED / Screened):** *Total worldwide cohort comprises fewer than 10 homozygous affected individuals.*  
  *Reason for deprioritisation:* Birnbaum et al. alone reported 13 affected individuals, which, combined with the 2 siblings in Turkyilmaz et al., totals 15 molecularly confirmed patients (F1, F2, F18).
